## LEARNINGS

### From *Glory of Rome*

**Military hierarchy (NCO chain):**
- Principales (corporal) → Optio (sergeant, deputy century commander) → Centurion → Pilus Prior (cohort's deputy) → Primus Pilus (legion's third-in-command, highest rank a plebeian could reach)
- Aquilifer: standard bearer; carried bearskin over armor. The aquila (silver eagle) was the legion's soul.
- Beneficiarii: soldiers excused from normal duties due to special skills (medical, scribal). Excused by the senior centurion.

**Crowns — *coronae* — military decoration system:**
- Grass Crown (*corona graminea*): awarded to anyone whose actions saved an entire legion or army. Made from grass taken from the battlefield. Rarest and most prestigious.
- Civic Crown (*corona civica*): oak leaves. Awarded for saving the lives of fellow soldiers (squad or century) in a desperate situation.
- Gold Crown (*corona aurea*): awarded to first man over the walls in a storm assault, or for outstanding valor in land or naval battle. Several types named separately.
- Having won one or more crowns gives a permanent reaction bonus from any Roman citizen: +1 (Gold), +2 (Civic), +3 (Grass). Double if the citizen witnessed the action.

**Army life detail:**
- Standard daily rations: bread, porridge, *posca* (sour vinegary wine), supplemented by foraging.
- Soldier pay was 5 asses/day but deducted for clothing, rations, burial insurance, replacement of lost kit. The clerk subtracted 2d4 × 10% leaving little actual cash.
- Soldiers regularly bribed their principales for lighter duties or time off.
- Garrison duty: cooking, foraging, cleaning weapons, training, day-long marches. Some officers hunted bandits and escaped slaves.
- Decimation: if a century or cohort mutinied, a random 10% were executed.

**Pilum mechanics (important tactical detail):**
- Last segment of shaft is soft metal (not wood). On impact it bends — stuck in a shield, it makes the shield useless because the weight drags it down. If it misses, the bent shaft disables the pilum so the enemy cannot throw it back.
- If a pilum misses a shielded target by 1 or 2, the shield is stuck useless until the pilum is removed (Strength check with –4 penalty, one full melee round, character can do nothing else during removal).

**Roman undead — Lemures:**
- Hungry ghosts of people who died kinless and alone. They prowl on 9–13 May (*Lemuria* festival).
- Treat as wights, wraiths, or shadows, but they do not create other undead, and they materialize only at night on May 9th through 13th.
- 10% chance that 1d10 lemures attack any family or individual who has forgotten to make the proper offerings.
- Festival response: the paterfamilias would walk through the house at midnight throwing black beans over his shoulder saying "I redeem myself and my family with these beans."

**German (Germanic) troops:**
- Fought in a close-packed mass on foot, armed with javelins. 90% infantry (no armor, medium shield, long sword, two javelins), 10% cavalry.
- Numerous but disorganized; deadly guerrilla fighters. Fought constant skirmishes on the Roman frontier.
- A Roman army could usually defeat several times its number in Germans. Exceptions: surprise ambush and betrayal — Legions XVII, XVIII, XIX destroyed in 9 AD in the Teutoburger forest.
- Infantry often ran alongside cavalry.

**Coins:**
- Aureus (gold): 25 denarii / 100 sestertii / 400 asses. State use only.
- Denarius (silver): 4 sestertii / 16 asses. Standard silver coin.
- Sestertius (brass/copper): 4 asses.
- As (copper): base unit, 1 cp equivalent.

### From *Lex Arcana: Britannia*

**Vindolanda specifically:**
- Dates to Agricola's advance north; initially auxiliary cavalry, then mixed cohort of 1000 men (infantry + cavalry). One of the LARGEST forts south of the Vallum.
- Decline narrative: when the Antonine Wall was built, most troops moved north. When it was abandoned, Vindolanda's troops did not return — it never regained military relevance. Sits in the "lesser turbulent areas" of the Vallum where travelers simply passed through.
- The VICUS prospered precisely because of the steady traffic of soldiers and merchants. Locals supply the garrison with timber, beer, footwear, and clothing items.
- The castra has a modest bath building, but the vicus has a larger, more modern one. Soldiers on leave prefer to bathe outside the walls.
- The garrison's relevance is social/logistical, not military. An interesting intelligence point.

**Hadrian's Wall — the Vallum as system:**
- Not just a wall: counterscarp + fighting ditch + main wall (18ft tall, 9ft wide, northern face plastered white) + military road (Via Liminalis) + vallum proper (wide ditch with stakes + two earthen mounds).
- 17 main forts (*castra*) with vicus. 80 milecastles (15–40 men). 160 watchtowers.
- Three road network: Via Imperialis (runs from Londinium north to Wall), Via Liminalis (inner side of Wall, "Border Way" connecting forts), Via Puellarum (connects south to west, named after a legend of dancing maidens who guided Agricola's lost scouts — possibly the *parva gens*, small folk).

**Gods of the Vallum (local frontier deities):**
- **Agrona**: Terrifying goddess of war and massacre. Originally Caledonian, worshipped beyond the Antonine Wall. Human sacrifice cult. Her followers interpreted the Roman abandonment of the Antonine Wall as her victory — her cult has been growing south ever since. Followers behind every insurrection against Rome in the north.
- **Belatucadrus**: "The Rightful Luminous One" (also "Rightful Slayer"). Equated with Mars by Roman soldiers. Cult widespread along the Vallum, main shrine at Luguvalium (western Vallum). Scholars believe he actually conceals Celtic Belenus (sun god, equated with Apollo). Under this sun-god guise, his followers justify violence as "righteous slaying." Under surveillance because the people who worship him consider "justice" to mean killing Roman citizens.
- **Coventina** (from earlier context): goddess of a sacred spring near Brocolitia fort. Her well contains thousands of votive offerings. Benign water deity, but springs in the area are known for iron-tainted water.
- **Cocidius**: "Cocidius the Red," a wild entity at the polar opposite of Roman civilization. Connected to Cernunnus's evil aspect. A god of the wilderness who is NOT safely equated with any Roman deity. Soldiers worship him for protection against what lies beyond the Wall.

**The Alaisiagae Goddesses:**
- Four Germanic war goddesses with a shrine in the temple of Mars at Vercovicium (fort on Hadrian's Wall). Their name means "Dispatching Terrors" or "All Victorious."
- Similar to the *Valchyriae* (Valkyries) — warlike, punish oath-breakers, protect those who fight for the weak.
- Names and domains:
  - **Baudihilla** ("Lady of Battle"): most aggressive, leads followers into battle, carries a sword with a winged hilt.
  - **Beda** (or Ricagambeda, "The Warden"): invoked by those who fight in defense of the weak; carries a shield carved with a raven.
  - **Fimmilena** ("The Avenger"): presides over oaths, punishes oath-breakers; carries a whip.
  - **Friagabis** ("The Liberator"): watches over those who fight oppression; carries a short dagger to cut the bonds of prisoners.
- Invoked during illegal rituals, including curse-casting. When the Alaisiagae are called, ravens appear as a response. A raven apparition is always treated as an omen of death by the Britanni.
- The local priests of Mars keep their altar at Vercovicium under discrete surveillance.

**Northern tribes (near Vindolanda):**
- **Brigantes**: the tribe whose territory includes Vindolanda and the central Vallum region. Their goddess Brigantia is patron of smithing/craftsmanship, equated with Minerva. Queen Cartimandua's bloodline still runs in the region — some descendants are now Roman senators and equites.
- **Selgovi**: most of the region just north of Hadrian's Wall. Cunning, organized tribal confederation with a high king. Hate the Vallum because it reduced their lands. Expert informants and guides — but one can never be sure they are entirely honest. Exceptional skills in balancing alliances.
- **Votadini**: occupy the east coast between Hadrian's and Antonine Walls. Wary allies — not friends but practical buffer against more northern tribes. Their main settlement: hill fort at Traprain (old name) or the new Din Eidyn. Guides and supplies available, but travelers should remember relations have often been precarious.
- **Novanti**: west of the Selgovi. The name means "The Mighty." Extremely isolationist; live in crannogs (lake island fortified dwellings, some over 100 feet diameter). Less hostile than Selgovi but unsupervised and their alliances are unknown.
- **Caledoni**: the northern threat. Red-haired warriors tall and powerful; can hide in swamps for hours and emerge without warning ("made of smoke"). Fight on chariots drawn by warhorses when possible; prefer guerrilla warfare. Love horses as symbols of status.
  - Live in *broch* towers: circular stone towers 15–45ft internal diameter, up to 24ft tall, walls up to 9ft thick. Both floors connected by internal stairs. Purpose unknown to Romans; possibly marking points of celestial alignment.
- **Picts**: "The Painted Ones" (face painted blue in battle, bodies tattooed). Mysterious — their true name is unknown. Matrilineal succession (king is his sister's son, not his own son). Their carved stones may serve as protection magic similar to Roman wall inscriptions. Extremely aggressive against Rome. Capital: Inbhir Nis (on a lake rumored to contain an aquatic monster).

**Celtic gods relevant to the campaign:**
- **Camulus**: war god equated with Mars-Camulus. Sacred animal = boar (boar insignia on legions near Britannia). Occasionally "points" devout followers to iron deposits with special qualities for forging extraordinary weapons.
- **Cernunnus**: "Lord of the Wilds," antlered, lord of life and death, both friend and foe of humanity. His domain connects to Cocidius the Red, an evil wild entity. The Collegium recommends caution — Cernunnus's domain hides "unpleasant surprises."
- **Taranis**: thunder god with human sacrifice cult. Eight-spoked wheel as symbol. Much more bloodthirsty than Jupiter; kept under strict control by the Pontifices.
- **Nemetona**: mistress of sacred groves (*nemeton*). Every forest is potentially her domain. Every whisper of leaves or creak of wood is potentially her message. Followers must ask permission before causing harm in any grove.

**Encounter table hooks (d20, usable in any session near the Wall):**
- 5: A druid wanders a grove examining plants.
- 7: Separatist fanatics of Taranis have taken priestesses of a Minerva shrine as hostages.
- 8: A veteran centurion has surrounded a farming village demanding they hand over anti-Roman agitators within days.
- 10: The son of the Votadini chieftain went missing during a diplomatic visit; his warriors are becoming aggressive.
- 12: Woodcutters awoke a Celtic Giant sleeping under a forested hill held sacred by locals.
- 14: Undead bog bodies (*Cadavera Turbarii*) attack a peat-gathering village.
- 20: A dragon powerful enough to burn and destroy a watchtower in a single night. Malcontent and superstition spreading among legions.

## SCENE 0: THE FARBOG
**Read aloud:**
> The trees end and the ground turns to black water. Ahead, a causeway of old skulls and packed bone runs across the marsh toward a hillside that has a face — a skull carved from the living rock, easily twenty feet across, its eye sockets dark even now. The water on either side glows green. Not fire. Something slower, like a pulse.
> At the bank's edge, a standing stone. Runes cut deep. Old work — older than the causeway.

**Standing stone riddle** (DC 13 History to read Germanic):
> "Born from the willing, fed by the blood, I grow in the silence where no god has stood. Worship without truth, devotion without cost — I am what remains after the believer is lost. Name me."

**Answer:** the hunger / hollow faith / false devotion. Any honest answer works.
**Reward:** carved bone token hidden in notch at stone's base. One free reroll on any undead saving throw this session. Rune = OTHALAN (same as Session 1 vault).

**Avoidance:** DC 14 group Stealth — stay on causeway, move quietly, majority must pass. Success = no combat. Failure = Wights rise mid-crossing.

**Why Wights are here:** Cult chieftains forced devotion tests — followers had to stand in the bog water overnight alone. The weak ones died. Cult magic bound them here. They didn't choose to stay.

**Encounter: 3 x Wight** (MM p.300, CR 3, AC 14, HP 45)
- Longsword: +4 to hit, 1d8+2 slashing
- Longbow: +4 to hit, 1d8+2 piercing
- Life Drain: +4 to hit, 1d6+2 necrotic, DC 13 Con save or max HP reduced
- **Sunlight Sensitivity** — it's daytime, they fight at disadvantage
- **Zombie rising:** PC killed by Life Drain rises as Zombie unless Wight destroyed first
- Terrain: Wights in the water, causeway is difficult terrain for them. Party has high ground.

## SCENE 1: THE VILLAGE
**Read aloud:**
> The village smells of woodsmoke and old bread. Chickens. A dog that barks once and stops. Two fires burning where ten should be. The longhouses are solid work, well-kept, but half the doors stand open on empty rooms. A woman stops grinding grain and watches you without moving. A child runs inside. An old man at the fire does not look up.
> Whatever took this village apart, it was not a raid. Raiders leave bodies and ash. This place just... thinned.

**What villagers know:**
- Seer named **Thusnelda** — went to grove alone before dawn with a blade. Three followed, came back frightened.
- Two chieftains went south months ago, never returned.
- DC 13 Persuasion: Thusnelda has spoken of "settling the gods' debt" for two weeks.
- Grove = north. Dungeon entrance = south past old offering stones.
- DC 13 History to learn Thusnelda is old-blood völva, trained since childhood. Not reckless. Has been right about everything for twenty years.

## SCENE 2: THE SACRED GROVE
**Read aloud:**
> The oaks here are old enough that their roots have lifted the path. At the grove's center, a stone no taller than a man stands alone, its face carved on the side facing the village — two faces, really, or one face trying to be two. Roman workmanship on top, older marks underneath. Someone has left fresh holly. Someone else has smeared ash across the Roman features.

**Belatucadrus shrine:** DC 13 Religion — Celtic sun god Belenus renamed Mars on the frontier. Ash over Roman features = recent anger at the Roman overlay.

**Consecration:** Cleric/Druid, 10 min prayer, no spell slot. Weapon gains Belatucadrus blessing: advantage on damage rolls vs cult-marked creatures, counts as magical.

**Past the shrine:** standing stone circle visible through gap in oaks. Thusnelda is there.

## SCENE 3: THE SACRIFICE CIRCLE
**Read aloud:**
> He stands in the centre of the ring with the stillness of someone who has made up his mind. His feet are bare on the cold stone. The knife in his hand is old work — older than him, older than the village. His left wrist is scored, blood running down his fingers and dropping into the earth. He is watching it fall.
> He hears you come and does not look up.
> "You are too late to stop the debt. But you can witness. A willing death settles what war begins. The gods will be satisfied. Your soldiers will stop burning our grain."

**Thusnelda believes (wrongly):** cult's blood ritual created divine hunger. He thinks a willing völva sacrifice closes the wound and stops Roman aggression. He's wrong — the dungeon is the source, not him.

**Path A (with evidence):** Show cult cloth / tally stick / cult symbol → DC 14 Persuasion. Success: he stops and waits. Failure: one more round before fatal.
**Path A bonus:** physical evidence grants advantage on the roll.
**Path B (no evidence):** DC 18 Persuasion. Outlander/Hermit background = double proficiency.
**Path C (physical):** Grapple/Disarm DC 13 vs his Dex. He won't harm party. Bridge to Path A.
**Path D (no intervention):** He dies in ~1 hour without Cure Wounds. Party loses local knowledge; grove blessing lost; S5 navigation at disadvantage.

**If saved, Thusnelda says freely:**
> "The dungeon entrance is south through the standing stones, down the old offering path. The chieftains used it. The first room below has been making the dead stay. Do not let the bones on the floor fool you — someone sorted them. Someone is still proud of that work."

## SCENE 4: DUNGEON ENTRANCE (COMBAT)
**Read aloud:**
> The offering path ends at a hillside that has a face. A skull carved from the living rock, its eye sockets dark even at midday. Inside its mouth, stairs descend into darkness. The air coming up is cold and smells of old blood. And at the base of the stairs, two shapes uncoil from the stone — serpentine, skeletal, moving with a purpose that bones alone do not explain.

**Encounter: 2 x Bone Naga** (MM p.233, CR 4, AC 15, HP 58) + **4 x Skeleton** (MM p.272, CR 1/4, AC 13, HP 13, animate init count 20)
- Skeletons: vulnerability to bludgeoning — one hit destroys them
- Bone Naga spells (DC 12, +4 attack): hold person, ray of sickness (2d8 poison), ray of enfeeblement
- Bone Naga bite: +5 to hit, 2d6+3 piercing + 4d6 poison (DC 11 Con, half on success)
- Terrain: stairs are narrow, two abreast max. Nagas use this.

## SCENE 5: THE OSSUARY — S4 ENDS HERE
**Read aloud:**
> The room ahead is round, and its far wall is shaped like the thing you just walked through — another skull, smaller, teeth picked out in inlaid bone. The floor is a carpet of femurs and ribs. Not scattered. Sorted — by size, by age, stacked with a care that took someone a very long time. Deeper in the dungeon, something breathes.

DC 13 History: bones curated deliberately, months of work. Tally stick matches. The passage at the far end leads deeper. **Session ends here.**

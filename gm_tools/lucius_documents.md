## SEALED ORDERS (from Corvinus, to Lucius)

*Wax seal: Corvinus's personal mark. Break to read.*

---

> **GAIUS PETRONIUS CORVINUS, Legate of the Sixth Cohort, Garrison Commander of Vindolanda, to TRIBUNE LUCIUS AEMILIUS CODA — EYES ONLY**
>
> You are dispatched to locate the object described in the attached supplementary brief. You will not read this letter in the presence of your unit.
>
> The object is not to be recovered. It is not to be transported. It is not to be inventoried, catalogued, or mentioned in any dispatch to Rome. If you locate it, you will destroy it by whatever means are available to you. If it cannot be destroyed in the field, you will ensure it is sealed below ground and the location is known only to you and to me.
>
> The Senate does not know this order exists. The Emperor does not know this object exists. These facts are not accidents. They are policy, and they are mine.
>
> If you are reading this after my death, the order stands. Finish it.
>
> If you show this letter to your unit, to Rome, or to any officer above the rank of Centurion, I will deny it. The letter will be called a forgery. You will be tried. I will win.
>
> Do not make me do that to you. You are the best Tribune I have had in eight years on this wall.
>
> Find it. End it. Come home.
>
> *— C.P. Corvinus, Vindolanda, the 14th day before the Kalends*

## CITIZENSHIP COMMENDATION LETTERS
*(One per PC — fill in name. All identical in structure, personalised in the middle section.)*

---

> **TRIBUNE LUCIUS AEMILIUS CODA to the OFFICE OF THE CURSUS PUBLICUS and any MAGISTRATE OF THE ROMAN PEOPLE who receives this letter:**
>
> I write in my capacity as Tribune of the Sixth Cohort, Vindolanda garrison, under the command of Legate Gaius Petronius Corvinus.
>
> I commend to the attention of Rome the person of **[PC NAME]**, who served under my direct command during the Germanic frontier crisis of this year. This individual performed acts of distinguished service beyond the scope of their assigned duties, demonstrating courage, discipline, and loyalty to Rome under conditions I am not at liberty to specify in a public document.
>
> I formally recommend elevation of status to **Latini** *(or Civis Romanus if applicable)* in recognition of this service, and I attach my personal seal as surety of this recommendation.
>
> This letter requires only the countersignature of the Garrison Commander to become executable. I have left it unsealed because I did not expect to deliver it in person.
>
> If you are reading this and I am not present: I wrote it freely. It reflects my honest judgment. Honor it.
>
> *— L. Aemilius Coda, Tribune, Sixth Cohort*
> *(seal impression)*

## THE AMBER TOKEN

*Small. Carved amber, fits on a thumb. One rune: OTHALAN.*

No writing. No explanation. He carried it from before Session 1.

**DM only:** Lucius received it from Corvinus before the mission. Corvinus found it in a confiscated cult shipment three months ago and kept it. He gave it to Lucius as a field identifier — "if you find objects marked with this, you've found the right place." Neither of them told the party.

## THE CARVED WOODEN HORSE

*Palm-sized. Germanic work — not Roman. The carving style matches the vicus craftsmen near Vindolanda.*

No inscription. Worn smooth from handling.

**DM only:** Lucius bought it from a Germanic trader in the vicus two weeks before the campaign started. He never explained why he carried it. Let the players decide what it meant to him.

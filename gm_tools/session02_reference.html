Session 2: The Tribune's Gambit — GM Reference

Where We Left Off

Session 1 ended at the mine entrance. The vault beneath Fort Vindolanda. The party came up carrying the Spear of Mars — or what they believed was the Spear of Mars — and Legate Corvinus was waiting in the dark.

He did not look at the spear. He looked at whoever was holding it, and then he looked away.

"Inside. Now. All of you. Not a word to anyone until I say otherwise."

He turned and walked back toward the principia. He did not check if they were following.

Tribune Sees the Party — Character Hooks

Lucius Valerius Maximus reads people for a living. The moment he looks at the party, he files each person in a category. Run these reactions in Scene 1 when the Tribune addresses the party directly.

Julia Jana (Noble, Optio, Senator's daughter):
He notices the Jana signet before he notices her face. A calculation happens — visible as a half-second pause before he speaks to her specifically. Senator Gaius Janus and his father Brutus move in the same circles: political rivals who respect each other's intelligence and trust each other's venality. Lucius knows more about why Julia is at Vindolanda than Julia does. He will not say this. But in every conversation with her he is talking to two people: the soldier in front of him and the Senator who sent her. He cannot easily move against her. Arresting a Senator's daughter creates a problem in Rome that outlasts this frontier posting. He will maneuver around her rather than through her.

Private moment available (Scene 2, if they are alone): He says something that should not be possible: "Your father chose this posting carefully. Vindolanda specifically. Did he tell you why?" He does not explain further. He is not sure himself. But he has noticed that Brutus noticed that Janus sent his daughter north this particular month. That kind of coincidence, in their world, is not coincidence.

Tanit bint Abdmelqart (Charlatan, Bard, Carthaginian):
She can see through his performance. He senses this within the first exchange — not through magic, through the quality of her attention. She is listening to the wrong things. The things people listen to when they are cataloguing a performance rather than believing one. This unsettles him more than any military threat would. He does not look at her directly after the first moment. He addresses his statements to other members of the party when she is watching.

Iris (Shadar-kai, Circle of Stars, Shadow-cursed):
He looks at her once and does not look again. Not from fear — from the calculation that looking directly at something that wrong, in front of witnesses, gives it more acknowledgment than he wants to provide. She is registered as a problem he does not have vocabulary for. In private, to his Praetorian captain: "Keep me informed about the grey one. Specifically." He does not explain what he means by grey.

Ursula Günther (Half-orc, Barbarian, Marcomanni heritage):
He dismisses her immediately and completely. She is registered as muscle, present because someone needed lifting done. He will speak his full plans in her presence without lowering his voice. This is the most catastrophically wrong assessment he makes this session. Ursula understands every Latin syllable he says and will remember all of it.

DM note — "G.C.B." / Gaius Cassius Brutus compound name

Players with classical history knowledge will notice "Gaius Cassius Brutus" combines two of the most famous Republican assassins. They are right. Brutus chose this cognomen deliberately — he is not descended from either figure. He adopted it as a political statement, aligning himself with senatorial checks on military power. If players notice: confirm their suspicion. This is his manifesto written in his name.

Scene 3: The Raid

Purpose: Crisis that removes thinking time. Decision in Scene 4 must be made under pressure.

Key reveal: The Praetorians protect the spear, not the fort. Vercingetorix arrives at the breach and wants to talk.

Secondary objectives matter: Who the party saves and how they lead determines options in Scene 4.

BEAT 1 — The Alarm
Read Aloud — raid start
The first arrow takes a sentry off the wall before the alarm horn sounds.

Then the drums start — Germanic war drums, deep and rhythmic, surrounding the fort on three sides. Torchlight beyond the palisade: dozens of torches, maybe hundreds.

Centurion Varro's voice cuts through the confusion: "WALLS! ALL SOLDIERS TO THE WALLS! Where in Pluto's name are those Praetorians?"

The Praetorians are nowhere near the walls. Through the principia window, you can see torchlight — they're inside, around the spear.

Survival DC 12 (active, before committing): Drums synchronized from three fixed positions. Torches spaced too evenly for a mob. Staged for hours. Not a raid — a demonstration.

Nature DC 11 (passive, if on night watch): Birds near treeline silent 2 hours before attack. Coordinated advance. Planned, not opportunistic.

BEAT 2 — THE MOMENT: Hold the Walls

Phase 1 (2 rounds): 12 Tribal Warriors in waves, 2 Berserkers on ladders.

Athletics DC 12 — push back a scaling ladder. Two characters halve resistance. Miss by 3 or less: holds one more round.

Persuasion / Intimidation / Performance DC 13 — rally frightened soldiers. DC 17: soldiers hold AND Flavus (age 19, first posting) is named. He appears in Session 4 as escort volunteer without being recruited.

Acrobatics DC 11 — non-standard route to ballista. Success: reach it one round faster. Fail: reach it, bonus action recovery.

Ballista: 2d10 piercing, 2 operators, Dexterity DC 12 to aim under pressure.

Phase 2 (3 rounds): Battering ram on the west gate. Wood holds 3 rounds.

Options: defend the chokepoint directly; stop the ram (fire, stones, cutting rope); rally counter-charge with Varro.

Varro fights with the party — Veteran stat block. Worth three normal soldiers.

BEAT 3 — Vercingetorix at the Breach
Read Aloud — Vercingetorix enters

He looks at the principia. Then he looks at you.

Scene 4: The Tribune's Move

Purpose: The session's decision point. Every choice has weight and no clean answer.

Key mechanic: The window of doubt. One round to catch the hesitation. It changes what's possible in the negotiation.

BEAT 1 — Setup

After the raid. Soldiers tend wounds. Gates being repaired. Three raiders captured.

The Tribune appears. Immaculate as ever. Praetorians behind him.

Read Aloud — the accusation
"Well," he says, looking at the carnage. "That was unfortunate."

He turns to the Praetorians: "Arrest them."

One of his guards moves to flank the party. The Tribune's voice is flat, businesslike:

"These soldiers conspired with the raiders. They signaled the attack timing from the walls. I have witnesses."

From the far side of the courtyard, the Legate steps forward. His expression is unreadable.

"I concur with the Tribune's assessment," he says.

Centurion Varro takes a slow step to stand beside the party. His hand moves to his gladius.

"Do you," he says.
BEAT 2 — THE MOMENT: The Hesitation

TEMPORAL GATE One round. One check per player. Window closes when Tribune finishes the accusation and steps forward.

Insight DC 14 (active, watching his face during the accusation): Before "enemies of Rome" — he hesitates. Half a second. His eyes move to Varro (or where Varro was). He has been given lines and is not committed to them.

What it opens: Negotiation DC drops from 17 to 14. The player can invoke it directly: "You paused. You know this isn't true."

Why the window closes: Once he finishes and commits, he has chosen. The doubt sealed over. Catching him before is catching a man in the act of choosing.

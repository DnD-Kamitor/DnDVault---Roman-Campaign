THE SHADOW OF MARS · CHAPTER 1
Blood and Omens
A Roman Adventure for Five to Six Characters, Level 3
3–4 hours · Fort Vindolanda & the vault beneath it · 175 AD
Scenes: 5 · Combat: 3 · Handouts: 7 · Maps: 6
Something ancient was unearthed beneath Fort Vindolanda, and the omens since have been uniformly bad. The Legate wants it retrieved and asks no questions. A three-century guardian is still keeping the oath he swore, and he is not the enemy: he is the last functional part of a bargain Rome just broke.

Adventure Background

The Premise

The characters are members of a special vexillatio extraordinaria newly assembled at Fort Vindolanda on the Germanic frontier. Three days ago, a work crew digging a well broke into ancient ruins beneath the fort and unearthed an iron spear covered in runes. That night every camp dog died. The augurs found black livers and reversed bird flights. One worker touched the spear and killed two companions before being subdued. Legate Corvinus wants the artifact retrieved, and he does not want to know how it happened.

What the Players Don't Know

The vault was sealed by treaty, not by accident. Three Roman generations ago, Rome and four Germanic tribes negotiated a genuine binding: the tribes sealed a divine weapon and its guardian below the ground; Rome agreed never to dig within fifty feet of the principia's eastern wall. Corvinus's excavation broke that agreement the moment the first shovel hit the ground.

The guardian is not a monster. The Wight at the altar was the High Priest of the binding ceremony. He chose, three centuries ago, to remain as guardian rather than pass on. He is tired, resolute, and honest. He is not the party's enemy: he is the last part of the bargain still holding.

Corvinus has done this before. The party is not the first team sent down. Four men are already dead. He has also quietly sent a private rider to his patron in Rome: he intends to keep this discovery, and its retrieval, entirely under his own control.

Someone is already inside the vault. A small cult cell reached the altar chamber before the party did. One member, a Cult Fanatic, is still down there when the party arrives, mid-ritual.

Running This Adventure

This is a branching dungeon crawl. At the T-junction (Room 3) the party chooses left (Shield Hall, no combat, rich lore) or right (Flooded Gallery + Bone Chamber, one hard fight). Both branches converge at the Binding Chamber before the Runic Corridor and the Altar Chamber climax. Neither branch is required content: the OTHALAN rune needed for the corridor puzzle can be found through the Shield Hall memory, the bone token (either branch), or a passive Religion check. Never force a return trip.

The Wight fight has a built-in exit: if the party backs away from the spear, he holds position and lets them leave. This is not a failure state. It is the correctly-designed outcome for a party that listens.

Adventure Synopsis

  1. Scene 0-1: Cold Open & The Legate's Briefing: A shared dream, dead dogs, and a mission with no room for questions. Tribune Lucius arrives unannounced.
  2. Scene 2: Descent into the Ruins: Shaft, Entrance Passage, T-junction. Left branch (Shield Hall) or right branch (Flooded Gallery, Bone Chamber). Both merge at the Binding Chamber, then the Runic Corridor.
  3. Scene 3: The Altar Chamber: The spear. The Wight. A choice between taking it and leaving it.
  4. Scene 4: Return to the Surface: A possessed stone-hauler and two cultists in the courtyard.
  5. Scene 5: The Reckoning: Corvinus wants the spear. The party decides who holds it going into Chapter 2.

Level & Rewards

Characters begin at Level 3. They advance to Level 4 at the end of this chapter regardless of choices made. Milestone advancement: no XP tracking needed, though adjusted encounter XP is provided per fight for reference.

The Adventure

Scene 0: Cold Open

Duration: 30 seconds. No choices. Visceral hook only: do not run as a separate scene from Scene 1.

Three nights ago. You dream. All of you, the same dream. You stand in darkness, ankle-deep in blood that doesn't ripple. In the distance, a spear stands upright in the earth, glowing like a dying star. When you try to look away, you can't. When you try to wake, you can't. A voice, old, vast, furious, speaks a single word in a language you shouldn't understand, but do: "MEUM." (MINE.) You wake. Your tent reeks of copper and smoke. Outside, the camp dogs are howling. By dawn, every dog in Fort Vindolanda is dead.

Cut to black. Do not answer questions yet. Move directly into Scene 1.

Scene 1: Meeting the Legate

Duration: 20–30 minutes. Roleplay. Handout 1 given here.

The principia is lit with more oil lamps than it needs. The air is thick with incense, but under it something else: organic and wrong, like the smell that comes up from a well when the water has gone bad. Legate Corvinus stands at the head of the table, both hands flat on the map, eyes bloodshot and specific. Augur Cassia Liviana sits to his left, a bloodstained cloth held loosely in her hands. Centurion Varro stands near the door, one hand resting on his gladius grip.

The briefing: Three days ago a work crew found ancient ruins beneath the fort. They unearthed an iron spear covered in runes. That night, all camp dogs died. The augurs found corrupted omens. One worker touched the spear and went mad, killing two companions. Corvinus wants the party to descend, secure the artifact, and bring it to him. When he formally assigns the mission, he sets a wax tablet on the table's edge without picking it up again: give Handout 1 now.

Cassia's one line: She does not oppose Corvinus directly. Quietly, to the party rather than to him: "The augury was clear. What is sealed below is sealed for a reason. If you go down, go knowing that." Then she stops.
Varro's one objection: "We don't know what sealed it or who is on the other side of that seal. Sending men down without understanding what they're walking into is how we lose the men." Corvinus: "Noted. Dismissed." Varro does not leave.

Tribune Lucius Valerius Maximus arrives mid-briefing, without knocking, carrying a sealed dispatch satchel with an imperial crest. Late twenties, composed. Hands Corvinus a sealed letter, waits while he reads it, takes a chair at the edge of the room uninvited. Does not introduce himself. If asked who he is, Corvinus answers flatly: "Tribune Lucius Valerius Maximus, attached to the Legate's office by order of Rome." His arrival visibly tightens Corvinus's posture. Cassia's expression when he enters: she has seen him before and is not surprised.

DM Note: Lucius remains in the fort during the descent and is present at Scene 5. Do not foreshadow his allegiance to Brutus this session: that reveal belongs to Chapter 2.

Scene 2: Descent into the Ruins

Duration: full session core. Branching dungeon crawl. Corvinus's aide hands over torches, tinderbox, and knotted twine at the shaft head without a word of luck.

Room 1: The Shaft

The hole is perhaps six feet wide. The Romans who dug it used short-handled picks: tool marks in the clay get more careful as they got deeper. A rope ladder, poorly anchored. Below: darkness, and a smell like old metal and earth wet for a very long time.

40-foot descent. Torch flames lean downward in an unnatural draft: no mechanical effect, simply wrong. Survival DC 11 (active, reading the draft before descending): the pulse means two chambers with different pressure; a cedar smell beneath the cold mineral note means deliberate sealed storage, not collapse.

At the bottom, scratched into the clay at knee height: seven scratches in a vertical line, one diagonal line through all seven, and the word NOLITE (Do not). Left by a previous work crew member. Not a puzzle: a warning.

Room 2: The Entrance Passage

The passage runs straight for forty feet, cut through earth reinforced with stone slabs, Roman at the top, Germanic at the bottom. Fifteen feet in, a Roman pick lies abandoned. Not rusted: the handle is broken, not decayed. The head points back toward the shaft.

Perception DC 10 (passive): the pick points back toward the shaft; whoever dropped it was retreating. Investigation DC 12 (active): the same pick made the warning scratches, deliberate, not panicked. History DC 14 (Librarius) and Religion DC 15 (Haruspex) unlock deeper reads on the countdown's meaning (see chapter1.qmd for full knowledge-barrier text).

The passage ends at a T-junction. Left: warmer air, dry stone. Right: colder air, the sound of slow water below the floor.

Left Branch: The Shield Hall and Hidden Alcove

No combat in this branch until the Binding Chamber.

Room 4: The Shield Hall

Dozens, maybe a hundred, shields hung edge to edge so the walls resemble a silent legion. Wolf heads in iron, boars in bronze, a hand with three fingers raised, a serpent coiled to strike. None are Roman. All angled as if waiting to be seized. At the far end, an arch gapes, swallowing light.

History DC 13/15, Religion DC 14, Perception DC 11, Nature DC 12, and Athletics DC 12 (lifting a shield: grip cut from the inside, deliberately decommissioned) all yield passive or active reads on this room. See chapter1.qmd for the full knowledge-barrier text per proficiency and role.

The rune-mapping payoff: The shields are arranged by tribal hierarchy with Mars's wolf highest. DC 13 Intelligence (History) to note the arrangement carries forward: the safe rune in the Runic Corridor is always OTHALAN, and this room explains why it is always safe: it is the dominant symbol all offerings answer to.

Sicus
Half-covered by a tipped stone bench: one of the four lost men. Missing lorica, shoulder wound, stable but dehydrated. Perception DC 11 finds his drag trail; Medicine DC 9 confirms stable. Wakes with water/healing in a round, or DC 12 Medicine + two minutes.
He speaks: the cultists were here first, red cloaks, oil lamps. A lever exists in the final chamber, one already used it. He presses an OTHALAN bone token into a character's hand: advantage on the Wight communication check, stacking with the contract scroll. Cannot fight; won't follow into combat.

The Hidden Alcove

Investigation DC 15 (a shield hung backwards): reveals a hand-widened crack in the stone. Inside, wrapped in degraded cloth: a wax tablet, a bone token, a bronze stylus.

"Third watch. If you are reading this, the garrison has not come back. The chains held for three hundred years. They held because we honored the bargain. Corvinus knows nothing of the bargain. That is why the dogs are dead. Warn the..." [obliterated]

The bone token carries OTHALAN: one half of a contract pair. Give Handout 12 to whoever made the check; let them share it at the table.

Three-clue rule: A bargain existed. Corvinus's excavation broke it. The violation caused the deaths. Cassia knows something about this. The party has evidence before they reach the altar.

Right Branch: The Flooded Gallery and Bone Chamber

Room 5: The Flooded Gallery

The right passage descends on a shallow grade. Within twenty feet the floor becomes wet, then chest-deep, thirty feet across. The water is black and cold and does not move. The ceiling is three feet above the waterline at the deepest point.

Survival DC 12 (reading the waterline): the eastern wall is shallower: advantage on the Athletics DC 11 stealth wade. Four Shadows lurk, drawn to light and sound. A group Stealth DC 14 check (torches extinguished) passes for free; failure triggers combat, but give the party one round of seeing the Shadows approach before initiative.

Shadow
Medium undead · CR 1/2 · AC 12 · HP 16

Strength Drain. +4 to hit, 2d6+2 necrotic; target's Strength score reduces by 1d4 (min 1). If reduced to 0, becomes a Shadow itself.

Advantage on attack rolls in dim light. Amorphous: can move through a space as narrow as 1 inch without squeezing.

At the far wall, a preserved Germanic warrior sits with equipment intact: a framea across his lap, a bone token at his throat. He died in seiðr position: deliberate, voluntary, not from violence. Disturbing the body disrespectfully wakes one Shadow with no warning.

Room 6: The Bone Chamber

The chamber is wide and low: a natural cave the builders worked into without fully finishing. Loose stone fills one wall. Three stone benches line the far wall, bearing corroded, unidentifiable equipment. The chamber smells strongly of organic decay.
Encounter: 2 Ghouls (CR 1) + 1 Ghast (CR 2), 1,700 adjusted XP for five players, exceeds the Hard threshold. Ghouls are dormant beneath the rubble; give the party one round of looking around before they activate. The Ghast emerges last, from behind the stone benches.
Ghoul
Medium undead · CR 1 · AC 12 · HP 22

Bite. +2 to hit, 2d6+0 piercing. Claws. +4 to hit, 2d4+2 slashing; target (non-Elf, non-construct) DC 10 Con save or Paralyzed 1 minute (repeat save each turn).

Ghast
Medium undead · CR 2 · AC 13 · HP 36

Stench. Creatures within 5 ft, DC 10 Con save or Poisoned until start of their next turn (immune once saved). Bite. +3 to hit, 2d6+2 piercing. Claws. +3 to hit, 2d4+2 slashing; DC 10 Con save or Paralyzed 1 minute.

If a Ghoul-paralyzed target is attacked by the Ghast: advantage plus automatic critical. Intentional design.

Escape route: the Flooded Gallery is always available; Shadows do not reactivate for a loud retreat. After combat: a clay vessel with carbonized grain and a pre-Roman Mars coin: someone has been feeding this place until recently. That someone was Titus Sempronius Caecilius, Cassia's predecessor as fort augur, who died of fever in AD 173. His death broke a generational chain. Session 2 thread.

Room 7: The Binding Chamber: Both Branches Converge

The passage squeezes you into a chamber whose ceiling hovers just above a legionary's helm. Chains hang from the stone in ranks: thin ones for wrists, thick ones for something with a neck wider than a man's chest. None of the collars are human-sized. Across the far wall, TIWAZ is carved deep. Below it, scratched Latin: "We turned back here."

Investigation DC 12, Arcana DC 14 (passive divine aura), Athletics DC 12 (one chain resists with steady tension: something is pulling against it right now), Religion DC 16, and Acrobatics DC 11 all yield reads here. If the party lingers 5+ minutes, one chain sways once with no air movement to explain it.

The Lock Mechanism

One chain runs from ceiling through a floor ring with a shaped iron grip. Investigation DC 14 reveals the sequence from oxidation patterns: oldest chain first (left wall), newest chain last (right wall), then the centre chain. Pulling the centre chain out of sequence: DC 13 Strength save or restrained 1 minute (no damage: wasted time and noise). Correct sequence opens a hidden alcove with an oil lamp and an oilskin scroll.

The contract scroll (DC 15 Intelligence to read): the tribes agreed to seal the vault for three Roman generations; Rome agreed never to dig within fifty feet of the principia's eastern wall. The binding was void the moment the first shovel hit that ground. Combined with the wax tablet from the Hidden Alcove, the party can now reconstruct exactly what happened and why the vault is waking up.

Room 8: The Runic Corridor

The floor changes from earth to cut stone, covered in a mosaic of interlocking rune shapes from wall to wall, row after row. The runes glow faintly: not with fire, but something cold, steady as a held breath. There is no safe path around them. There is only through.

Thirty feet, eight rows of five rune tiles each. Wrong tile: 1d8 lightning damage, no save, applied instantly. On the first wrong tile, advance the Altar Chamber guardians one free preparation round: the Wight draws his weapon, the Cult Fanatic flanks, one Shadow coalesces behind the altar.

The safe rune is always OTHALAN, but its column shifts every row.

Room 9: The Altar Chamber

Give Handout 2 (Vault Inscription) as the party crosses the threshold.

The final chamber opens into a perfect circle. At the center stands a granite altar carved from a single block older than the Roman stones around it. Upon it rests a spear eight feet long, forged of iron so black it seems to swallow light. Runes coil along its haft, glowing ember-red. Within five feet of the altar, the air stops moving.
The moment your lead foot breaks the circle around the altar, the torchlight gutters sideways and the temperature drops to winter. Every rune flares once, illuminating the chamber like a heartbeat. You understand, without being told, that this weapon is awake. It has been waiting. And now it knows your name.

The Wight rises as the party enters fully. He does not attack immediately.

He has been here for three hundred years. Not the restless hunger of the walking dead: the settled weight of something that made a choice and is still keeping it. When he speaks, in a language three of you do not understand: "The bargain was broken. Not by you. But by your kind. I am still bound. You are not."

(The character with the highest Wisdom understands without knowing why. Ursula understands regardless: she grew up hearing this dialect at her mother's knee, and may choose not to share it immediately.)

Encounter: 1 Wight (CR 3) + 1 Cult of Mars Fanatic (CR 2) + 2 Shadows (CR 1/2), 1,800 adjusted XP for five players. Exceeds Hard, approaches Deadly at Level 3 with depleted resources: the designed climax.

Medium undead · CR 3 · AC 14 · HP 45

Life Drain. +4 to hit, 1d6+2 necrotic; target max HP reduces by that amount; Wight heals equal amount. Non-undead reduced to 0 max HP dies and rises as a Zombie under the Wight's control.

Longsword. +3 to hit, 1d10+1. Longbow. +3 to hit, 1d8+1.

Dark Resolve (unique trait). Regains 2 (1d4) HP at the start of his turn while any creature within 30 ft is Frightened, from any source. Cannot suppress this.

Cult of Mars Fanatic (adapted)
Medium humanoid · CR 2 · AC 13 (leather + shield of faith) · HP 33

Gladius. +4 to hit, 1d6+2 piercing, plus one cantrip/spell as multiattack. Spells (DC 13, +5 to hit): cantrips: sacred flame, thaumaturgy; 1st (3 slots): bane, inflict wounds, shield of faith; 2nd (2 slots): hold person, spiritual weapon.

Dark Devotion: advantage on saves vs. Charmed and Frightened. Acts on Initiative 10; already pulled the lever creating difficult terrain at the corridor exit before the party arrived.

The OTHALAN token (tactical target): pinned at the Wight's sternum, AC 13, 5 HP. If destroyed or removed, the Shadows lose their binding for one round (-2 attack, no Strength Drain), then act independently.

Communication Attempt

On success, the Wight pauses: "The one who ordered this. His debt is greater than yours. But you carry it now." This is a transfer of responsibility, not forgiveness: combat may still resume.

Scene 4: Return to the Surface

You hear him before you see him. A rising chant that rides the wind like sparks from a brazier. When you crest the shaft: a Gallic stone-hauler, shoulders like pillars, standing over three fallen legionaries. Marius Coda: a reliable man until today. Each fist grips a gladius slick with fresh blood. "The spear calls, the spear drinks, the spear wants a champion." His head turns as though a wire hauls it, toward whoever holds the spear.
Encounter: Berserker (Marius Coda, CR 2) + 2 Cultists of Mars (CR 1/8). The Cultists (Titus Flavinus, Gnaeus Peregrinus) are not combatants unless directly threatened: they stand near Marius, muttering "Mars aversus est," the first proof the spear's corruption spreads through proximity.

Scene 5: The Reckoning

Corvinus does not run. A Legate does not run. He walks, Tribune Lucius two steps behind. His eyes stay on the spear a moment too long. "You found it. Good. Give it to me and your bonus pay is doubled. Tonight."

Cassia approaches at a near-run from the east side entrance. If she reaches the party first: "Do not give it to him. He will use it. I have seen what that means." If Corvinus sees her, he tells her flatly to return to the shrine. She does not move.

Corruption Mechanic

Track corruption levels (0–5) for any character who touches or attunes to the spear: 1 disturbing dreams of conquest; 2 disadvantage on Wisdom saves; 3 DC 12 Wisdom save to retreat from combat; 4 DC 14 Wisdom save to avoid attacking allies when bloodied; 5 fully corrupted, becomes an NPC under DM control.

Spear of Mars: Attunement

The spear is an artifact. Attunement requires only a short rest holding it; it will not refuse, which is the danger. While attuned: +2 to attack and damage rolls, +1d8 necrotic on a hit, disadvantage on all Wisdom saves, and increasing aggression/paranoia following the corruption track. The spear whispers once per scene the attuned carrier is stressed, matched to that character's private ambitions, never generic.

Scene 1 — Farbog Crossing  map: s3_farbog_crossing.html
The trees end and the ground turns to black, sucking mire. A causeway of bleached skulls and packed ribs spans the marsh toward a limestone bluff carved into a colossal skull. The water on either side glows a sickly, breathing green. From Rivia's pack, the scorched box answers with a deep hum you feel through your boots. At the bank's edge, a standing stone. Runes cut deep. Older than the causeway.

Dramatic Question: Can they cross without waking what drowned in the water?

The Standing Stone — riddle (DC 13 History to read)
"Born from the willing, fed by the blood, I grow in the silence where no god has stood. Worship without truth, devotion without cost — I am what remains after the believer is lost. Name me."
  • Answer: the hunger / hollow faith / false devotion. Any honest answer counts.
  • Reward: bone token in a notch at the base: one free reroll on a save vs an undead ability this session. Rune = OTHALAN (DC 11 History, or automatic if they held the S1 contract token: older than the cult).
Supply caches near shore (DC 11 Investigation)
  • Green-glowing rations + tally stick (cult here for months)
  • Torn cloth in the two chieftains' warband colours
These are Thusnelda's evidence (advantage on Path A in the Circle). Make them findable.
Quiet crossing: DC 14 group Stealth, more than half must pass, at disadvantage if the box is unwrapped. Pass = no fight. Fail, or anyone steps in the water = Wights rise mid-crossing.
Sinking causeway: every 2 rounds on it, bones sink 6 in. After 4 rounds: difficult terrain, DC 12 Dex or slip in; vapours DC 12 Con or 1 exhaustion.
The Drowned Wights ×3Wight · CR 3 · AC 14 · HP 45 each · tokens: Wight/Vaettir

Look: Early cult converts in rotted Germanic wool, skin bog-brown and tight over the bone, cold light where the eyes were. They rise out of the green water on both sides of the causeway, dripping.

What they want: Nothing anymore. The chieftains made each convert stand in the bog overnight as a test of devotion. The ones who broke or drowned never came home; cult magic bound them here. They sense warmth and movement in the water, not on the bone.

How they fight: Multiattack: 2 longsword (+4, 1d8+2 slashing), or swap one swing for Life Drain (+4, 1d6+2 necrotic, DC 13 Con or max HP drops by the damage until long rest). Anyone who backs off gets the longbow (+4, 150/600 ft, 1d8+2). They flank the single-file line and focus whoever is at the back.

Weak spots: It's daytime: Sunlight Sensitivity, disadvantage on attacks and Perception. Causeway edge is difficult terrain for them, so the party holds high ground.

Tough spots: Resist necrotic + nonmagical B/P/S (silvered or magic weapons get through). Immune poison. The box means they cannot be surprised.

Say this before initiative: a PC killed by Life Drain rises as a Zombie next round unless the Wight dies first or the body is dragged off the causeway.
The Myling (optional)drowned boy · becomes a Specter if wronged

Trigger: DC 13 Perception mid-crossing: a child crying with water in his lungs, 30 ft left of the causeway. Ignored: he climbs onto the causeway behind the last PC.

Look: Twelve or thirteen. Bog-coloured skin, months-rotted work clothes, unfocused eyes. Arms held up the way a small child asks to be carried. One Germanic word, over and over: "home."

What he wants: A cult test victim, too young to refuse. He wants to be carried to holy ground. He means no harm.

The burden: carrier's speed halved; each round DC 10 + rounds carried Str or speed 0 (he gets heavier away from the bog). Can't be shaken off. Holy ground: Belatucadrus shrine (Scene 3), a Cleric/Druid 10-min consecration, or the village burial mound (DC 13 Investigation).

At the shrine: "It doesn't hurt here." He sits, closes his eyes, and is gone.

Reward: carrier gets Myling's Rest: advantage on death saves, immune to Frightened, this session.

If attacked or abandoned: Specter. AC 12, HP 22, incorporeal, Life Drain +4, 2d6 necrotic (DC 13 Con or max HP drops), sunlight disadvantage. His shriek: DC 13 Wis or Frightened 1 min, and it can wake the Wights.

Scene 2 — Germanic Village  map: s3_germanic_village.html
The village smells of woodsmoke and old bread. Chickens. A dog that barks once and stops. Two fires burning where ten should be. Half the doors stand open on empty rooms. A woman stops grinding grain and watches you without moving. A child runs inside. An old man at the fire does not look up. Whatever took this village apart, it was not a raid. Raiders leave bodies and ash. This place just... thinned.

Dramatic Question: Can they earn enough trust, fast, to learn where the seer went and why?

What the village knows (if approached without threat)
  • Free: Grove is north. The dungeon entrance is south, past the old offering stones.
  • Free: Their seer, an older man named Thusnelda, went to the grove alone before dawn. Three followed and came back frightened. He would not stop or explain.
  • Free: The two chieftains "went south" months ago and never came back. People stopped asking.
  • DC 13 Persuasion: for two weeks he's talked of "settling the gods' debt". Nobody understood until this morning, when he took a blade.
  • DC 13 History or Persuasion: old-blood völva, his family has read omens in this forest for generations. Right about everything for twenty years. Not reckless, not despairing. That's what makes this dangerous.
Clock: Thusnelda has been bleeding since dawn. If they linger here long (shopping, resting), he's further along when they arrive: start the Circle with 2 rounds instead of 3.

Look: Flour to the elbows, hands still on the grindstone. Doesn't run, doesn't welcome.

Wants: The Romans gone without trouble, and her seer back. She'll talk if weapons stay sheathed and nobody looms.

Gives: directions and the "settling the gods' debt" line.

"He went before light. With the old knife. Are you here to stop him, or to watch?"

Wants: To be left alone. He won't say the chieftains' names. Push him and he says the warbands went south and the south kept them.

"Everyone who walks that path comes back thinner, or not at all."
The three who followed the seerthe hook

Look: Young, frightened, still muddy to the knee from the grove path.

Saw: him barefoot in the ring of seven stones, singing a death-song, cutting his own wrist. He told them to go home.

Wants: Someone braver to bring him back. They'll point the way but won't go.

Box aura here: +2 Intimidation works, but doors close and the village goes quiet. Calm talk is at disadvantage while the box is close. Leaving it at the edge of the village helps.

Scene 3a — Belatucadrus Shrine  map: s4_sacred_grove.html
The oaks here are old enough that their roots have lifted the path. At the grove's center, a stone no taller than a man: two faces, or one face trying to be two. Roman workmanship on top, older marks underneath. Someone has left fresh holly. Someone else has smeared ash across the Roman features.
  • DC 13 Religion: Belatucadrus, the Celtic sun god Rome renamed Mars on the frontier. The ash is recent: anger at the Roman overlay, not the god.
  • Consecration: Cleric/Druid, 10 min prayer, no slot. One weapon gets the Belatucadrus blessing: advantage on damage vs cult-marked creatures, counts as magical (gets through Wight resistances in S5 too).
  • Holy ground: carrying the Myling? Set him down here.
  • Through a gap in the oaks: seven standing stones and a man in the middle. Give them one round to observe before he notices.
Scene 3b — The Sacrifice Circle
He stands in the exact center of the seven stones with the stillness of a man whose fate is sealed. Bare feet on frost-rimed granite. An archaic flint blade in his right hand. Blood wells from a deep cut across his left wrist, pattering into the frozen moss. A guttural death-song rises from his throat. Thirty paces away, the box rattles its hinges. He hears you come and does not look up.
"You are too late to stop the debt. But you can witness. A willing death settles what war begins. The gods will be satisfied. Your soldiers will stop burning our grain."
3 rounds until the wound is fatal. Each round a PC stands inside the ring: DC 13 Wis or 1 temp corruption. PCs at corruption 3+ inside for 2+ rounds: +1 temp corruption, no save.
A: EvidenceShow cloth / tally stick / cult symbol. DC 14 Persuasion with advantage. Success: lowers the knife. Fail: one more round.
B: Words onlyDC 18 Persuasion. Outlander/Hermit adds proficiency twice.
C: Grab himGrapple/Disarm, Athletics or Acrobatics DC 13 vs his Dex. He never fights back, only struggles. Buys time for A or B.
D: Too lateHe dies in ~1 hour unless someone casts Cure Wounds or Healing Word. Shrine blessing fades, guide lost, S5 dungeon-purpose checks lose his context.
Box penalty: within 30 ft of him, A and B roll at disadvantage (and it cancels A's advantage). He smells slaughter in the wood and recoils. Someone holding the box outside the ring fixes it.
Thusneldavölva / seer · he/him · AC 12 · HP 27 · Dex +2 · Passive Perc 16

Look: An older man, barefoot, wrist open, a flint knife older than his village. Calm. Chanting. He doesn't look at the party until he has something to say.

Objective: End the divine hunger he believes is eating his people's future.

Goal: Finish the rite in the ring of stones before anyone stops him.

Agenda: Started at dawn. He won't rush and won't stop unless he gets proof (A), a miracle of argument (B), or is held down (C).

Secret: The cult's blood rites poisoned the spiritual landscape. He can't tell their manufactured hunger from a real demand of Mars, so his death would do nothing. He's also spent three months hunting the cult's dungeon and never found it. He was looking in the wrong direction, and took that as a sign the gods wanted a different answer.

How to play him: Right about everything except the one thing that matters. Not a villain, not despairing, not mad. He believes he's the only one brave enough to pay. His calm is real.

If grabbed: "You do not understand what you are preventing. Let go of me."
If persuaded: "Show me the place you describe. If the hunger ends when you unmake it, then I was wrong. I will wait."
After, freely: "The dungeon entrance is south, down the old offering path. The chieftains used it. The first room below has been making the dead stay. Do not let the bones on the floor fool you. Someone sorted them. Someone is still proud of that work."

If the party wins below: he's at the dungeon mouth when they come out. No apology. "The debt is paid. Not the way I thought."

Restrained but unconvinced: he just waits. The party has to decide what to do with a tied-up seer who still believes he's right.

Scene 4 — The Skull Entrance ★ COMBAT  map: SkeletonFortressEntrancePublic.jpg
The offering trail ends before the gaping mouth of the colossal skull. Cold air pours out like the breath of a tomb: embalming oil, limestone dust, old blood. A raspy clatter echoes up the stairwell as vertebrae grind on wet basalt. Out of the gloom slide two shapes, serpents built from bleached human spines with fanged skulls, their empty eye sockets lighting green.

Dramatic Question: Can they force the stair before they're pinned on it?

Before / around the fight
  • DC 13 Investigation at the archway: cult markings around the teeth match the marsh cloth. Third confirmation: right place.
  • Terrain: stair is two abreast. Nagas wait at the base. Retreat up the stair and the Skeletons come in behind.
  • Thusnelda (if saved) won't go in. He waits at the mouth.
Danger: the stair is a perfect line for Lightning Bolt. Use it once, when 3+ PCs bunch up, and let them see it coming (the Naga rears back and the air crackles).
Bone Naga ×2CR 4 · AC 15 · HP 58 each · speed 30

Look: Long serpents of human vertebrae, a fanged serpent skull, green fire in the sockets. They taste of ash when they get close.

What they want: Keep anyone out of the Ossuary. The chieftains sorted those bones by hand as devotion and put the Nagas here so the order would never be disturbed. Dormant until something moves on the stair.

How they fight: Bite +5, reach 10 ft, 1d6+3 piercing + 3d6 poison. Spells (DC 12, +4): ray of frost (cantrip, slows 10 ft), sleep, charm person, hold person (2/day), lightning bolt (1/day, 8d6, DC 12 Dex half).

Tactics: Round 1: Hold Person on whoever leads down the stair, so the line behind is stuck. Bite from 10 ft so they never need to step into the stair. Lightning Bolt up the stair when the party bunches. Second Naga charms or sleeps a healer.

Tough spots: Immune poison; can't be charmed, paralyzed, poisoned, or exhausted. Darkvision 60 ft. Not vulnerable to bludgeoning (only the Skeletons are).

Skeleton ×4CR 1/4 · AC 13 · HP 13 each

Look: Scattered bones on the steps snap together on initiative 20.

How they fight: Shortsword +4 (1d6+2) or shortbow +4 (80/320, 1d6+2). Mindless: they go behind the party and cut off the retreat up the stair.

Weak spot: vulnerable to bludgeoning; one mace or warhammer hit usually drops one. Immune poison.

Bone Naga numbers are the Monster Manual block. chapter4.qmd lists a homebrew version (ray of sickness / ray of enfeeblement, bite 2d6+3 + 4d6 poison, DC 11 Con half). Pick one before the fight.

Scene 5 — The Ossuary  ★ SESSION ENDS HERE — advance to L6
The room ahead is round, and its far wall is shaped like the thing you just walked through: another skull, smaller, teeth picked out in inlaid bone. The floor is a carpet of femurs and ribs. Not scattered. Sorted, by size, by age, stacked with a care that took someone a very long time. Deeper in the dungeon, something breathes.
  • No combat. This is the quiet beat after the stair fight.
  • DC 13 History (examining): not war dead. Curated, months of deliberate work, by someone proud of it. Matches the tally stick.
  • A short passage at the far end bends out of sight. Stop here. Level up to 6 at the break; they're still inside when next session opens.
  • Missed one of the three confirmations (cloth, tally stick, archway marks)? Leave it findable at the start of next session.

Scene 1 — Farbog Crossing  map: s3_farbog_crossing.html
The trees end and the ground turns to black, sucking mire. A causeway of bleached skulls and packed ribs spans the marsh toward a limestone bluff carved into a colossal skull. The water on either side glows a sickly, breathing green. From Rivia's pack, the scorched box answers with a deep hum you feel through your boots. At the bank's edge, a standing stone. Runes cut deep. Older than the causeway.

Dramatic Question: Can they cross without waking what drowned in the water?

The Standing Stone — riddle (DC 13 History to read)
"Born from the willing, fed by the blood, I grow in the silence where no god has stood. Worship without truth, devotion without cost — I am what remains after the believer is lost. Name me."
  • Answer: the hunger / hollow faith / false devotion. Any honest answer counts.
  • Reward: bone token in a notch at the base: one free reroll on a save vs an undead ability this session. Rune = OTHALAN (DC 11 History, or automatic if they held the S1 contract token: older than the cult).
Supply caches near shore (DC 11 Investigation)
  • Green-glowing rations + tally stick (cult here for months)
  • Torn cloth in the two chieftains' warband colours
These are Thusnelda's evidence (advantage on Path A in the Circle). Make them findable.
Quiet crossing: DC 14 group Stealth, more than half must pass, at disadvantage if the box is unwrapped. Pass = no fight. Fail, or anyone steps in the water = Wights rise mid-crossing.
Sinking causeway: every 2 rounds on it, bones sink 6 in. After 4 rounds: difficult terrain, DC 12 Dex or slip in; vapours DC 12 Con or 1 exhaustion.
The Drowned Wights ×3Wight · CR 3 · AC 14 · HP 45 each · tokens: Wight/Vaettir

Look: Early cult converts in rotted Germanic wool, skin bog-brown and tight over the bone, cold light where the eyes were. They rise out of the green water on both sides of the causeway, dripping.

What they want: Nothing anymore. The chieftains made each convert stand in the bog overnight as a test of devotion. The ones who broke or drowned never came home; cult magic bound them here. They sense warmth and movement in the water, not on the bone.

How they fight: Multiattack: 2 longsword (+4, 1d8+2 slashing), or swap one swing for Life Drain (+4, 1d6+2 necrotic, DC 13 Con or max HP drops by the damage until long rest). Anyone who backs off gets the longbow (+4, 150/600 ft, 1d8+2). They flank the single-file line and focus whoever is at the back.

Weak spots: It's daytime: Sunlight Sensitivity, disadvantage on attacks and Perception. Causeway edge is difficult terrain for them, so the party holds high ground.

Tough spots: Resist necrotic + nonmagical B/P/S (silvered or magic weapons get through). Immune poison. The box means they cannot be surprised.

Say this before initiative: a PC killed by Life Drain rises as a Zombie next round unless the Wight dies first or the body is dragged off the causeway.
The Myling (optional)drowned boy · full brief in the 1b · The Boy tab

Trigger: DC 13 Perception mid-crossing: a child crying with water in his lungs, 30 ft left of the causeway. If ignored, he climbs onto the causeway behind the last PC.

The Boy in the Bog (Myling)optional · tone scene, not a fight

Who he was: A village boy, twelve or thirteen. The chieftains sacrificed him at the old altar in the grove north of the village, singing to cover his cries, and left his body there. His spirit wandered to the bog looking for the way home. Nobody came for his body, so nobody mourned him properly. He has been calling since the night he died.

Look: Skin the colour of the bog, hair plastered flat, work clothes rotted to threads. Eyes open but not quite focused, as if looking through you at a fire far away. Water runs from his mouth when he speaks.

Wants: To go home. In his story that means holy ground, where the dead can rest. He doesn't want to hurt anyone and doesn't understand that he's dead.

Improv, not canon yet: he has no name in the files. If they ask, he says "Wulf". His bones are still under the old altar in the grove's ring of stones (DC 12 Investigation, or automatic if he is carried into the ring). Laying him beside his bones / burying them releases him. Song: see notes.

How it starts
A sound under the wind, low and wet: a child crying with water in his lungs. Then it stops, and starts again, closer.

Approached:

It is a boy. Twelve, maybe thirteen. His skin is the colour of the bog. He holds his arms up the way a small child does when he wants to be carried. He says one word, over and over, in Germanic. "Home."

Ignored:

Something cold and small takes hold of the back of your leg. You look down. A grey hand, a child's hand, and a face rising out of the water behind you on the causeway. "Home," it says. "Please."
What the party can learn
Insight DC 10He isn't hostile. He's terrified, and very cold.
Religion DC 12A myling: the unmourned dead child of northern stories. Carry him to holy ground and he rests. Refuse him and he turns. (Romans would call him one of the lemures, the hungry dead.)
Medicine DC 13(improv) Religion/History DC 13 on the cauldron: old northern offering-cauldron, but fresh cult marks scratched over the old patterns. Song or cauldron counts as evidence for Thusnelda (Path A): "That is not an offering. That is a murder dressed as one."
His lines (Germanic; he only understands simple words)
"Home." (always first, and whenever he's frightened)
"It's cold. Why is it always cold?"
"The water got warm, the way baths do. Then it got too hot."
"Is my mother still at the fire?"
Near the box: he presses his face into the carrier's neck. "It's hungry. Like them. Don't let it look at me."
The burden
  • He climbs on willingly, or grapples (DC 13 Str contest) if refused but not attacked.
  • Carrier's speed halved. Each round: DC 10 + rounds carried Str check or speed 0. He gets heavier the further from the bog he goes.
  • Can't be shaken off. Hitting him turns him hostile.
  • He can be set down to wait on holy ground: the Belatucadrus shrine (Grove), a Cleric/Druid 10-min consecration, or the village burial mound. He only rests beside his own bones at the old altar in the grove (Scene 3).
(improv) Carried into the village: the woman at the quern is his mother. "They told us the bog took you." Then, quietly: "The chieftains took him north, to the old altar in the grove. His bones are still there. Lay him with them and he can sleep." Party gets advantage on village Persuasion.
Release (at the grove altar, beside his bones)
He slides down from your back and stands very still for a moment. Then he looks at whoever carried him, really looks, for the first time. "It doesn't hurt here." He sits down, closes his eyes, and is gone. Not dramatically. Just gone.

Reward: carrier gets Myling's Rest for the session: advantage on death saves, immune to Frightened.

If attacked or abandoned: he turnsSpecter · AC 12 · HP 22
His mouth opens wider than a mouth should, and the bog water pours out in a scream.

Shriek: DC 13 Wis or Frightened 1 min. It can wake the Wights if they're still dormant.

Fights: Life Drain +4, 2d6 necrotic, DC 13 Con or max HP drops. Incorporeal Movement. Sunlight: disadvantage on attacks.

Scene 2 — Germanic Village  map: s3_germanic_village.html
The village smells of woodsmoke and old bread. Chickens. A dog that barks once and stops. Two fires burning where ten should be. Half the doors stand open on empty rooms. A woman stops grinding grain and watches you without moving. A child runs inside. An old man at the fire does not look up. Whatever took this village apart, it was not a raid. Raiders leave bodies and ash. This place just... thinned.

Dramatic Question: Can they earn enough trust, fast, to learn where the seer went and why?

What the village knows (if approached without threat)
  • Free: Grove is north. The dungeon entrance is south, past the old offering stones.
  • Free: Their seer, an older man named Thusnelda, went south to the sacrifice stones alone before dawn, with the old knife. Three followed and came back frightened. He would not stop or explain.
  • Free: The two chieftains "went south" months ago and never came back. People stopped asking.
  • DC 13 Persuasion: for two weeks he's talked of "settling the gods' debt". Nobody understood until this morning, when he took a blade.
  • DC 13 History or Persuasion: old-blood völva, his family has read omens in this forest for generations. Right about everything for twenty years. Not reckless, not despairing. That's what makes this dangerous.
Clock: Thusnelda has been bleeding since dawn. If they linger here long (shopping, resting), he's further along when they arrive: start the Circle with 2 rounds instead of 3.

NORTH — The Sacred Grove  where the boy was sacrificed · map: s4_sacred_grove
The oaks here are old enough that their roots have lifted the path. Small gold lights drift between them. At the centre, a stone with two faces, Roman work over older marks, fresh holly at its foot. Behind it, a low altar black with old blood.
  • Shrine (Religion DC 13): Belatucadrus, the sun god Rome renamed Mars. 10-min prayer (Cleric/Druid) = Belatucadrus blessing on one weapon (magical, beats Vercingetorix's Relentless). The Völva wisp can grant it instantly.
  • Wisps (tab 2b): speak in riddles. The Völva's line "Blood is what it eats" is the key that the South ritual is wrong.
  • The boy's altar: his bones under it (Inv DC 12, automatic if carried in). Lay him with them: he rests, Myling's Rest. The bones are evidence for the South: the cult murdered here and called it an offering.
  • Leaving south: the Small One flickers after them: "He thinks the stones will break it. The stones only drink."

The offering trail ends before the gaping mouth of the colossal skull. Cold air pours out like the breath of a tomb: embalming oil, limestone dust, old blood.

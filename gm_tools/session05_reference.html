Session 5: The Wrath of Mars

GM Reference Card — All content DM-only — Divine Stakes — Final Session — No level advance (already at cap for arc)
Beat 1 — The World Stops

Dawn at Vindolanda. The rain stops mid-drop. Not gradually — arrested in air. Germanic arrows frozen at the apex of their arc. A soldier's shout stops between vowels.

"The world holds. Not peace — suspension. As if someone put their hand up and said: wait."

THE MOMENT — Mars Arrives

The parade ground is black sand. Not transformed — replaced. The fort's stones are still there at the edges but the center is something else.

A figure on a throne of weapons — fort-height, armored in what armor means rather than what armor is. The helmet's crest is red and does not move in wind that does not exist.

He does not raise his voice. He does not need to.

"MORTALS. I WATCHED. I ARRIVED. NOW YOU ANSWER."

All prep checks close at this moment. The window before he addresses the party directly is brief — approximately one round of real-time. Run the checks fast or call time.

Beat 3 — The Window Closes

Every environmental check must be made before he speaks to them. Once addressed, the party is in Scene 1. Do not let players re-open the window. Mars is patient. He is not slow.

Five Senses — Cold Open

Sight
Rain drops suspended mid-air — hundreds of them, each perfectly still. Arrows frozen at their apex. The parade ground: black sand with no horizon — it extends to the fort walls and stops exactly there. The throne: assembled from every weapon Mars has been given over a thousand years.
Sound
Nothing. Then: his voice, which is not a voice — it arrives in the mind at the volume he intends, regardless of distance. The frozen arrows are audible as a hum — metal at the frequency of tension.
Smell
Blood and iron and the clean smell of new weapons drawn, multiplied. The arena smells of every battlefield that ever petitioned Mars. Also: the smell of rain that stopped — ozone, petrichor, arrested.
Feel
The black sand gives underfoot like real sand — each step sinks slightly. It is warm. Whatever it is, it is not cold. Gravity is unchanged. The world is otherwise normal, physically, which is deeply wrong.
Taste
Iron — as if a sword hilt were pressed against the back of the tongue. Consistent from the moment the sand appears. Does not fade. This is the taste of his attention.

NPC — Mars (form)

LookFort-height. The armor is what armor means — not Roman, not Germanic, not any specific tradition. Every army's idea of war given shape.
WearingThe weapons-throne is built of everything ever offered to him. Some recognizable. Some impossible. The spear from Session 1 is at the throne's center — he has always had it.
PostureSeated at first. When he stands, there is no visible transition — seated, then standing, without the movement between.
MemorableHe looks at one character first: the one who made the genuine grove sacrifice. Not warmth — recognition. He has known since it happened.
First impressionHe is satisfied, not angry. Not pleased — satisfied. There is a difference. The trial is a formality he is performing because it is correct to perform.

Frozen Witnesses

Cassia: Frozen, but her crow is not. It circles the genuine-sacrifice character. Her expression: confirming details she has already seen in a vision.

Varro: Frozen mid-salute — right hand raised, not quite reaching his helmet. He chose to salute when he realized what was happening. He had approximately one second of warning.

Vercingetorix (if allied): His warriors circle the arena's edge but cannot enter. He is not frozen — he is standing outside the sand. He knows what this is. He waits.

Lucius (if alive): Frozen where he stood. His fate depends entirely on what Mars decides.

Beat 1 — Three Paths Visible

Mars presents the options without preamble. Not as a menu — as a fact. The arena weapons are there (Option A). He himself is there, fully present, not a proxy (Option B). And he is listening, which means words are possible (Option C).

He states no preference. The choice reveals character. He is watching that, too.

THE MOMENT — Choose or Be Chosen

If the party hesitates for more than one minute of real table time: Mars chooses for them. He chooses Option B — the trial of direct combat. Not as punishment. As the default when intent is unclear. He respects hesitation less than he respects a wrong choice made with conviction.

One character — the one he looked at in the Cold Open — will feel the weight of the decision most directly. If they speak for the group, Mars responds. He does not address committees.

Option A: Champion Trial — Fausta Luperci, arena weapons, physical contest.
Option B: Trial of Blades — Mars himself, buildings phasing in, 300 HP / AC 20 / four attacks. The party fights a god.
Option C: Trial of Voice — make the argument. Persuasion DC 19. He listens. He pushes back.

Beat 3 — The Trial Begins

No further preamble once the choice is declared. Cut directly to Scene 2. The arena is already configured for whichever option. It was always configured for this one.

Five Senses — Scene 1

Sight
The arena weapons visible between the frozen witnesses. Handout 11 cards face-down on the sand. For Option B: no phasing buildings yet — they arrive each round. For Option C: Mars sits back slightly, which is the only physical indication he is listening.
Sound
The frozen arrow hum. Mars's voice arriving without traveling. When the choice is made, a single tone — the same tone that announced him in Session 3. Then silence of a different quality: expectation, not suspension.
Smell
The iron smell intensifies when the trial path is committed. Each option has a slight variation: Option A — hot metal of arena combat. Option B — the specific smell of blood on cooling armor. Option C — nothing. He clears the air, which is more alarming than filling it.
Feel
The sand temperature matches the option chosen. Option A: warm, arena-floor warmth. Option B: cool — the battle-field cold. Option C: neutral, which means he has removed his own preference from the environment. That is the most unsettling.
Taste
The iron taste sharpens to the edge of unpleasant when the choice is locked. Then normalizes. The anticipation is over. Something is beginning.

Arguments That Move Mars (Option C)

1. Honor argument: The spear dishonored him — it sowed madness, corrupted a worker, infected Cassia's visions, degraded Varro's men. A weapon in his name that spreads chaos is an insult to what war is supposed to be.

2. Discipline argument: Destroying it preserved the disciplined war Mars demands. Reference the sacrifices, the kneeling, the escort, the escort's deaths. The discipline was demonstrated, not claimed.

3. War vs. murder: They chose war's harsh honor over easy cruelty — sparing Vercingetorix when they could have killed him, saving civilians when it cost them tactically, capturing traitors instead of killing them. They fought as Mars would want soldiers to fight.

Arguments That Anger Mars

"It was too dangerous." — He is a war god. He approves of dangerous.
"We had no choice." — He spent five sessions giving them choices. He noticed.
"Rome needed this." — He is not Rome's bureaucracy. He is older than Rome.
"We completed the ritual for you." — He did not ask. He watched. These are different things.

On any of these: DC rises by 2. If three consecutive failures on arguments that anger him — go to Virtus fallback.

Vercingetorix dies within a year regardless. If the party succeeds, he dies peacefully. If the party fails catastrophically, he dies in the aftermath of what happens to the fort.

Hesitation Mechanics

One minute of real table time before a committed choice = Option B selected by Mars. Track this. It is not cruel — it is his character. He chose to be here. He expects the party to choose too.

If the party immediately commits to Option C without the opening checks: They begin without the Armilustrium bonus, without having read his first look. DC 19, no initial advantage. Achievable. Harder.
Beat 1 — The Trial Begins

Option A: Handout 11 cards flipped. Fausta Luperci materializes — she was a real soldier at the Battle of the Frozen Rhine. She respects endurance and discipline. Feints have no effect on her.

Option B: Mars stands. The phasing buildings begin materializing: each round, one barracks building phases in solid, blocking movement and line-of-sight. He has four attacks. HP 300. AC 20. He does not retreat.

Option C: The first argument is stated. Mars listens. Then: "Say it plainly. Without decoration."

THE MOMENT — The Pivot

Option A — Frozen Advance: Fausta uses her Frozen Advance ability — she closes distance impossibly fast, Constitution save DC 14. This is the moment she becomes a real threat, not an exhibition.

Option B — The Look: Midway through the combat, Mars focuses on one specific character — whoever has the highest corruption, or the one who made the genuine sacrifice. Everything else stops for one round. He is asking something without words. A character who understands what he's asking can respond with the Virtus demonstration: unarmed, in the open, holding still.

Option C — The Argument Lands or Fails: DC 19 Persuasion or Performance. On 14–18 (fail by 5 or less): "Say it again, differently." DC drops to 17. On 19+: he adjusts one trial term. On 24+: he concedes and cites something specific from that character's Session 0 material.

Beat 3 — Resolution

The trial ends. Not with death — with completion. Mars does not kill the party. He tests them. What they showed during the test is what carries into Scene 3.

Five Senses — Scene 2 (all options)

Sight
Option A: Fausta in full Roman combat posture, helmet crest catching no light, sand pluming from each step. Option B: each phasing building arrives with a visual compression of the arena — the sand rearranges around it. Option C: Mars's armor shifts subtly when an argument lands — a slight relaxation at the shoulders.
Sound
Option A: the specific sound of parade-ground training, not battlefield chaos — controlled, metered. Option B: his footsteps on black sand, which sound like a drum. Option C: his voice arrives at different volumes depending on his reaction — louder when skeptical, quieter when engaged.
Smell
Option A: hot blood on sand — the arena smell. Option B: the cold smell of a battlefield three days after — what remains. Option C: nothing. He maintains the neutral air throughout. This is a discipline. He is choosing it.
Feel
Option A: arena sand under boots — warm, giving, not destabilizing. Option B: each of his attacks arrives with a pressure wave — the concussive feel of something that doesn't need to hit you to affect you. Option C: when an argument lands, a warmth through the floor — the antechamber's warmth, hundreds of feet up. He is the same presence as below.
Taste
All options: the iron taste calibrates to the trial's progress. Intensifying: he is more engaged, more present, more pleased or more displeased. Stable: he is watching. Fading: the trial is concluding, one way or another.

Option A — Arena Weapons (Handout 11)

Three cards face-down on the sand. Flipped at trial start (or after Perception DC 13 in Cold Open). Each has a benefit and a cost — the benefit is significant, the cost is real.

Investigation DC 14 during the trial: identify which weapon suits which character's build, or which cost is most dangerous for their specific situation.

History DC 14 (passive): Fausta Luperci was real. She survived the Frozen Rhine by outlasting, not outfighting. She respects endurance. Tricks do not affect her. Hold your ground for three rounds and her attacks stop escalating.

Option B — Mars's Stats

HP: 300 — AC: 20 — Attacks: Four per round

He does not drop below 1 HP — that is not what this is. At 50 HP remaining, he pauses. "Enough. You have proven the point." Combat ends.

Phasing buildings: One per round materializes, solid, 30 ft × 30 ft. Choose placement to create tactical complexity — not to trap, to challenge. Reposition with Stealth DC 15. Show of fearlessness (unarmed in the open): Intimidation DC 15. On success: "...Interesting." Advantage on next roll.

Corruption Level 5 PC: Mars targets them preferentially until the party calls their name three times (echoing Session 3's mechanic). On third call: he pauses, looks at the party, then at the character. "You chose them. That tells me something too."

Virtus Fallback (any option)

When: Option C has failed three times, or Option B is going catastrophically, or Option A has left the party broken.

What: One character steps into the fighting circle without a weapon and holds still. Wisdom save DC 14 (not flinching under divine scrutiny, alone, unarmed).

Success: Mars stops. "That. That is what I asked for." Trial resets to neutral. Option C becomes available regardless of which option was chosen.

Fail: They flinch. Trial continues at normal parameters. Do not punish the attempt — it counts as an action and a courage point. Mars notices.
Beat 1 — Mars Approaches

The trial is done. He approaches on foot — not throne-height now, but still larger than a man. Still armored. Still present. The arena does not shrink; he does not diminish. He chooses to be at a scale that allows conversation.

He addresses each character directly — one sentence each. Not judgment. Assessment. He has five sessions of observation. He uses it.

THE MOMENT — The Mark

He marks them. Not a ceremony — a fact. The mark appears, character by character, in the order he assessed them. See Handout 10.

"You stood before a god of war and lived. That is not a small thing. I have seen many try."

Then, for each one specifically — one thing about what they did in the five sessions that he noticed. This should come from the campaign log: what they did that cost them something, what they chose that wasn't easy. He noticed all of it. He cites one thing per character, specific, accurate.

This is your preparation homework for Session 5. Before play, write one sentence per character from the log. Use it here.

Beat 3 — The Consequence Choice

For characters who distinguished themselves (genuine sacrifice, Virtus demonstration, saving a specific ally at cost): he offers a consequence from the menu. Not all five — at most two or three, depending on the trial path. He does not press for a decision immediately. "You have time. The mark will remind you that I am waiting."

Five Senses — Scene 3

Sight
Mars at conversational scale — still armored, still present, but no longer fort-height. The black sand is receding at the arena's edges. The frozen rain drops have not fallen yet. They are still waiting.
Sound
His voice, this close, arrives at normal volume — which is more intimate than the divine volume was. He chose this too. When the mark appears on a character, a single clear tone — the same tone as Session 3, Session 4. He has been here since then.
Smell
The iron smell fading — slowly, over this entire scene. By the time he leaves, it will be gone. Replaced, at the edges, by the petrichor of rain about to fall.
Feel
The mark when it appears: warmth, specific and localized, like a brand that doesn't burn. It fades to normal skin temperature within a minute. What remains is not heat — it is the knowledge that something has changed permanently.
Taste
The iron taste is gone before the mark finishes appearing. What replaces it: nothing unusual. The ordinary taste of dawn air, and rain about to fall. Ordinary is notable after five sessions of not-ordinary.

Consequence Menu

Mark of Mars: Handout 10. Advantage on Intimidation checks. Once per long rest: ask "What does Mars want from this situation?" and receive an honest answer. In exchange: he may call once. At his choosing.

The Chosen (1–2 characters, crit success): The Mark plus a Call to War obligation. When he calls, they come. Refuse = mark sears, lose benefits until the quest is completed.

The Debt: If they bargained at any point, one favor owed. "When I call, you will come." Simple. Exact.

The Transformation: Offered to any player wanting a noble exit — become a guardian spirit of the arena. The fort earns permanent Advantage on morale checks. That character's story ends here, at their choice.

Prophecy: Vindolanda becomes a pilgrimage site for a century. Rome will fall from within in three generations. He says it simply, as a fact, not a warning. "I have seen it before."

Mars Departs

He does not leave dramatically. He becomes less present — the way the temperature of a room changes when someone who was in it leaves. The arena sand remains for approximately one minute after he is gone. Then it is gone too.

The rain drops fall.

The frozen arrows fall into the sand and vanish. There is no physical evidence they were ever there.

The fort reasserts. The north gate is exactly as it was before the siege paused — whatever state the party left it in. The Germanic forces outside have heard it too. They are discussing what to do next. That window is Scene 4.

If Mars Leaves Dissatisfied

He does not destroy the fort. He is a god of war, not of punishment. But he leaves without the Mark. The spear is gone regardless — it was his to begin with. What remains: a cold wind off the sand for a moment, and then: nothing. No mark. No blessing. No consequence menu. The party carries the absence.

The siege resumes in full. The Germanic forces outside heard nothing — no pause, no divine intervention, no reason to hesitate. The campaign ends without resolution of the divine thread. That is a valid ending. Not every campaign concludes with divine approval.

Corruption Level 5 — After Trial

The corruption does not automatically clear with the trial. If Mars offered the Mark, the Mark's presence burns against Level 5 corruption — the character must choose: accept the Mark and let the corruption fight it (d4 levels reduced per long rest until gone, with Wisdom DC 13 each time), or refuse the Mark and keep the corruption as it is.

"Cannot shed corruption without a selfless deed unseen by any authority, within one year." The deed must be real. Mars will know if it happened. He will not say anything. The corruption will simply be gone one morning.
Beat 1 — The World Reasserts

The rain falls. The arrows have already vanished. Frozen soldiers complete their movements — mid-salute, mid-shout, mid-step — with no gap in their memory. From their perspective, nothing happened between one moment and the next.

Varro finishes his salute. He holds it for a long moment. Then lowers his arm. He says nothing immediately.

Outside the north gate: the Germanic forces are conferring. Whatever they heard/felt through the divine pause — they need time to decide. The party has approximately one hour before the siege resumes or concludes by other means.

THE MOMENT — Witnesses Come Forward

Cassia first. Her crow lands on her shoulder and is still. She does not say "I saw." She says: "I told you." — past tense, referencing augury sessions 1 and 2. She was always right. She needs someone to acknowledge that.

Varro second. He waits until Cassia is done. One sentence. He has had five sessions to decide what it is. You wrote it before this session — based on what actually happened in your campaign. It references something specific. Give it to the player who paid the most attention to him.

Then the citizenship document. If Lucius sent the dispatch in Session 4, a rider has been waiting at the gate since dawn. Imperial epistula, sealed. One sentence in a different hand: "Distinguished service on the Germanic frontier, confirmed by two independent sponsors. The Emperor's office takes note."

Beat 3 — Epilogue Questions Begin

The siege outside resolves (however it resolves — leave this to the established fiction of who is leading it and what they decide when the divine pause ends). The party gathers. The epilogue opens.

Ask each player: "Where are you, one year later?" Wait for the answer before asking the next. Do not rush this.

Five Senses — Scene 4

Sight
The rain falling — ordinary, northwest British frontier rain. The parade ground: normal stone, worn smooth by boots. The sand is simply gone. No trace, no residue. Varro standing with his salute arm coming down slowly. Cassia with the crow on her shoulder.
Sound
Rain on stone. The confused voices of soldiers who completed movements they can't account for. From outside: Germanic voices in conference — not battle sounds, deliberation sounds. The fort is quiet in the specific way that comes after something that was not quite violence.
Smell
Rain on dry stone — petrichor, specific and real. The iron smell is completely gone. The smoke from the granary fire (if it was set) has cleared. The air smells ordinary. After five sessions of not-ordinary, ordinary is striking.
Feel
The mark on each character: settled now, room temperature. Permanent but quiet. Like a scar that doesn't hurt — you know it's there without looking. The black sand left the parade ground warm; the rain is cooling it slowly.
Taste
Rain. Food, suddenly — appetite returns with the normalization of the world. After five sessions of divine interference in the body's chemistry, the body wants something simple. Someone is hungry.

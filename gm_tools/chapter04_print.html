The Blood Cult Dungeon
A causeway of bone across a glowing marsh. A dungeon curated, not looted, by two chieftains who got exactly what they prayed for. What devotion costs, and what's waiting at the bottom of it.

Scene 0: The Marsh

The trees thin out and the ground goes soft underfoot before you see why. Ahead, the hillside opens in a mouth — not a metaphor. A skull carved from the living rock, easily twenty feet across, its eye sockets black even at midday. A causeway of bone and old skulls runs from between its teeth down into a wide, glowing green marsh that splits around the path like water around a stone. The light doesn't flicker like fire. It breathes, slow, the way something alive breathes.

The bone causeway is the only dry footing across the marsh.

Marsh hazard (leaving the causeway): DC 12 Constitution save each round in the glowing water, or one level of exhaustion + 1 temporary corruption point. The causeway itself is always safe — this discourages wading without being lethal.

Two supply caches at the marsh's near corners: DC 11 Wisdom (Investigation) turns up rations gone green, a tally-marked stick (the cult has been here a long time), and — second cache — a torn strip of cloth matching the two chieftains' warband colors. Third and final confirmation this is the right place.

2 × Banshee
Undead, CR 4 each — early followers who didn't survive tending this place

Corrupting Touch. 3d6 necrotic.

Horrifying Visage. DC 13 Wisdom, fear + aging on a failure.

Wail (1/day). DC 13 Constitution, 10d8 psychic; the Banshee dies at 0 HP.

Trigger: the Banshees rise mid-causeway, roughly a third of the way across — no easy retreat, no easy grouping. Stagger their Wails; never let both go off in the same round.

Scene 1: The Ossuary

The room ahead is round, and its far wall is shaped like the thing you just walked through — another skull, smaller, teeth picked out in inlaid bone. The floor is a carpet of femurs and ribs, stacked with a care that took someone a long time.

History DC 13 (active): these bones are sorted by size, by age, deliberately — curated for months, not years. Matches the marsh tally-stick.

2 × Bone Naga
Undead, CR 4 each

Bite. 4d6+3 piercing.

Hold Person / Ray of Enfeeblement (spellcasting) — control, not just damage.

Rejuvenation. Returns in 1d6 days if destroyed.

Trigger: wait until a character disturbs the bone floor or casts a spell. Stack Hold Person on the party's striker, Ray of Enfeeblement on a melee threat.

Scene 2: The Blood-Growth Rooms

The stone here has stopped being stone. Something has grown over it — clusters of dark red matter, wet-looking, veined, thick as a fist in places and swollen tight. They shift, very slightly, when you get close. Not with the room's air currents.

Medicine or Nature DC 12 (active): organic, fed on blood, deliberately and repeatedly, over a long period.

Yochlol + 2 × Dretch
Fiend (Demon), CR 6 / CR 1/4 each

Yochlol — Poisonous Tentacle. 2d6+2 poison + grapple, reach 15 ft. Change Shape (spider form). Magic Resistance. Insect Plague 1/day if the fight runs long.

Dretch — Bite/Claws. Chaff, not a threat alone. Fetid Cloud (recharge 6): DC 11 Constitution, poison, 10-ft radius.

Trigger: the Yochlol drops from the ceiling once the party investigates the growths. Dretches emerge a round later — the room "reacting," not reinforcements.

Scene 3: The Minor Altar

Four pillars ring a low dais at the room's center, and the dais itself pulses — not light exactly, more like a heartbeat you can see. This isn't the room you came here looking for. It feels like a rehearsal for one.

Religion DC 14 (active): this altar never completed a working — every mark is a false start. Whatever the cult built up to happened elsewhere.

2 × Shadow Demon
Fiend (Demon), CR 4 each

Claw. 3d6 necrotic + reduces max HP.

Shadow Stealth (bonus action, dim light). Illumination/Sunlight Weakness.

Trigger: on sight, but they vanish between the four pillars instead of engaging directly — striking from a different pillar each round. Being stalked, not a fair fight.

Scene 4: The Rune Lock

Four tiles are set into the floor here, each etched with a different symbol, each lit from beneath in a different color — cyan, green, magenta, yellow. There's no door ahead. Whatever this room is guarding, it's guarding it by making you choose.
DetailEffect
SolutionCyan → Green → Yellow → Magenta ("beginning, growth, harvest, blood"). Investigation/Insight DC 13 on wear patterns hints at order.
SuccessWall grinds aside, direct passage to the Gathering Hall.
FailureDC 12 Dexterity save or 2d6 force damage, sequence resets. No lockout, ever.

Session's breather beat — no combat by default. A fled Dretch from Scene 2 can ambush only if pacing needs it.

Scene 5: The Gathering Hall

This room was built to hold people. Rows of worn stone mark where dozens once knelt, facing a long table at the far end, still laid with offerings gone black with age. Whatever congregation gathered here, it hasn't gathered in some time. But something is still standing at that table.
3 × Wraith
Undead, CR 5 each — the two chieftains + a devoted follower

Life Drain. Standard Wraith attack.

Create Specter. From any humanoid corpse killed in the last minute within 10 ft. Cap total specters at 3 for table sanity.

This room needs to land as tragedy. The chieftains got exactly what they thought they wanted, and it consumed them entirely. First round: recognition, not aggression — they turn slowly, watch, say nothing. If spoken to directly before combat: one fragmentary sentence each ("we asked —" / "it heard us"). Stagger Create Specter the same way the Banshees' Wails were staggered.

Scene 6: The Core Room

The stair curves up into a perfectly circular chamber. Four stone faces watch from the walls at the compass points — not gods, not ancestors, just teeth and eyes carved to be looked at from every angle at once. At the center, on a low dais, a crystal the shape of a broken star burns steady and cold. It is the only thing in this entire dungeon that looks cared for.

Arcana or Religion DC 15 (active, or automatic if Vercingetorix already spoke): this is the cult's real altar and the marsh's real source — every drop of blood below was tribute funneled here, keeping something that answers to Mars's name fed and present a little longer. The rooms below weren't separate encounters. They were one working.

Glabrezu
Fiend (Demon), CR 9 — bound to the focus, not merely standing near it
HP157
AC17
SPD40

Multiattack. 2 Pincers (grapple, reach 10 ft) + 2 Fists vs. grappled targets.

Innate Spellcasting. Darkness/Dispel Magic/Detect Magic at will; Confusion/Fly/Power Word Stun 1/day, DC 16.

Magic Resistance. Truesight 120 ft.

Opens with Darkness over the dais, then isolates whoever gets closest with Confusion or Power Word Stun. Will not chase a fleeing party far — its purpose is guarding the focus, not hunting.
The crystal cannot be destroyed here. Trying visibly fails — it doesn't crack, doesn't dim, simply absorbs whatever hits it. Doing that is Session 5's job. This scene ends with the party knowing the name of the place they need to go next and the shape of the cost waiting there, not with the spear's story resolved.

Advance to Level 7.

NPC Secrets

The two chieftains (now Wraiths). Objective: feed the war-god who could finally give their people lasting victories. Secret: they were not lied to about Mars's hunger — they were right about what he wanted. They were wrong about what answering it would cost them personally.
Tribune Lucius (present, per Session 3). Watches, mostly — this dungeon confirms everything he already suspected about what the spear does to ambitious men who think they can control it. Covers the party's retreat if the Core Room fight goes badly; does not touch the crystal himself under any circumstance.

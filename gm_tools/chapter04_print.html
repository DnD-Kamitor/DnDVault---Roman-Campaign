Scene 1: The Germanic Village

The village smells of woodsmoke and old bread. Chickens. A dog that barks once and stops. Two fires burning where ten should be. The longhouses are solid work, well kept, but half the doors stand open on empty rooms. A woman stops grinding grain and watches you without moving. A child runs inside. An old man at the fire does not look up.

Then the woman's eyes go past you, to the small grey shape on your back. The quern stone stops turning. Nobody in the village breathes.

Dramatic Question: Can the party earn the village's trust fast enough to learn where the seer went, and what the boy on their back really is to these people?

The woman at the quern is the boy's mother. She knows him on sight. She does not scream. She walks to the party, touches his wet hair, and says: "They told us the bog took you." Then, to the party, very quietly: "The chieftains took him north, to the old altar in the grove. His bones are still there. Lay him with them and he can sleep." The villagers add that Thusnelda went south, to the sacrifice stones, with the old knife.

What the village knows

  • Free: the grove and the old altar are north; the skull-faced hill is south, down the old offering path.
  • Free: their seer Thusnelda (an older man, he/him) walked south to the sacrifice stones before dawn with the old knife. Three followed and came back frightened.
  • Free: the two chieftains led the warband south months ago. People stopped asking.
  • DC 13 Persuasion (advantage: they brought the boy home): for two weeks Thusnelda spoke of "settling the gods' debt."
  • DC 13 History or Persuasion: his family has read omens here for generations. Right for twenty years. Not mad. That is what makes this dangerous.

Scene 2: North, the Sacred Grove

The oaks here are old enough that their roots have lifted the path. At the grove's centre, a stone no taller than a man stands alone, its face carved on the side facing the village: two faces, really, or one face trying to be two. Roman workmanship on top, older marks underneath. Someone has left fresh holly. Someone else has smeared ash across the Roman features. Behind it, a low altar black with old blood, and a man standing in the middle of them.

Dramatic Question: Will they stop to understand the shrine before they run to the circle?

Belatucadrus (DC 13 Religion): the Celtic sun god Rome renamed "Mars" on the frontier. Consecration: a Cleric or Druid prays 10 minutes, no slot: one weapon gains the Belatucadrus blessing (counts as magical; advantage on damage rolls against cult-marked creatures; defeats Vercingetorix's Relentless in Session 7).
Sun-Path carving (puzzle clue for Scene 8): around the stone's base, a carved sun is shown four times, rising in the east, high in the south, setting in the west, and dark in the north. DC 10 Investigation, or automatic for anyone who walks round the stone. Note who saw it.

Scene 3: South, the Sacrifice Stones

Geography: the grove and the boy's altar are NORTH of the village; the seven-stone ring is SOUTH, on the way to the skull. Thusnelda's ritual is a false unmaking: he wants the spear on the centre stone and his blood on it, believing that breaks the spear. The stones feed Mars instead. Clues it is wrong: Religion/History DC 14 (war-runes cut over sun-marks), Arcana DC 14 (power flows into the stone), the boy's bones, or a wisp riddle ("Blood is what it eats"). Clock: 3 rounds in the ring (1 round if the spear is set on the stone), then 2 Stone Golems tear loose; they cannot leave the ring, so it is a run, not a fight. Full table: cheat card tab "2-3 North Grove / South Stones".
He stands in the centre of the ring with the stillness of someone who has made up his mind. His feet are bare on the cold stone. The knife in his hand is old work, older than him, older than the village. His left wrist is scored, blood running down his fingers and dropping into the earth. He is watching it fall. He hears you come and does not look up.

"You are too late to stop the debt. But you can witness. A willing death settles what war begins. The gods will be satisfied. Your soldiers will stop burning our grain."

Dramatic Question: Can they stop him before the cut becomes fatal?

3 rounds (2 if they lingered). Each round a PC stands inside the ring: DC 13 Wis save or 1 temporary corruption.

The old altar: the boy's bones

In the NORTH grove, behind the shrine, stands the old altar the chieftains used: the "temple" of the village's story. Under it, wrapped in a scrap of the same rotted wool the Myling wears, lie a child's bones (DC 12 Investigation, or automatic if the boy is carried into the ring: he goes quiet and reaches for them). The bones are Path A evidence for Thusnelda: "That is not an offering. That is a murder dressed as one."

Scene 4: The Skull Entrance

The offering path ends at a hillside that has a face. A skull carved from the living rock, its eye sockets dark even at midday. Inside its mouth, stairs descend into darkness. The air coming up is cold and smells of old blood. And at the base of the stairs, two shapes uncoil from the stone: serpentine, skeletal, moving with a purpose that bones alone do not explain.

Dramatic Question: Can they fight down a stair two abreast without being pinned on it?

Tactics: the stair is 10 ft. wide. The nagas open with hold person on whoever leads, then Bite at reach 10 ft. On initiative 20 of round 1, four skeletons assemble from the bone piles at the stair foot. The box means no surprise: the nagas are waiting. Bludgeoning wrecks the skeletons (the nagas are not vulnerable). Watch for lightning bolt up the stair.

Encounter: 2 × Bone Naga + 4 × Skeleton. Hard for five Level 6 PCs (adjusted 4,800 XP).

Scene 5: The Ossuary

The room ahead is round, and its far wall is shaped like the thing you just walked through: another skull, smaller, teeth picked out in inlaid bone. The floor is a carpet of femurs and ribs. Not scattered. Sorted: by size, by age, stacked with a care that took someone a very long time.

In the far wall, three niches, each carved with a single rune. Deeper in the dungeon, something breathes.

Dramatic Question: Will they understand what this room is for, and find the way down?

Puzzle: the Three Niches (the way down)

The far wall has three skull-niches carved with Elder Futhark runes: FEHU (cattle, wealth), TIWAZ (the war-god, victory), OTHALAN (home, inheritance). A bone offering placed in the right niche makes the skull's jaw grind open onto the lower stair.

Answer: OTHALAN. The cult's working is the theft of home and inheritance: a village's children, its warband, its future. (The obvious guess, TIWAZ for the war-god, is the trap.)

Three clues: (1) the boy's only word is "Home"; (2) the party already carries the OTHALAN rune: Lucius's amber token, the scorched mark on Rivia's box, the bone token from the Farbog stone; (3) DC 13 Religion or History: the cult tally sticks scratched OTHALAN over every name they took.

Wrong niche: the sorted bones stir. 2 skeletons assemble (no lockout; try again). TIWAZ also makes the box hum loudly: every undead below now knows they are coming.

Short rest: the Ossuary is safe once the nagas are down. Offer a short rest here.

Scene 6: The Blood-Growth Room (cuttable)

The corridor walls stop being stone. Dark red matter, wet-looking and veined, has grown over them like ivy over a ruin, thickening as you go. At the centre of the room it rises into a single pillar, taller than a man, and inside it, pressed against the skin of it, something with too many limbs turns over in its sleep. The pillar pulses. Faster, now that you are here.

Dramatic Question: Can they kill it before it is born?

Pressure cooker: 3 rounds. The Gestating Yochlol is rooted (speed 0, reach 10 ft.). At the start of round 4, if the pillar stands, it tears free with +40 HP. The pillar: AC 10, 30 HP, vulnerable to fire and radiant. Destroying it halves the yochlol's HP and it can never hatch. Two iron brazier stands sit just outside the door: a torch or oil turns them into fire damage.
DC 12 Medicine or Nature: the growth is organic and was fed on purpose. DC 14 Arcana: the pillar is the womb; burn the womb.

Encounter: Gestating Yochlol + 2 Dretch. Hard while rooted; deadly if it hatches.

Scene 7: The Rune Lock

A round iron door with no handle. Four glass studs are set in it, each a different colour: blue, green, yellow, red. Faint marks of fingers, many fingers, are worn into the iron around them.

Solution: touch the studs blue → green → yellow → red ("beginning, growth, harvest, blood": the cult's year, ending in the slaughter).

Three clues: (1) DC 12 Investigation: the finger-wear is deepest on blue and fades in that order; (2) a dead cultist's wax tablet in the Ossuary niches: "sow, grow, reap, bleed"; (3) DC 13 Nature: the colours are the marsh in spring, summer, autumn, and the colour of the Farbog water.

Failure: DC 12 Dex save or 2d6 force; the sequence resets. No lockout, ever.

Scene 8: The Minor Altar (cuttable)

Four square pillars ring a low dais. An iron brazier stands at each point of the compass, cold, packed with old pitch. The dark between the pillars is wrong: it sits too thick, and it moves when you do not. Something in it is laughing without a mouth.

Dramatic Question: Can they bring the sun underground?

Puzzle: the Sun-Path

Light the braziers in the course of the sun: east, south, west, north. Each brazier is an action to light (torch, fire cantrip, or DC 11 Dex with flint while threatened). When the fourth catches in the right order, the room floods with gold light: bright light everywhere. The Shadow Demons suffer Light Sensitivity (disadvantage on attacks) and lose Shadow Stealth.

Three clues: (1) the Sun-Path carving on the Belatucadrus shrine (Scene 2); (2) Thusnelda's prayer, if he was saved; (3) DC 13 Investigation: the east brazier's soot is thickest and the north one's is thinnest.

Wrong order: all four gutter out and the dark thickens: the demons have advantage on their next attack. Start again. A Belatucadrus-consecrated weapon touched to any brazier lights all four at once.

Encounter: 3 × Shadow Demon. Hard in the dark; easy in the light.

Scene 9a: The Gathering Hall

Rows of worn stone mark where dozens once knelt, facing a long table still laid with offerings gone black. Two figures stand at the head of the table. They were big men once. Their armour is good Germanic work, and it hangs on nothing now. They turn together, and one of them speaks in a voice like wind in an empty longhouse:

"We asked. It heard us. Why did it hear us?"

Dramatic Question: Do the chieftains get to confess before they are destroyed?

Land this as tragedy. Round 1 is recognition, not aggression. The party can talk:
  • DC 14 Religion or Persuasion (or show Lucius's carved wooden horse: one of them carved it for a fostered Roman boy, long ago): they confess. They asked Mars for victory against Rome. He gave them strength, and the price was their whole warband, one man at a time, into the bog. Then them.
  • DC 15 Intimidation (+2 from the box): one chieftain fades, ashamed; only one fights.
  • Either success: they reveal the Core Room below holds "the star that drinks," and a fiend "that sells what you want most."

Encounter: 2 × Wraith (the two chieftains). Hard (adjusted 5,400 XP); one wraith if Intimidated.

Scene 9b: The Core Room ★ Session Ends Here

Four stone faces watch from the walls. At the centre of the room, on a raised dais, a crystal shaped like a broken star burns steady and cold: the only thing in this dungeon that looks cared for. Something huge squats beside it, four-armed, dog-headed, its pincers folded like a clerk's hands. It smiles with too many teeth and speaks in good Latin.

"Soldiers. You carry something that belongs to my master. Let us not be crude. I can give you what you want most. Every one of you wants something."

Dramatic Question: Will anyone take the bargain?

The Glabrezu's bargain (play it straight, let it tempt): it offers Ursula freedom from the greataxe's hunger; Rivia an answer that makes her box mistake "never have happened"; any PC citizenship, rank, a dead friend's voice. The price is always the same: open the box and leave the spear on the dais. Insight DC 15: it means every word, and its master would get the spear either way. If anyone agrees, the box opens, Mars's attention lands on that PC (2 corruption), and the party must fight to get the spear back before the session ends.
The crystal (Arcana or Religion DC 15): the cult's real altar. Every drop of blood shed in the dungeon was funnelled here, and down. It absorbs all damage. It cannot be destroyed in this session.

End of Session: the cliffhanger

The fiend falls, and the crystal-star does not go dark. It gets brighter. Cracks spread across its faces like frost across a window, and each one lets out a note too low to be a sound, felt in the teeth. The floor is warm. Behind the dais, a stair you did not see before goes down into red light. In Rivia's arms, the box is humming in exactly the same rhythm as the crystal. Whatever is down there has stopped sleeping.

Stop here. The Core Room seals behind them; a long rest is possible. Advance to Level 7. Session 7 opens on the stair (Chapter 5).

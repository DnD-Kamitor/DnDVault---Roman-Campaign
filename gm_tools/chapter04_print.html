Scene 0: The Farbog Crossing

The trees end and the ground turns to black water. Ahead, a causeway of old skulls and packed bone runs across the marsh toward a hillside that has a face — a skull carved from the living rock, easily twenty feet across, its eye sockets dark even now. The water on either side glows green. Not fire. Something slower, like a pulse. At the bank's edge, a standing stone. Runes cut deep. Old work — older than the causeway. Someone wanted something remembered here.

Dramatic Question: Can the party cross the bone causeway without waking the drowned Wights beneath the glowing black water?

Pressure Cooker (Sinking Causeway): Leaving the bone causeway or lingering causes the packed bone to sink 6 inches every 2 rounds. After 4 rounds, footing is difficult terrain (DC 12 Dex check to avoid slipping into corrosive mire; DC 12 Con save each round in water or 1 exhaustion + 1 temp corruption).

The Standing Stone Riddle: DC 13 Int (History) translates: "Born from the willing, fed by the blood, I grow in the silence where no god has stood. Worship without truth, devotion without cost — I am what remains after the believer is lost. Name me."

Answer: "The hunger", "false devotion", or "the cult's work". Correct naming yields a hidden carved bone token with the OTHALAN rune (one-time reroll on any save vs undead ability).

Three Clue Rule (Locating Entrance): Two supply caches on the near bank (DC 11 Investigation) reveal rotting grain, cult tally sticks, and warband cloth matching the two chieftains — confirming the path south to the skull entrance.
The Box Cataclysm (Rivia's Blunder): Rivia placed the Spear of Mars inside the box under dark influence. After Ursula was possessed by the Goliath Berserker Greataxe in the forest fight, Rivia locked the greataxe in the box thinking it would nullify the curse. The two artifacts resonated violently, scorching the OTHALAN rune into the wood and projecting Mars's aura (+2 Intimidation). In the Farbog, undead detect the party from double range: the group Stealth DC 14 check is at disadvantage unless the box is heavily wrapped or kept shielded.

Avoidance / Stealth: DC 14 group Dexterity (Stealth) lets the party slip across without disturbing the water. On failure or stepping into water, the Wights rise.

Scene 1: The Germanic Village

The village smells of woodsmoke and old bread. Chickens. A dog that barks once and stops. Two fires burning where ten should be. The longhouses are solid work, well-kept, but half the doors stand open on empty rooms. A woman stops grinding grain and watches you without moving. A child runs inside. An old man at the fire does not look up. Whatever took this village apart, it was not a raid. This place just... thinned.

Dramatic Question: Can the party earn the trust of the abandoned villagers to discover where Thusnelda went before his sacrifice is complete?

What the Villagers Know (DC 13 Persuasion):
  • Their venerable völva (he/him), Thusnelda, walked north to the sacred grove at dawn with a ritual blade to "settle the gods' debt". Three villagers followed and returned terrified.
  • The clan's two chieftains led their warband south months ago down the old offering path and never returned.
  • DC 13 History/Persuasion: Thusnelda has read omens correctly for twenty years. His people trust him unconditionally — making his desperate self-sacrifice lethal.

Scene 2: The Sacred Grove

The oaks here are old enough that their roots have lifted the path. At the grove's center, a stone no taller than a man stands alone, its face carved on the side facing the village — two faces, really, or one face trying to be two. Roman workmanship on top, older marks underneath. Someone has left fresh holly. Someone else has smeared ash across the Roman features.

Dramatic Question: Can the party decipher the ancient Belatucadrus shrine and consecrate a weapon before reaching the circle of sacrifice?

Belatucadrus Consecration: DC 13 Int (Religion) recognizes the syncretic Celtic sun god / Roman Mars. 10 minutes of prayer consecrates a weapon: advantage on damage rolls against blood cult creatures and counts as magical for the Ossuary.

Scene 3: The Ritual Sacrifice Circle

He stands in the centre of the ring with the stillness of someone who has made up his mind. His feet are bare on the cold stone. The knife in his hand is old work — older than him, older than the village. His left wrist is scored, blood running down his fingers and dropping into the earth. He is watching it fall. He hears you come and does not look up. "You are too late to stop the debt. But you can witness. A willing death settles what war begins. The gods will be satisfied. Your soldiers will stop burning our grain."

Dramatic Question: Can the party stop Thusnelda's ritual suicide before the blood circle activates?

Pressure Cooker (The Ritual Clock): Started at dawn; the party has exactly 3 rounds to intervene before the cut is fatal. Each round spent inside the circle forces a DC 13 Wis save vs 1 temporary corruption point.
Box Diplomacy Penalty: Mars's violent aura radiating from the box imposes disadvantage on calm diplomacy. If carried within 30 ft of Thusnelda, he smells the bloodlust of Mars and recoils — Path A and Path B Persuasion checks are made at disadvantage unless the box is kept 30+ ft away!
Intervention PathMechanicsConsequences
Path A: Evidence DC 14 Persuasion (Advantage if showing cult cloth, tally stick, or symbol) Lowers knife. Convinced the hunger is cult-made. Waits at dungeon mouth; guides party south down offering path.
Path B: Rhetoric Only DC 18 Persuasion (Double prof for Outlander/Hermit) Success stops him shaken. Failure: finishes ritual; 1 round left to heal or bind wounds.
Path C: Physical Restraint DC 13 Athletics/Acrobatics (Grapple or Disarm) Restrained; does not attack party. Buys time to present Path A evidence.
Path D: No Action / Fail Party allows cut to finish Dies in 1 hr without healing magic. Grove blessing lost; party loses guide. Mars remains hungry.
Thusnelda's Intel (Unlocked if Saved): "The dungeon entrance is south down the old offering path. The first room below has been making the dead stay. Someone sorted the bones... someone was proud of that work."

Scene 4: The Skull Entrance

The offering path ends at a hillside that has a face. A skull carved from the living rock, its eye sockets dark even at midday. Inside its mouth, stairs descend into darkness. The air coming up is cold and smells of old blood. And at the base of the stairs, two shapes uncoil from the stone — serpentine, skeletal, moving with a purpose that bones alone do not explain.

Dramatic Question: Can the party defeat the Bone Nagas and shatter the skeletal guardians before being trapped on the narrow entrance stairway?

Chokepoint Tactics: The stone stair is narrow (two abreast). Nagas cast Hold Person (DC 12) and Ray of Enfeeblement to lock frontliners on the stairs while 4 Skeletons animate from loose bones on initiative count 20.

Scene 5: The Ossuary ★ S4 ENDS HERE

The room ahead is round, and its far wall is shaped like the thing you just walked through — another skull, smaller, teeth picked out in inlaid bone. The floor is a carpet of femurs and ribs. Not scattered. Sorted — by size, by age, stacked with a care that took someone a very long time. Deeper in the dungeon, something breathes.

Dramatic Question: Can the party interpret the curated ossuary's ritual pattern to discover the safe descent into the lower vault?

History DC 13 (active): These are not war dead. They are curated — months of deliberate ritual work matching the marsh tally stick. Session 4 concludes at this threshold. The party advances to Level 6.

Lower Chambers & Session 5 Transition

Beyond the Ossuary lie the deeper chambers that funnel blood into the lower vault of Mars:

  • Blood-Growth Rooms: Yochlol (CR 6, AC 15, HP 136) & 2 × Dretch (CR 1/4, Fetid Cloud).
  • Minor Altar: 3 × Shadow Demon (CR 4, AC 12, HP 66, radiant vuln; braziers trigger Sunlight Weakness).
  • Gathering Hall: 3 × Wraith (CR 5, AC 13, HP 67, Chieftains' spirits).
  • Core Room: Glabrezu (CR 9, AC 17, HP 157) guarding the cracking crystal-star focus. Defeating it allows a Long Rest to reach Level 7 before descending into Chapter 5.

NPC Secrets

Thusnelda (Völva Seer): He is not mad or evil. He has read omens for twenty years correctly, but the cult's blood rites corrupted the forest's spiritual ground. His self-sacrifice would accomplish nothing; Mars's hunger would remain unquenched.
The Bone Nagas: Placed at the entrance by the cult chieftains to prevent anyone from desecrating the curated bones of their fallen brethren. Both Nagas and Skeletons share vulnerability to bludgeoning damage.
The Two Chieftains (Wraiths): Mars did not trick them with false words — he gave them military strength, and the divine price was the slow consumption of their entire warband.
Glabrezu (Core Room): Manifested from the planar rip torn by months of continuous blood sacrifice. Its defeat stabilizes the upper chambers, opening the descent into the Vault of Mars.

Chapter 3
Through the Dark Forest
Shadow of Mars Campaign — Session 3 of 5
Level 5 → 6  |  3–4 Hours  |  6 Players
Travel + Investigation + Sacred Grove Ritual
The party carries a corrupting divine weapon through the Teutoburg Forest toward the only place it can be destroyed. The forest is old, hostile, and watching. Someone in the group is no longer entirely themselves.

Scene 0: Cold Open

Start in media res. Five seconds of silence. Then:

You wake before dawn. The forest is completely silent.

Not quiet. Silent. No birds. No insects. No wind in the branches. The kind of silence that has weight.

The spear is glowing.

A faint red light pulses from its wrappings like a heartbeat. Around the cart — everything within five feet of it is still. A leaf is frozen mid-fall between the spear and the ground.

One of your companions is standing beside it. They've been there for some time. They aren't moving.

On a nearby tree, carved deep into the bark:

MARS VIDET OMNIA

Give Handout 7 now. Do not explain who carved it. Do not resolve the sleepwalking. Cut to Scene 1 immediately.

Scene 1: Something Is Wrong

The party must discover which companion the spear has compromised. Three independent clues are available — they don't need all three, but the revelation should feel earned.

Clue 1 — Physical Signs
Perception DC 13 (passive, during travel) or Medicine DC 13 (active examination).
Eyes faintly red in direct light. Rubbing the same spot on their arm. Sleeping with their face turned toward where the spear is stored — even when it moved.
Clue 2 — Behaviour
Insight DC 14 (active: watching them during any conversation about the spear).
Advocates keeping it more strongly than before, but can't articulate why. Visibly agitated when someone else touches the spear. Micro-pause before "I'm fine."
Clue 3 — The Spear Reacts
Arcana DC 13 (passive) or Religion DC 14 (passive: examining the wrappings).
The spear physically tilts toward that person. Cassia, if present: "There's a thread between that person and the spear. I missed it. I'm sorry, I missed it."
Private Note (pass folded to influenced player at session start):
"You woke last night and stood beside the spear for two hours. You don't remember doing it. The spear feels warm when you're near it. It has been making a quiet argument: the power in this weapon could protect the people you care about. You haven't told anyone. You're not sure you're wrong."

Scene 2: Thusnelda's Scouts

If Allied with Vercingetorix

Four figures appear in the trees. Forest-dark clothes, no armor. One raises a hand — not a weapon.

Vercingetorix says quietly: "My people. We are safe here."

The scouts take the party to Thusnelda. Toll: information about Roman troop movements. Technical treason. Practically necessary.

If Not Allied

Ambush — 12 Tribal Warriors + 2 Scouts (CR 1/2). Perception DC 12 (active): semi-circle of disturbance to both flanks, none ahead — any character who covers a flank before initiative has advantage on their first roll. Ends diplomatically on Persuasion DC 14 with any Germanic phrase, or by demonstrating they're destroying the spear. Vercingetorix arrives after 3+ rounds regardless.

Thusnelda — First Words

A small, ancient-looking Germanic woman. Oak staff carved with spiraling runes. She smells of pine smoke and something metallic. She looks at the spear. Then at the party — with the calm gaze of someone who has spent decades looking at hard things.

"God-Killer. Still breathing. I had hoped you would destroy it by now. I see you have not."

Give Handout 6 now (DC 14 Persuasion success or Vercingetorix vouches).

DM: Thusnelda's truth
She performed this ritual once before, fifty years ago, with Vercingetorix's grandfather. It failed because the sacrifice was insufficient. She has carried that failure since. She will not let it happen again — that is why she tells the party everything now, in the forest, before they stand at the altar making panicked decisions.
She will sayShe will not say
Exactly what the ritual requiresWhat the "right" sacrifice is for any specific character
That the grove rejects insufficient sacrificesWhether a sacrifice is "good enough" before it is offered
That someone in the party is influencedWho — she knows, but it is the party's work to discover

Sample lines: On how she knows: "My teacher's teacher sealed it. I have been waiting since I was eleven years old. I am seventy-three. You are late." On alternatives: "There is not. I have thought about this for sixty years. This is not an opinion." On the influenced NPC: "Your friend there has a thread in them. Cut it soon. The forest is not kind to loose threads."

Scene 3b: The Farbog

The ground changes before you see it. The grass shortens, then disappears. Water appears between the roots. The trees grow thin and angular — bone-pale trunks, dead branches reaching sideways.

The smell is iron and rot and something sweet underneath. Not pleasant-sweet. The smell of things left here a long time.

Thusnelda raises one hand. "There are old ones here. Do not touch the water. Do not take anything. Do not run. If something approaches — stop and let me speak first."

Far ahead, a light pulses once. Blue-white. Then dark.
Disrespect tracker: Mentally count transgressions — damaged shrine (Event 5), attacked the Myling, took from the sacrifice site without acknowledgment, ignored the boundary stone. Two or more triggers Encounter C (Hostile Vættir).

Encounter A — The Myling

Child-shaped, translucent, wrong. Stands on a hummock 30 feet off-path. Hands extended. Does not approach.

  • Ignore: Follows at 20 feet, weeps after 10 minutes. DC 13 Wisdom save or Frightened 1 hour. One disrespect.
  • Acknowledge (Religion DC 12): Say "Unnamed one, we see you" and drop any item of value into the water. The spirit brightens, nods, stills.
  • Ask Thusnelda: She performs the rite but loses one ritual prep action in Scene 4 (takes an extra round).
  • Attack: It dissipates. Two more appear at the next hummock.
Cassia (if present, after the rite): "The Romans burn our dead. The Germans bury theirs with grave goods. We both know the same thing, don't we? That the dead need witness."

She doesn't say this to anyone in particular. Let it breathe.

Encounter B — The Bog Sacrifice Site

Twenty meters off-path: offering pole, three meters tall, knotwork carvings. At its base, birch bark offerings. Beneath the water, barely visible: a preserved human figure.

  • Investigation DC 13: Maintained recently. New bark. A Roman military sandal buckle.
  • Religion DC 15 (one minute contact with pole): Two religious traditions active. One old, rooted, comfortable. One new, hot, angry. Not in harmony.
  • Thusnelda's reaction: "Someone has been using our holy sites. For something else. Mars has been here. Not recently. But he has been here."
  • Bog valerian (Cassia's herb): Nature DC 13 to identify. Gathering requires leaving the path (Perception DC 11 to spot soft ground). Payoff in Session 4: Siege Ward — 3 uses, each restores 2d8 HP and removes 1 exhaustion level.

Encounter C — Hostile Vættir (conditional)

Run only on two or more disrespect triggers.

Five, seven, more blue-white lights move toward the path. Purposefully. Use Will-o'-Wisp stat blocks (CR 2): cannot flee, cannot be Turned, deal cold damage.

Dispersed by Group Acknowledgment: Every party member simultaneously drops one item of value into the water channels. DC 10 Religion (group, one action each) to coordinate.

  • Success: Lights halt, pause, drift back one by one.
  • Failure: One additional Vættir per round. Cannot be outrun.

Scene 4: The Sacred Grove

Approach: Survival DC 11 (active, before stepping into the stone ring): the grass inside is simply absent — not grazed. Two natural cover positions (north and south standing stones) displace animated golem movement by one full round each in Scene 5.

Seven standing stones ring a granite altar. Each stone is twice your height, carved with spiraling runes that don't hold still — they shift as if the stone itself is thinking.

The air is different here. Heavier. Older. The spear pulses in long, slow throbs now, like a heart slowing toward sleep.

Thusnelda kneels at the altar. She doesn't speak immediately. Then: "Leave nothing at the door. What you carry in, you carry to the altar. All of it. The weapon, the doubts, and whatever part of you this journey has taken."

The Four Ritual Requirements

  1. Spear on the altar, unwrapped — bare iron on stone
  2. Blood from a warrior: voluntary, 1d6 damage, cannot be healed until after the ritual
  3. Group speaks in unison: "We unmake what was made in darkness." (Latin or Germanic — both work)
  4. A sacrifice — willingly given, of genuine value
OfferWhat it costsGrove's response
A magic itemLoss of powerAcceptable

A year of lifeLifespan shortenedAcceptable
Cassia volunteers herselfHer death, willinglyMore than acceptable; Thusnelda tries to stop her
Something cheapNothing realAltar goes cold. The grove knows.

If the sacrifice is insufficient, Thusnelda says: "That is not a sacrifice. That is a fee. The grove is not a toll booth."

DM: The influenced NPC's crisis
As the ritual begins, the connection intensifies. If detected and supported: group Wisdom check DC 14 to help them resist. Success: they participate, corruption cleared. Failure: they physically reach for the spear. Stop them with Athletics grapple DC 12 or by calling their name three times — if stopped, they collapse, weeping, with no memory of the last hour.

If undetected: at the ritual's climax they attempt to take the spear and run.

Scene 5: Mars Intervenes

"MORTALS."

The word is not a sound. It moves through you like a wave — like a bell struck inside your chest.

"YOU DARE UNMAKE WHAT A GOD FORGED."

The standing stones crack. Not shattering — cracking, like old bones, like the grove itself bracing for something.

Thusnelda doesn't move. Her voice is steady: "Finish the ritual. Do not look up. Whatever you see, do not look up."

Option A: Defensive Hold

Mars manifests through the standing stones: 3 animated Stone Golems (120 HP each — avatars, not killable at Level 5). Each party member actively fighting a golem keeps it occupied for 2 rounds. Golems fade when the ritual completes. Cover positions (spotted with Survival DC 11 before the ritual) displace each golem one full round.

Option B: Skill Challenge (4 successes before 3 failures)

Ritual Skill Challenge
4 successes before 3 failures — one roll per round, one person holds the ritual while others defend

Religion DC 14 — Speaking the words of unmaking with genuine conviction

Arcana DC 15 — Channeling the grove's ancient power, not fighting it

Athletics DC 12 — Holding the spear steady as it fights back

Insight DC 14 — Resisting the spear's final promise: "They'll abandon you. Take me and they won't have to."

Stealth DC 13 — Defense, not ritual: reposition behind a standing stone; golem wastes one movement; ritual holder has advantage this round

Each failure: one additional animated stone joins.

Option C: Bargain (before ritual begins)

Persuasion DC 17 or Religion DC 16 — address Mars directly, name what the spear did in his name:

"Great Mars, the spear was being used to corrupt and destroy men in your name. We destroy it not to defy you, but because what it was doing dishonored what you stand for."

A long silence. Then: "...CONTINUE. I WILL NOT MAKE IT EASY. BUT I WILL NOT STOP IT."

Grants advantage on all Skill Challenge rolls.

Conclusion

The iron shatters — not with a bang, but with a sound like an exhalation. Like something finally resting.

The runes on the standing stones go dark. The air clears. Somewhere far away, in a direction that isn't quite north, you hear a sound that might be a god, furious, far off.

The influenced NPC's eyes clear. They look at their hands. They say nothing for a long time.

Thusnelda sweeps iron fragments into a clay pot and seals it with beeswax. "I will bury these in seven places. It will take time. Go before he finds his voice again."

Any corruption below level 5 is reduced by 1. Level 5 characters remain affected.

Advance to Level 6.

If the ritual fails: The spear remains intact. The party must flee into the forest with a god's attention and a cursed weapon they came to destroy. Session 4 begins in a significantly worse position. Corruption is not cleared.

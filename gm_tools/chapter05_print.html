The Final Dungeon and the Three Horrible Options
The crystal-star focus is failing. The party goes down, past a dead king who has been fighting since 52 BC, to a granite altar where a starving god waits. No option saves everyone. That is the point.

Scene 0: The Crystal Is Failing

The crystal-star's light does not fade when the demon guarding it falls. It gets worse. Cracks spread across its faces like frost across a window, and each one lets out a note too low to be a sound, felt in the teeth more than heard. The floor of the Core Room is warm. Somewhere below, something that has been sleeping under all this weight is starting to notice you.

Tribune Lucius says it before anyone else does. "It's not stopping. Whatever this feeds, it just fed it more than it wanted."

The crystal cannot be destroyed here (established in Chapter 4). Assume the party goes down.

  • Arcana DC 13 (passive): The cracking pattern matches the shape of divine attention, not structural failure. Something is looking back through it.
  • Survival DC 12 (active, gauging descent time): The warmth in the floor increases at a readable rate. Answer honestly: 3-4 scenes remain before it matters mechanically.

Scene 1: Sunken Armory / Choking Hall

The passage narrows fast. Old weapon racks line a room that has not seen a living hand in longer than the fort has stood. Half the blades have rusted to lace. The other half are untouched, as if the room itself decided which ones still mattered. Beyond it, a hall barely three men wide, walls scored with symbols the torchlight will not sit still on.
  • Investigation DC 13 (active, examining the untouched weapons): Ram's-eye stamp — Mars's own mark. One can be taken; counts as Belatucadrus-grade consecrated for this session only if no other was brought. Using a god's own gift against him is a detail Mars notices in Scene 4.
  • Constitution DC 12 (active, pushing through the Choking Hall): Failure: 1d4 poison damage from the strain. A mood, not a puzzle.
Optional encounter (read the table, don't force it): 2 Shadows or 1 Wraith, reused from earlier sessions. Only if the party has burned few resources so far.

Scene 2: The Convergence Hall — Three Passages

The hall opens into three throats of stone, all leading the same direction by different roads. Something about the air says only one of these was meant to be walked.
PassageContents
Left (west)Pressure-plate rune, Perception DC 14 to spot. Trigger: 3d6 necrotic, 30-ft burst, DC 15 Dex half. Dead cultist's journal confirms the chieftains knew exactly what they served.
Center3 Zombies, slow and unsubtle. No trap. The honest road.
Right (east)1 Specter per party member minus one. No trap, hardest fight of the three.

No wrong answer, no answer that avoids a cost. Let the party choose based on what they can afford, not on which you think is "correct."

Scene 3: Vercingetorix's Last Stand

He stands where the three passages become one, and for the first time since the fort's gate, you see him clearly. The wounds from that night never closed. They never will. His armor is Gallic work two centuries out of fashion, rusted through in places where a living man would already be dead of infection ten times over. He is not breathing. He was never going to stop being here.

"Again," he says, in Latin thick with an accent that predates the Empire itself. "Always again. Tell me this time is the one that ends it."

Final Death
When a killing blow lands that Relentless does not prevent, he does not simply fall. He drops to one knee, focuses on something actually in front of him for the first time, and says "...Ended," like the word is unfamiliar. For 1 minute his spirit remains, lucid, and answers up to 3 questions truthfully before dissolving to ash: Alesia, Caesar's true corruption, and Mars's real hunger. This is the party's first confirmation of the whole story. Hand it over as a gift, not a quiz. He does not know about Corvinus, Brutus, or Pompey — only his own war.

Scene 4: The Antechamber of Mars

The passage opens into a circle wide enough to lose an army in. Twelve stones stand at its edge like an audience that arrived centuries early. At the center, a granite altar, chained to the floor by links thick as a man's arm, all of them scored and stretched as if something enormous has strained against them for a very long time. The air itself is red at the edges of your vision, and it is getting redder.

Rounds 1-2: presence only. Radiating cracks (DC 12 Dex or 1d6 necrotic crossing one). Legendary Actions available even without a body: Strike, Summon Fallen (Wraith/Wight, max 2 here).

The Session 1 Wight-general (branch on what actually happened): If spared in Session 1, his spirit rises once from a plinth: "I held this three hundred years so it would not have to end like this. I am glad I do not have to decide." Released the moment the spear's fate is settled. If destroyed in Session 1, this beat does not happen — do not manufacture a substitute.

Decision Point: The Three Horrible Options

The campaign's actual thesis. Present all three plainly. None is secretly correct.

Running the choice: Ask each player what their character actually wants. Let the disagreement between characters be part of the scene. If a corruption-5 PC exists, Mars addresses them directly, by name, before anyone else answers.

Epilogue

Whatever is left of the light drains out of the crystal-star behind you as you climb. By the time you reach the surface, the marsh is just a marsh again. Somewhere behind you, a granite altar is either empty, occupied by someone new, or holding a weapon that will wait for someone else's grandchildren.

Return to Fort Vindolanda. Corvinus, Cassia, and Varro greet the party, along with Lucius if he didn't stay behind as guardian. See chapter5.qmd for the full epilogue table (per-ending NPC states) and the citizenship resolution.

Consistency guard
Mars is genuinely Mars — diminished, corrupted, starved. Not an impostor. If a player asks "is this really him," any NPC suspicion (Cassia is the natural one) stays suspicion, never confirmation. Do not resolve into "it was someone else all along."

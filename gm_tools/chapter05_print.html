Scene 0: The Stair Down

The stair behind the dais is older than the temple above it. The steps are worn into shallow bowls by feet that came down and did not come back up. The red light ahead does not flicker like fire. It breathes. Each breath, the box in Rivia's arms answers with a hum you feel in your back teeth, the spear and the axe inside it singing to each other. Somewhere below, very far down, something enormous shifts its weight.
Arcana DC 13: the crystal's cracks are the shape of divine attention, not structural failure: something is looking back up the stair. Survival DC 12: the warmth in the stone rises at a readable rate; tell them honestly they have time, but not forever.

Scene 1: The Sunken Armory and the Choking Hall

The passage opens into a long room of weapon racks. Half the blades have rusted to lace. The other half are untouched, bright as the day they were forged, as if the room itself decided which ones still mattered. Beyond it, a hall barely three men wide, its walls scored with symbols the torchlight will not sit still on.
Investigation DC 13 (the bright blades): each carries a ram's-eye stamp, Mars's own mark. One may be taken: it counts as a consecrated weapon for this session. Mars notices, and comments on it in the Antechamber: "You came to unmake me with my own gift."

The Choking Hall: DC 12 Constitution to push through; failure costs 1d4 poison damage and the PC's next Perception check is at disadvantage. A mood, not a puzzle.

Optional: 2 × Wraith (the last of the chieftains' warband) guard the hall. Use only if the party arrives rested and the clock is kind.

Scene 3: Vercingetorix's Last Stand

He stands where the three passages become one. His armour is Gallic work two centuries out of fashion, rusted through in places where a living man would have died of the wound ten times over. He is not breathing. He has not breathed since Alesia. He raises his sword, not in threat, but the way a man raises a hand to greet someone he has waited for a very long time.

"Again," he says, in Latin thick with an accent older than the Empire. "Always again. Tell me this time is the one that ends it."

Dramatic Question: Can they give a two-hundred-year-old enemy the one thing he wants: an ending?

He is an enemy. He is bound to fight Rome and he will. He is also a victim, and the party already knows his story from Mars himself (Session 3). Play his death as confirmation and weight, not a first reveal. Champion's Duel: if a PC formally challenges him (Ursula is the natural one), he holds his skeletons back and fights one-on-one.

Final Death: the unmaking secret

He goes down on one knee, and this time he does not get up. The red light in his eyes goes out, and for the first time something looks out of them that is only a tired man. "Caesar would not listen either," he says. "Listen to me."

For one minute he is lucid. He answers up to three questions truthfully (Alesia, Caesar, what Mars wants). Then he gives the secret, unprompted:

"At the altar there are four winches. Caesar's engineers forged them, the same men who built the walls at Alesia. They were made to hold a god still. Lock all four rings and his armour opens. Then cut him from the spear: speak the binding backwards, the way the priests spoke it forwards. Then break it on the anvil. But hear me. Something has to hold the pieces, or they find each other again. Someone has to stay." He looks at each of you in turn. "I would have. They never let me choose."

He dissolves into ash and old iron. Whoever struck the final blow feels, for a moment, a hand on their shoulder.

Scene 4: The Threshold of Janus

The passage ends at an archway carved with a face on each side: one looking down into the red, one looking back up the way you came. As you pass beneath it, both faces open their eyes. The voice comes from both at once.

"Mars is neither good nor evil. He is war itself, and war is a door. Every door looks two ways at once. Choose how you cross."

Janus answers one question per PC, always as a pair of truths ("If you break it, the frontier is quiet and someone stays below. If you keep it, the frontier is loud and no one stays."). He never recommends. He never fights.

Scene 5: The Antechamber of Mars

The passage opens into a circle wide enough to lose an army in. Twelve stones stand at its edge like an audience that arrived centuries early. At the centre, a granite altar and, set into it, a black anvil. Four great iron winches stand at the compass points, their chains running to rings in the altar, every link scored and stretched as if something enormous has strained against them for a very long time.

He is sitting on the altar. Larger than a man, armoured in bronze that glows like a forge, a helmet crested in red, a bundle of pilum across his knees. He is thin. Even a god can be thin. He looks at the box, and smiles.
Mars reacts to the box: "You brought me an offering. You thought your little wooden cage would choke the flame? You fed it. Every heartbeat those two spent together drew my eyes straight to you, Rivia." To Ursula: "And you. You felt it, in the forest. You liked it."
The Session 1 Wight-general (only if spared): his spirit rises from a plinth. "I held this three hundred years so it would not have to end like this. I am glad I do not have to decide." He is released the moment the spear's fate is settled.
"MORTALS. You have carried my anchor through two ghosts and a king who would not die. I am not your enemy. I have never been anyone's enemy. I am what you do to each other. Now choose, and choose knowing I will remember which one you made."

The Choice: Three Horrible Options

Present all three plainly. None is secretly correct. Ask each player what their character actually wants, and let the disagreement between characters be the scene. If a corruption-5 PC exists, Mars speaks to them by name before anyone else answers.

Option 1: The Unmaking Ritual

Roll initiative. Mars fights. He targets whoever is working the ritual and never strikes a downed PC: he wants witnesses. The spear must be on the anvil: opening the box releases the greataxe too (Ursula: DC 14 Wis save or she spends her first turn in a battle-frenzy, attacking the nearest creature, friend or foe).

Round 1: Lock the Chains. Four winches, one at each compass point, 20 ft. from the altar. Each needs one action: DC 13 Athletics or thieves'/tinker's/smith's tools (Rivia's artificer tools count). Locks persist; unfinished winches can be finished next round. When all four are locked: Mars's AC drops to 16 and he loses all Legendary Resistance.
Round 2: Sever the Bloodline. A PC at the altar speaks the binding backwards: DC 14 Religion or Arcana (an action). Advantage if Vercingetorix's secret was heard; +2 if the speaker holds the amber OTHALAN token. Others may use the Help action. On success: Multiattack drops to two Pilum, Divine Smite is silenced. On failure, try again next round; Mars's War Cry recharges automatically.
Round 3: The Guardian, then the Shatter. First someone must step into the guardian's seat (the stone chair between the winches). Then any PC strikes the spear on the anvil: any hit with a magical or consecrated weapon, or DC 15 Strength with anything else. If no one takes the seat, the spear will not break: the ritual fails and the party must choose Option 2 or 3 with Mars still standing.
Who can take the seat: any PC (the character retires as a legend: they do not die, they stay, awake, forever, and their player narrates their last words). Thusnelda, if he is alive and the party brought him down or he followed: "I came to the grove to pay a debt with my death. Let me pay it with my life instead." Nobody else qualifies. Do not let a summoned creature, a stranger or a Roman legionary take it: the binding needs someone who chose to come.
The spear breaks with a sound like a city wall coming down. The red light goes out all at once. Mars does not scream. He stands up off the altar, and for a moment he is not thin any more, he is enormous, and he looks at each of you with something that might be respect. "Well," says the god of war. "That has never happened before." Then there is only the dark, and the chains, and someone sitting very still in the stone chair, keeping watch.

Option 2: Give It to Rome

The spear goes into Lucius's iron lockbox, sealed with his ring. Mars does not stop them, but he does not let them leave untouched: one round of fury. War Cry (DC 18 Wis, frightened 1 minute), then four Divine Pilum at four different PCs. Then he lets them go.

"Take it, then. Carry it to Rome. Rome has fed me better than any tribe ever did. I will be patient. Rome always gives me what I need, in the end." He is still smiling when the red light closes behind you.

The greataxe: separated from the spear, it goes quiet but not dead. Ursula keeps it. On a natural 1 in any future fight, it remembers.

Option 3: Give It Back to Mars

Lay the spear on the altar and step back. No combat. Mars takes it up, and each PC must make a DC 13 Wisdom save or gain 1 corruption: they felt how good it would be to follow him.

He weighs the spear in his hand like a man greeting an old friend. "No one bleeds today. You have my word, and I keep my word. Today." He walks past you up the stair, and the stone does not stop him. Long after he is gone you can still hear him, far above, laughing like thunder rolling north toward the Danube.

The greataxe: Mars hands it back to Ursula himself. "Keep it. You will need it." It is now a +1 greataxe that grants advantage on initiative and whispers.

Epilogue

By the time you climb out of the skull's mouth the marsh is just a marsh again. The water is black, not green. A dog is barking in the village. Somewhere behind you, a granite altar is either empty, guarded, or waiting.

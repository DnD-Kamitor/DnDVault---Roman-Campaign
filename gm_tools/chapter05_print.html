Chapter 5
The Wrath of Mars
A god descends upon Vindolanda
Level 7  ·  Final Session  ·  3–4 hours  ·  6 Players
Iris · Julia Jana · Tanit · Rivia · Ursula · Brian
The rain halts mid-fall. Every Germanic arrow freezes in the air. The parade ground becomes black sand.

Mars steps through the veil above the fort and demands judgment.

The fort is the stage. The god is the audience.
The players determine whether Rome earns another sunrise.

Scene 0: The Silence Before Judgment

The air tastes of brass. Wind grazes your face without moving your cloak. Every sound in the fort muffles except your heartbeat. In the center of the parade ground the sand funnels upward into a throne carved from weapons that have never touched rust. Upon it sits a figure the height of the fort wall, armored in plates that drink light. His eyes resemble cooling lava. He speaks your name without sound and the syllables shake loose dust from the barracks beams.

"MORTALS. I WATCHED. I ARRIVED. NOW YOU ANSWER."

The party appears at the center of the sand. Arena weapons hang along the perimeter, embedded in air. The witnesses stand frozen at the edges.

Opening Checks (close when Mars speaks)

CheckDCResult

Nature (passive)12Black sand has no natural origin. No heat, no mineral signature. It came with the god. Environmental manipulation will not work.
Arcana (passive)14Divine stasis, not a spell. Witnesses frozen because they are witnesses. Mars intends a record. Useful framing in Option C.
Insight (active)13Mars looks at one specific character first — the one who made the genuine grove sacrifice. Recognition, not warmth. Advantage on first Option C argument if they open with the grove sacrifice. Window closes when he addresses the party.

Scene 1: Mars Specifies the Trial

"You broke a covenant and mended another. You brought my spear home without my leave. Tell me why I should not salt this fort with your bones."

He offers three options. He will accept any. If the party hesitates more than one minute of table time: he selects Option B without comment.

OptionDescription
A — ChampionOne mortal faces divine champion Fausta Luperci in single combat.
B — Trial of BladesThe full party faces Mars himself until he is satisfied (victory at 150 HP).
C — Trial of VoicePersuade Mars through argument that destroying/entombing the spear honored him. DC 19.
Option C advantage conditions: genuine grove sacrifice, kneeling volunteers speak first (+advantage); Vercingetorix allied, rune message invoked (+3 bonus); Religion DC 13 + History DC 14 both made (+2 bonus). Lucius sabotaged or Corvinus unrebuked: disadvantage.

Scene 2A: Champion Trial

Mars gestures. A warrior forms from ash: armor older than Rome, scars that radiate light, eyes closed until she inhales.

"This is Fausta Luperci. She held the line at the Frozen Rhine when your Empire would not. She died alone. She serves today."

Flip Handout 11 face-up now.

Frozen Advance. Difficult terrain does not slow Fausta. When she moves within 5 ft of a creature, that creature must make DC 15 Constitution saving throw or have its speed reduced by 10 until end of its next turn.

Legendary Defiance (1/round). When reduced below 50 HP, she gains 20 temporary HP and automatically succeeds one saving throw.

After Frozen Advance triggers, her guard drops to her left side: the attacker in the next immediate action has +2 to hit from the left (DM narration, no mechanical bonus — frame it as opportunity).

Victory: Reduce to 0 HP or survive 10 rounds.

Arena weapon cards (Handout 11) — grab as bonus action: Gladius +2 to hit/damage, 2d6 radiant on hit, but 1d6 radiant self-damage each attack  ·  Pilum 2d8+4, restrain on hit, wielder cannot move that round  ·  Scutum +3 AC, last stand (1 HP instead of 0), disadvantage on attacks  ·  Veteran's Spear +2d6 if stationary, but must stay in place  ·  Seax ignore armor AC bonus, 1d4 self-damage on miss.

After victory: Fausta kneels, presses spear butt to sand, nods. Mars inclines his head a fraction. Sand drinks her form.

Scene 2B: Trial of Blades

Mars rises. The sky fractures into burning lines. Germanic warbands clutch their heads on the frozen walls.

Mars halts mid swing. He does not pant. He simply stops, looks at the weapon in his hand, looks at you, and nods as if to himself. "Enough." The weapon dissolves. He stands helmetless, and for a moment he resembles an exhausted general after campaign season.

Scene 2C: Trial of Voice

The party steps forward. Mars sits. Every heartbeat in the fort listens.

Three Valid Arguments

ArgumentGrounds
HonorThe spear dishonored him by sowing madness. Cite corrupted worker, Cassia's visions, Varro's men.
DisciplineDestroying/sealing it preserved the disciplined war Mars demands. Reference sacrifices, escort kneeling.
War vs. MurderThey chose war's harsh honor over easy cruelty. Examples: sparing Vercingetorix, saving civilians, capturing traitors instead of executing.

Arguments that anger him: "it was too dangerous" / "we had no choice" / "Rome needed this."

Mechanics

CheckDCResult
Persuasion or Performance19Core argument. Advantage if genuine grove sacrifice. +3 if Vercingetorix allied and rune invoked. +2 if Religion+History both made. Disadvantage if Lucius sabotaged or Corvinus unrebuked.
Insight (active)15Read Mars' posture shift between argument points — toward interest, away from dismissal. Identify which argument is landing before committing.
Deception (active)16Partial truth as full truth. Fail: "That is not what you believe. Say what you believe." DC +2. Success: must maintain the framing — changing it mid-argument cancels success.
History (passive, honor arg)14Paired with Religion DC 13 from opening: +2 to Persuasion roll. Structured argument Mars recognizes as formally correct.

Mars is silent long enough to hear the rain begin to fall again outside time. When he finally speaks, the single syllable feels like pausing the world. "...You are not wrong."
Critical success: Two PCs may accept the title of Chosen at Scene 3.

Scene 3: The Price of Standing

He steps off the throne. Up close he smells of hot iron and cedar smoke. Behind him the fort flickers like a painting held in uncertain hands. Every soldier on the walls stands frozen mid cheer or mid prayer. Mars' gaze passes over them and returns to you. "You have proven yourselves. I am not appeased, but I am satisfied."

The black sand recedes. Time resumes. Every cohort member collapses to their knees simultaneously as reality snaps back.

Call to War — for Chosen characters:
1–2 years: Dreams of burning watchtower → defend beleaguered cohort without pay. Mark sears on refusal.
3–4 years: Hearing distant trumpets in storms → lead civilians through hostile territory. Allies suffer nightmares on refusal.
5+ years: Wolves with ember eyes → avenge a dishonored legionary standard. Mars withdraws favor permanently on refusal.

Scene 4: Return to Vindolanda

Cassia touches the glowing mark on her arm and whispers, "He listened." Varro exhales for the first time in hours. The Germanic horns outside the ditch sound a single long note and then go silent. Vindolanda still stands.

Allow each player a short epilogue speech. Speeches to the cohort, promises to the vicus, treatment of prisoners.

Citizenship resolution: If Lucius dispatched in S4, a rider has been waiting at the gate since dawn. An imperial epistula arrives in the quiet after the trial — before the epilogue fully lands. Short document. Confirms new legal status, unit, Lucius' attestation. Final sentence, different hand: "Distinguished service on the Germanic frontier, confirmed by two independent sponsors. The Emperor's office takes note." — Do not narrate what the character feels. Ask the player.
Last Varro check: If the party asks him what he thought of the trial: Insight DC 12 shows he is holding something back. If pressed, one sentence — write it before the session based on the actual campaign: "I didn't expect you to [specific action]. That surprised me. I'm hard to surprise."
This is not a mechanical reward. It is the last character moment of the campaign.

Scene 5: Epilogue — One Year Later

Vindolanda breathes. Stories of the god who descended upon its parade ground travel faster than any courier. Some soldiers sign up just to see the scar in the sand. Some never speak of it at all. Mars called you interesting. History called you necessary.

Prompt each player: Where are you a year later? How often does the mark glow? What rumors follow you?

Commendationes Final Tally

  • Explicit mercy/restraint at mechanical cost during the trial: +1 (filed by Varro personally)
  • Expected range: 3–5 total. 6+ = exceptional service record for continuation play.
  • Fewer than 3: citizenship track closed for this campaign.

DM Notes: Corruption Level 5

Corruption Level 5 characters:
Option A: DC 16 Wis each round to act freely. Three successes reduce corruption to 4.
Option B: Mars targets them preferentially until called back by name three times (recreates Session 3 mechanic).
Option C: Mars uses them as mouthpiece — "What do you want?" Their honest answer modifies Persuasion DC (truthful self-awareness = no penalty; selfish answer = –5 penalty).
After session: Cannot shed corruption without performing a selfless deed unseen by any authority within one year.

DM Notes: Marius Coda (if subdued, not killed)

Marius appears at the arena edge among the witnesses. He does not fight. He watches. If the honor argument is made in Option C, he speaks one sentence unprompted: "It was the spear talking. Not me. I remember now." Mars hears this. It counts toward the argument's weight — treat it as +2 to the next Persuasion roll.

Visions in the Grove of Grannus

Session 6 · North Grove · the party sleeps beside the springs (incubation). Read each vision privately to its player: pass a note, or take them aside.
How it works. The old way to ask a healing god: sleep beside his spring and he answers in a dream. Short rest or long rest in the grove = one vision each. Rite of Rest done: the visions are clear and warm; each player may ask Grannus one question on waking and gets a plain one-sentence answer. Rite not done: the boy is in every dream, crying "I'm cold", and the visions come broken: read only the first paragraph of each. Spear forced into the water: no visions; Grannus is too weak. Everyone dreams of a red river instead.
The box: whoever sleeps nearest it wakes with DC 12 Wis or 1 exhaustion (the god and the box fight over that sleeper's dream).
Rule of thumb: never explain a vision. If a player puts it together, nod.

All of them: the first dream

read aloud to the table before the private visions
You sleep, and the grove is full of warm light. A tall man made of noon sits at the foot of the golden tree with his feet in the pool. He is very old and very thin, like a fire that has been fed on twigs for a hundred years. A woman with wet hair sits beside him and holds his hand. "Little soldiers," he says. "Five of you, and five things to see. I can only show. I cannot carry. Look closely: I do not have the strength to show you twice."
The sun walks round the sky above the grove, quickly: up in the east, high in the south, down in the west, home in the north. Four fires light under it, one by one, in that order. Then the light splits into five, and each of you falls into your own.
HintsSun-Path braziers: east, south, west, north (Session 7). Grannus and Sirona are real, starved, and on their side.

Iris

Druid · Shadar-kai · prays to Tanit · the shadow-curse, a family held by Rome
You stand in a ring of seven stones under a full moon, and your shadow will not stay with you. It walks to the centre stone without you and lies down on it. The stone drinks it like water. Every stone grows a little taller. Far below the ground you hear something swallow.
Then you are underground, in a hall where a pillar of black flesh grows up through the floor like a tree. Something curled inside it is moving, almost ready to be born. Its eyes open, and they are your eyes. A torch falls from your hand onto the pillar, and it screams, and burns, and the thing inside never wakes.
Last, the moon sets, and the goddess of your adopted family stands on the water of the pool. "What is taken comes back," Tanit says, "if you do not feed the thing that takes it." She opens her hand. In it are small figures, carved in bone. You count them. They are all there.
South GroveThe stones drink; blood (or a shadow) on them feeds what is below. Thusnelda's rite is wrong.
Gathering HallThe Yochlol grows in a pillar and hatches on round 4 unless the pillar burns. Fire wins it.
Her familyStill alive. Feeding Mars (more war, more killing) is what keeps people like them taken. A promise, not a guarantee.
Her one questionSuggested answer if she asks about the curse: "Your shadow is hungry because something below is hungry. Starve that, and yours grows quiet."

Julia Jana

Cleric · Aasimar · of the Janus Clan · senator's only child
You walk a stair that goes down forever, and at every landing there is a door, and every door has two faces carved on it, one looking up the stair and one looking down. The faces are your father's. "Which way?" they ask, both at once. You do not know, and you are ashamed not to know, and you keep walking down.
At the bottom is the last door. It is not carved. It is a god. He stands in the doorway with one face to you and one face to the dark behind him, and you understand that he has been standing there since before Rome had a name. "Ask me to open," Janus says, "and I open. But a door does not choose who walks through it. You do." Behind him, in the dark, something enormous breathes.
Then you see a throne of black iron with four iron arms, open and waiting, in a room lit red. The arms close on whoever sits. You see someone sit down. You cannot see their face. You only see that they chose it, and that the door behind them closes very gently, the way you would close a door on a sleeping child.
Threshold of JanusSession 7: Janus himself guards a threshold before Mars. She is the one he will speak to. The correct invocation matters; he asks a question, not for a fight.
The ChoiceDestroying the spear means someone takes the guardian's seat forever (the four iron arms). It must be chosen freely. A PC, or Thusnelda if he lives and comes.
Rule 1 of her faith"Closing the door gently" on the one who sits: her vow not to close a door on someone leaving is going to be tested here.
Her one questionSuggested answer: "The one who sits must want to. Ask, and listen to who answers."

Rivia

Artificer · Gnome · prays to Vulcan · the box is her work
You are in a forge that is also the inside of your box. The walls are your own wood, the OTHALAN rune burned into every plank. In the middle, the spear and the great axe lie side by side on an anvil, and they are talking to each other in a language that sounds like teeth. Every time they speak, a crack runs through the wood. You reach for your tools. Your hands are too slow. They are always too slow.
A huge figure made of smoke and claws leans over your shoulder, friendly as a merchant. "Open it," it says, reasonably. "Let me take the axe, and you keep the spear. Or I take the spear and you keep your friend's soul. Fair trade. Just open the lid." Its four arms are already reaching for the latch.
Then a smith with a lame leg and arms like oak roots stands beside you at the anvil. Vulcan does not look at you; he looks at the work. He shows you three things with his hammer: a winch turning, a chain pulling taut, and an iron lockbox with a dead tribune's seal on it. "Machines and locks," he says. "Your trade. Something that keeps, or something that breaks. Not something that bargains."
The GlabrezuIt will offer a bargain: open the box, leave the spear. Opening the box is the mistake. It lies about the terms.
The UnmakingSession 7: destroying it is a 3-round ritual with winches and chains. Her skills run it.
Option: RomeLucius's iron lockbox can seal the spear for Rome. One round of Mars's fury.
Her fearToo slow, someone dies. The vision is telling her this time the work is a machine, and she can be ready in advance.
Her one questionSuggested answer if she asks about the axe: "Two hungers in one box feed each other. Part them before the end, or the end comes faster."

Tanit

Bard · Halfling · from Carthago Nova · will not falsify an omen
You stand in a long low room lined with bones, sorted with terrible care: skulls with skulls, hands with hands. Three empty niches wait in the wall. A child's voice asks you a riddle you cannot quite hear, and you know the answer the way you know your own name: it is the home-rune, the one burned on the box. You carve it, and a wall slides open, and the bones sigh.
Then two crowned dead men kneel in front of you, chieftains with their throats still cut, and they want to talk. They have been waiting a long time for someone who would not lie about what they say. "Tell it true," one begs. "Tell them we were bought. Tell them what we sold." Behind them, four coloured lights burn on a door: one like the sky, one like spring grass, one like ripe barley, one like blood.
Last, you are home. Your mother's kitchen. The bread is in the oven and the smell fills the whole world. Your mother does not turn round from the fire. "Accurate," she says. "Always accurate. Even at the end, when everyone wants you to say something kinder." You wake, and you can still smell the bread.
OssuaryThree Niches puzzle: the answer is OTHALAN, the home-rune.
Chieftain WraithsTwo of them can confess instead of fight, if someone listens and promises to tell it true. A Bard's honesty is the key.
Rune LockThe order is blue, green, yellow, red (sky, grass, barley, blood).
The endingAt the Choice someone will want a softer reading. Her honour (accuracy) is the test.
Her one questionSuggested answer: "Say what you saw. Others will try to make it mean something easier."

Ursula

Barbarian · her great axe is locked in the box with the spear
You stand on a road made of bones over a lake of green fire. Two snakes made of spines rise on either side of you, and the air between them crackles blue. You see your friends walking behind you in a straight line, and you know, the way you know a punch is coming, that the line is the danger. You shout at them to spread out. In the dream, they listen.
Your axe is calling you from inside a box. It sounds like your own voice, only happier. "Let me out and we'll never lose again." You want to. You want to more than you have ever wanted anything. Then you look at your hands and they are red to the elbow, and none of the blood is an enemy's.
Last, a king in red stands in a flooded hall full of old armour. He has been killed a thousand times; you can see every wound, healed and opened and healed. He does not want to fight you. He wants to stop. "Your iron cannot finish me," he says. "Only a blade the sun has touched. Bring me one, warrior, and I will thank you for it." He bows to you, one fighter to another.
EntranceThe bone bridge: Bone Nagas in the bile, Lightning Bolt along the line. Spread out.
The axeTaking it back out of the box is a real temptation and a real danger (possession, corruption). The vision warns her what it would cost.
VercingetorixSession 7: he is Relentless and won't stay down unless struck with a sun-blessed weapon. The shrine prayer here (Grannus's blessing) gives one. He may welcome death.
Her one questionSuggested answer: "The bravest blow you strike will be the one you do not."

Asked at the table: Rivia

"How do I break the cycle of war without forging another way to destruction?" · answered by Vulcan
(He does not look up from the anvil; the hammer keeps its rhythm.) "You cannot. Not with a hammer. Every blade I ever made, someone carried to a war. I made his armour too, Mars's, and he thanked me by taking my wife to bed.
So do you know what I made him then? Not a sword. A net. Fine chain, so fine he never saw it, and I caught him in it and held him up in front of all the gods until he was ashamed. I did not kill him. I could not. I held him.
War does not die, little smith. It is bound, or it is fed. A smith's work is to build the thing that holds: the chain, the lock, the seat. Then someone has to be brave enough to stay and keep holding it.
Make tools that mend. Make chains that hold. And when they tell you the answer is one more weapon, put down your hammer and walk away. That is the hardest thing a smith ever does."
One-line version"Do not forge, bind: build the chain that holds him, and know that someone must stay to hold it."
Points toVulcan's net (real myth: he caught Mars with Venus) = the Unmaking's winches and chains and the guardian's seat. "Bound or fed" = Destroy vs give it to Mars. "Tools that mend" = her quiet repairs, her pacifist upbringing.

Asked at the table: Tanit

"Who bought what the chieftains sold?" · answered by Grannus (she prays to no god, so the grove answers)
The pool goes still, and in it, like a reflection, a Roman hand counts silver onto a chieftain's palm. The hand wears a gold ring. The sleeve above it carries a broad stripe of purple, the deep sea-purple your family boils out of shells in Carthago Nova. You would know that colour anywhere. You have worn it on your belt all your life.
"A man in Rome paid the silver," Grannus says. "He never came here. He sent other hands, and those hands sent others. He wanted the spear kept from Rome's own masters, and he thought the chieftains were his tools." The reflection darkens, and below the silver, below the chieftains, something under the skull opens its mouth. "But he only paid. The one who took what they sold, their sons and their oaths and this village, was the hungry one below. The man in Rome bought a war. The war bought him."
One-line version"A senator in Rome paid the silver; the war-god under the skull took what was sold."
What it revealsSenator Brutus (Never Present Villain) bribed the chieftains through agents, to keep the spear from Rome and the Senate. Do not say his name: the broad purple stripe (latus clavus) marks a senator, and her family's own dye is on him. Let her connect it.
TiesHer honour is accuracy: the Wraith chieftains (Gathering Hall) can confess this and give the name, if she promises to tell it true. Julia Jana's father is a senator close to the emperor: this is evidence she could carry home.

On waking: all of them

You wake at first light, all at the same moment, with dew on your faces. The pool is warm. On the stone beside it, where none of you put them, lie five small things: a feather, a key, a nail, a crust of bread, and a single ring of iron chain.
The giftsFeather (Iris), key (Julia Jana: thresholds), nail (Rivia: the machine), bread (Tanit: home), chain link (Ursula: the seat's iron arms, and her own restraint). Each can be held once to reroll one saving throw against Mars or the spear in Session 7.
RestLong rest taken here also clears 1 exhaustion from the Myling carrier (if the Rite was done, he is no longer carried).

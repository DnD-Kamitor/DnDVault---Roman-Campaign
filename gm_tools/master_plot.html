Five-Session Arc — Story in Order

1
Blood and Omens
The party descends into a vault beneath Fort Vindolanda and emerges holding a god's weapon.
Personal Stakes
THE MOMENT: The Wight speaks. It knows the spear's name and the party's. It offers them a choice before it dies.
Key choice: Who touches the spear? That character becomes the corruption anchor for the campaign.
Session ends: Corvinus is waiting at the mine entrance in the dark. "Inside. Now. All of you."
Seeds planted: The OTHALAN contract token. The Wight's knowledge. Cassia's warning before descent.
2
The Tribune's Gambit
A murder, a Senate envoy, a raid, and an accusation — all before nightfall.
Fort-Level Stakes
THE MOMENT: Tribune accuses the party of treason. Varro takes one step to stand beside them. His hand moves to his gladius.
Key choice: Expose / Negotiate / Intimidate / Fight / Flee with Vercingetorix / Surrender.
Session ends: One of five possible Session 3 openings depending on the choice.
Seeds planted: Quintus Flavius (sleeper agent). Vercingetorix's trust level. The bog valerian shortage.
3
Through the Dark Forest
The spear is influencing one of them. The forest is watching. Thusnelda knows everything and will guide them — once.
Regional Stakes
THE MOMENT: The influenced party member tries to stop the ritual at its climax. The party must reach them before the spear does.
Key choice: What is sacrificed at the grove altar? The sacrifice must be real. The grove knows.
Session ends: The spear is destroyed or the ritual fails. Either way, Mars noticed.
Seeds planted: What was sacrificed. Whether Corvinus's pursuit caught the party's trail. Vercingetorix's dying condition revealed (full trust only).
4
The God's Demand
The fort is under siege. Mars wants three figures to kneel. A Senator's agent is burning everything that might stop it.
Imperial Stakes
THE MOMENT: In the antechamber, the spear levitates above the altar. Mars' voice (one word, no body): "KNEEL."
Key choice: Who kneels? One volunteer / two / three correct figures (Roman, Germanic, spear-touched). The number determines Session 5's opening parameters.
Session ends: Siege pauses. Germanic horns go silent. The escort descends. The principal question of the campaign is now with the god.
Seeds planted: Which three knelt. Whether Corvinus was honored or sidelined. Lucius's dispatch to Rome — sent or not.
5
The Wrath of Mars
Mars arrives. The rain stops mid-drop. The fort becomes an arena. He has been watching since Session 1. He will not say he is impressed.
Divine Stakes
THE MOMENT: Mars looks at one character first — the one who made the genuine grove sacrifice. Not warmth. Recognition. He has known since it happened.
Key choice: Option A (Champion Trial) / Option B (Trial of Blades, default) / Option C (Trial of Voice — argue with a god).
Session ends: The Mark of Mars. The Debt. The Prophecy. Vindolanda becomes a pilgrimage site. Epilogue: one year later.

Villain Arc — Three Types (Sclanders)

Blunt Force Nemesis
Legate Corvinus → Tribune Lucius

Direct opposition. Takes command of the situation, controls physical access, can arrest the party. Changes hands: Corvinus (S1-2) hands off to Lucius (S2-3) as the primary blunt obstacle. By S4, both have been navigated rather than defeated.

Corvinus arc across sessions

S1: Waiting at the mine. Furious. Compliant with orders he hates.

S2: Loses command to Lucius. Seething privately, cooperating publicly.

S3: Pursues with 8 legionaries. His scouts may already be ahead.

S4: Has filed a report blaming the party for the Session 2 events. Fears exposure. Either arrested or sidelined by end of session.

S5: Frozen witness. His soldiers may arrive hostile mid-arena if Mars is displeased with what happened in S4.

Lucius arc across sessions

S2: Arrives confident, performing authority. The hesitation moment exists — one check catches it.

S3: Travels with party (if negotiated) or is absent. Either way Brutus's ravens still reach him.

S4: Still receives orders to kill Cassia. Citizenship window (Session 4): can file an attestation if party approaches. Offer not made twice.

S5: Frozen witness. His future is in Mars's judgment. If he sabotaged the spear: all DCs +2 this session.

Never Present Villain
Senator Gaius Cassius Brutus

Manipulation from shadows, S1–S4. His agents, ciphered orders, and raven network create all the political pressure. Physical appearance only in the epilogue (if the party's story forced accountability into Rome). The name "G.C.B." is decoded in Session 2. His face is never shown until it is too late for him to be the primary threat.

Brutus's network across sessions

S1: OTHALAN contract token in the vault — placed before the party descended. He knew the spear was there.

S2: Quintus Flavius (sleeper agent, killed Sextus). Scholar Aldric (raven spy). Encoded letter ordering "permanent removal."

S3: Aldric's final report if not confronted. Corvinus's pursuit may have Brutus's backing.

S4: Three simultaneous attacks: gate breach, granary fire, shrine desecration. Plan B already arranged for Session 5 if Session 4 fails. Agent inside the fort named in interrogation (surfaces in epilogue).

S5: Whatever happened to his network determines his exposure in the epilogue.

Divine Antagonist
Mars

Ultimate challenge beyond mortal scale. Present since Session 1 as a watching presence, explicitly arrives Session 5. His attention is not malevolent — it is demanding. He does not want to destroy the party. He wants to see if they're worth keeping.

Mars across sessions

S1: The vault is his. The seal was his. He felt it when the spear moved. He has been watching since the first torch was lit below.

S2: Cassia's visions intensify since the spear surfaced. She cannot sleep. She does not tell the party how bad it has gotten.

S3: The ritual at the sacred grove is an attempt to use his domain without his leave. He will stop it — but the grove passes a message through the ritual: "The grove belongs to me. Bring me what remains." He will not make it easy. But he will not stop it.

S4: "KNEEL." One word. No body. The antechamber knows what it means. Three figures must kneel in submission for him to receive what is brought to him. He is watching who chooses to kneel and who has to be convinced.

S5: He arrives physically. "MORTALS. I WATCHED. I ARRIVED. NOW YOU ANSWER." He is conducting judgment in the Armilustrium form — the ancient festival of weapons purification. That is a choice. He chose a shape Rome can understand for a reason.

Dream sequence — how to run it (S2 before Scene 3)

Before Session 3 begins, the player with the highest corruption level describes a dream to the table. No dice. No saves. Just narration.

Fort burning. Comrades dead. Themselves standing over the ruins, holding the spear. The spear is not burning. Everything else is.

Don't make it mechanical. Don't frame it as a warning. Make it felt. Give the player the first line and let them run with it. The table listening to one player describe their own corruption is more powerful than any mechanic.

The Truth Behind the Campaign

Full DM context — what this campaign is actually about

The Spear's Real History

The Spear of Mars (the God-Killer) was used three generations ago by a Germanic hero who wounded one of Mars's demigod sons. Mars cursed it: anyone who wields it with ambition will be consumed by war. The Romans sealed it in the vault beneath the future site of Vindolanda, believing they were containing a threat. They were also hiding evidence of a military failure they didn't want in the record.

Why Brutus Wants It Destroyed

Brutus's intelligence network identified the spear as the Emperor's potential tool — a divine weapon that could legitimise military action against the Senate's frontier contracts. Brutus is not a cartoon villain. He believes a divine weapon in the Emperor's hands ends the Senate's structural check on military power. He is correct about the danger. He is wrong about who should be making this decision. His methods — sleeper agents, sabotage, murder — are the methods of a man who stopped trusting institutions and started substituting his own judgment for theirs.

What Mars Actually Wants

Mars does not want the spear destroyed. He wants the party to earn the right to destroy it in front of him. The difference is everything. If it were destroyed secretly, without his acknowledgment, he would have noticed — he has been watching since Session 1 — and his response would not have been a trial. His response would have been the frontier burning.

The trial in Session 5 is Mars conducting the Armilustrium — the ancient Roman festival of weapons purification and martial review. He chose a shape Rome understands. He chose judgment, not punishment. That is the closest thing to mercy that a god of war can offer, and the party should feel the weight of that by the time they understand it.

Vercingetorix

His grandfather helped seal the spear. The tribe has guarded that sealing for three generations as a sacred obligation — not because they fear the spear, but because they understood the curse before the Romans did. His dying condition is not tragedy for its own sake: it is the cost the tribe has paid for maintaining that vigil while Rome built a fort on top of it and called it infrastructure.

Cassia

She has known the outcome of Session 5 since her third vision after the spear surfaced. Her visions stopped at the moment of Mars's arrival. She will not say what she saw in the silence where a fourth vision should have been. She knows that an augur who reveals a fated outcome before it occurs collapses the possibility space. She is choosing not to speak because the party's genuine choices are more important than the outcome being predicted correctly.

What questions players are most likely to ask — and honest DM answers
QuestionHonest answer (give this)
"Is the spear actually sentient?"No. But it resonates with the ambitions of whoever holds it, and amplifies them. The influenced NPC is hearing their own desires at higher volume, not a separate voice.
"Is Mars evil?"No. He is the god of war as Rome understood war — discipline, sacrifice, martial virtue, the right kind of violence at the right kind of moment. He is not cruel. He is demanding. These are not the same thing.
"Could we have just left the spear in the vault?"No. Once found and touched, it began the process. Once Corvinus moved it, Rome knew. Once the Tribune arrived, the political situation was already in motion. The characters were never going to be able to put it back and walk away. That is the opening hook working correctly.
"Does Brutus ever face consequences?"That depends entirely on what the party documented, who they told, and whether they had the political leverage to make it stick. The epilogue can include Brutus facing accountability, exile, or nothing. The campaign does not guarantee justice. It guarantees the party's actions mattered.
"What happens to Vercingetorix?"He dies. Within the year. His tribe remembers the party in a specific way depending on how they treated him. If they reached Complete Trust, there is a specific grave marker the party can visit in the epilogue.

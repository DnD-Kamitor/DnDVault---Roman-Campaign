# Three-Ring Rune Lock — Riddle (unplaced scene)

A stone door with three concentric rings, each rotating independently. All three
must be turned to their correct answer and held in alignment at once before the
door releases — turning one ring can knock another out of position.

**Answers:** Outer ring = MANNAZ. Middle ring = OTHALA. Inner ring = two crossed
axes (a carved pictograph, not a rune).

Not yet assigned to a session/chapter or wired into any build.

---

## Read-aloud

> Three rings turn on the one stone door. Answer them all, or answer none.
>
> "Count me and you will always be wrong. I am never one, and I am never many,
> only the shape between them. Burn a village to nothing and I stand in the ash
> before the first stone is relaid. What is left when nothing is left?"
>
> "I was old before your grandfather's grandfather drew breath, and I will be
> young again after your name is forgotten. No man has ever bought me. No man
> has ever truly sold me, though fools have tried. I do not remember who first
> stood on me. I remember everyone since."
>
> "I have no sound. Say me aloud and you have said nothing at all. I am not
> given. I am not earned. I am only ever taken up, and by the time you
> understand why, it is already in your hands."

## Answer key

**Outer ring — MANNAZ.** The first line describes the self as a category, not a
headcount: indivisible into one or many, and what a razed community still *is*
even with no survivors left to count.

**Middle ring — OTHALA.** Ancestral land: outlives every owner, was never
truly a possession, and carries the memory of everyone who held it, not just
the current one.

**Inner ring — two crossed axes.** A pictograph, not a rune, so it "has no
sound" when spoken aloud. It is lifted into a hand, not given or earned.

No vocabulary overlap with the rune names or their canonical meanings in
`germanic_tribes.qmd` (Tyr's Aett: Mannaz = "mankind, community, the self";
Othala = "ancestral land, inheritance, belonging"), so a player can't
pattern-match straight from the book table — the concept has to be reasoned
out from the riddle itself.

## Hints (graduated, per ring)

**Outer ring (MANNAZ):**
- *Free, on close inspection:* The carving isn't one figure, it's dozens of tiny
  identical silhouettes, worn so shallow you can't tell where one ends and the
  next begins.
- *DC 12 Intelligence (Religion):* This border pattern matches the boundary-marker
  figures used on Germanic village threshold stones, the ones that name a
  settlement as a single body, not a headcount.
- *Stuck 5+ minutes:* An NPC present (Thusnelda, if she's in the scene) says:
  "It does not ask what you are alone. It asks what you are when no one is
  counting."

**Middle ring (OTHALA):**
- *Free, on close inspection:* One spot on this ring is worn glass-smooth, oils
  and handprints layered so deep the carving underneath is nearly gone, while the
  rest of the ring is sharp and crisp.
- *DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (History):* That wear pattern isn't
  from one hand. It's centuries of hands, all touching the same spot for the same
  reason: something inherited, never bought, never truly sold.
- *Stuck fallback:* "Everyone who ever stood here touched that same place. Not
  because they were told to. Because it was already theirs before they arrived."

**Inner ring (two crossed axes):**
- *Free, on close inspection:* This mark isn't cut into the stone like the
  others, it's set into it, a separate darker material, like something dropped in
  rather than carved.
- *DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation), or a character proficient with axes/smith's
  tools:* The inlay is worked metal, not stone, shaped like a real weapon a hand
  would actually grip, not a written symbol at all.
- *Stuck fallback:* "It has no sound because it is not a word. Say it, and you
  have said nothing. Pick it up, and you already know."

**Overall stuck fallback (all three, if the table stalls past ~10 minutes total):**
the door itself groans, a ring shifts a fraction on its own, and whichever NPC is
present says plainly: "The stone is not trying to keep you out. It is trying to
make you slow down before what's past it." Nudge without solving it outright.

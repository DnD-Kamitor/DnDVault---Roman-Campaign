# GM Upgrade Plan — The Shadow of Mars

*Simulated across all five sessions. Written from DM's chair.*

---

## Executive Summary: What Needs Fixing

After running each session forward in simulation, the following categories of problem surface.

**Critical holes (will break the session at the table):**
1. Session 1/2 Tribune arrival timing is contradictory — he arrives in S1 mid-briefing but S2 opens "the morning before the Tribune arrives"
2. Session 3 → Session 4 geographic transition is never explained — the party is in the forest at S3's end and besieged at the fort at S4's start
3. The grove sacrifice (Session 3's climax) has no concrete success criteria — "something real" is not enough for the DM to call the ritual's result
4. Session 5 Option C (Trial of Voice) is the most dramatically important path and its three arguments are not specified

**Story threads planted but never resolved:**
- "The son of Mars fell here" (vault inscription, Session 1) — who was this son, and why does it matter that Cassia is his SISTER?
- Brutus's Plan B — mentioned four times, never described
- The nine shields from the tribe that "came once and did not return" — who were they?
- Decius Turbo's handler in Carnuntum — what did Rome receive? When does it respond?
- Titus Sempronius Caecilius's papers — Cassia inherited them but not his knowledge; the party can find these and reconstruct the complete vault history

**Balance concerns:**
- Session 1: Four Shadows in the Flooded Gallery do not clearly telegraph the "extinguish lights" bypass, making a Deadly encounter near-inevitable for parties who don't read the passive clues
- Session 1: The Wight in the Altar Chamber must have its exit condition communicated BEFORE initiative is called, or the party fights to the death not knowing they can negotiate
- Session 5: Option B (fight Mars) has no visible "until satisfied" stop condition in the current chapter text

**Puzzles that are real and work (leave them alone):**
- Runic Corridor: OTHALAN always safe, three independent routes to find this, consequences for failing are survivable. ✓
- Binding Chamber chains: oxidation-order sequence, DC 14 Investigation to find the pattern. ✓
- Caesar cipher on Handout 5: functional but needs a physical key card

**Missing hidden information layers:**
- Each major NPC should have one secret the party can find that is not in the OGAS table
- Brutus's divine pacts (Jupiter, Pluto, Bellona) have no observable consequences in play
- The cascade unlock table doesn't specify what happens when a key check is MISSED for three consecutive sessions

## Session 1: Blood and Omens

### Simulation run

The session works well through the principia briefing. The vault branches feel distinct and the puzzles are functional. The following points break during simulation.

**HOLE 1 — The Tribune's arrival moment vs. Session 2's premise**

Current state: In Session 1, Tribune Lucius arrives mid-briefing. In Session 2's cold open, it is "the morning before the Tribune arrives" with Sextus dead.

These cannot both be true. Quintus Flavius (the murderer) killed Sextus that specific morning because it was his last opportunity before the Tribune's departure, but the Tribune has to be AT THE FORT for Quintus to be departing with him. If the Tribune is already inside the fort during Session 1's briefing, then Quintus would have killed Sextus the NIGHT BEFORE the briefing — which means Session 2's cold open is not "before the Tribune arrives" but "the morning of the day the Tribune arrived last night."

**Fix:** Restructure the timing as follows:

- Session 1 ends with the Tribune spotted approaching on the road (three long trumpet notes, riders visible through the south gate). The Legate dismisses the party. Session 1 ends.
- Session 2 cold open is that same morning: dawn. Sextus found dead. The party investigates in the 30-minute window before the Tribune formally enters the gate. The trumpet call that closes the cold open IS his official arrival.

This gives the party exactly one scene of investigation before the Tribune's presence reshuffles everything — which is the pressure cooker design intent. It also makes Quintus Flavius's timing coherent: he killed Sextus during the night before the Tribune's official arrival because he knew the Tribune would want to move quickly.

Mechanically: The party can run DC 12 Medicine and DC 13 Investigation on Sextus's body in that window. If they hit DC 13 Investigation and DC 12 Survival (tracking the killer), they get Clue 1 of the Sextus murder before the Tribune displaces their attention. That's the design intent.

**HOLE 2 — The Altar Chamber Wight: exit condition vs. combat default**

Current state: Milestone 76 says "Wight speaks, exit condition exists, Persuasion DC 14 with contract scroll advantage." The chapter text does not describe what the Wight says or when the exit condition is communicated.

A party that enters the Altar Chamber and finds a Wight will, by default, initiate combat. The Wight is CR 3. For a Level 3 party that has already fought Shadows and Ghouls, this fight could kill characters. If the party only discovers the exit condition (Persuasion DC 14) after three rounds of combat and two unconscious PCs, the mechanic feels like a gotcha.

**Fix:** The Wight must SPEAK before initiative is called. Add the following to the Altar Chamber section:

> *The Wight stands behind the altar with both hands flat on the stone, not gripping for combat. When the party's torchlight reaches it, it does not move. Its voice is dry stone against dry stone: "You have the contract token. Or you have the contract scroll. One of these things is required to speak here. Present it, or prove yourself by other means."*

The phrase "prove yourself by other means" is the combat invitation. It is not the only option. A party who presents the OTHALAN bone token (from either the hidden alcove or the preserved warrior's throat) or the contract scroll from the Binding Chamber can open the negotiation path immediately.

Persuasion DC 14 (backed by the contract token: advantage; backed by the contract scroll: advantage; backed by both: auto-success at DC 14 level).

What the Wight says on a successful Persuasion check: it is the spirit of Tiberius Claudius Maximus, the Roman officer who negotiated the original covenant with the Germanic tribes 300 years ago. He sealed himself here as the Roman party to the contract — the same way the Germanic warrior in the flooded gallery sealed himself as the Germanic party. He knows the full terms of the covenant. He will tell the party everything:

- The excavation voided the covenant
- The spear is Mars's weapon, not a tribal artifact
- The spear was used to kill Mars's son (name: Atavitus — see Hidden Information section below)
- Returning the spear to Mars or destroying it in the sacred grove are both acceptable endings
- Claiming it for mortal use will end badly because the spear remembers what it was made for

If the party fights the Wight: he fights without the two Shadows (they are in the altar room, not allied with the Wight — he dislikes them and they will not cooperate with each other). This makes the fight winnable but still difficult. On death, the Wight exhales one sentence in Latin: *"The contract is still valid. Find the grove."* Whether this lands or not depends on whether anyone speaks Latin and is listening during a fight.

**HOLE 3 — Shadow bypass needs clearer telegraphing**

Current state: The four Shadows in the Flooded Gallery are described as "not aggressive by default: they are drawn to light and sound." The bypass (extinguish torches, move quietly, DC 14 Stealth group check) is in the body text but is easy to miss if a player reads fast and keys on "four Shadows."

**Fix:** Add an explicit beat before the party steps into the water. When the first character's foot touches the flooded section, the torchlight projects their silhouette onto the far wall. The Shadows, 30 feet away, do not move — but one of them turns toward the light.

> *The Shadows don't move. They turn. Two faces without features, orienting on the torchlight the way plants orient toward a window. They have not come forward. They have noticed you.*

This gives the party a clear, observable moment to reconsider their approach before initiative. A player who says "I put out the torch" has made the correct decision. The Stealth DC 14 group check follows. The four Shadows do not pursue a party moving in darkness because there is nothing to pursue.

## Session 2: The Chieftain's Price / Tribune's Gambit

### Simulation run

**HOLE 4 — Session structure confusion: fort raid vs. road journey**

The session overview says "Germanic raiders attack in force — with suspicious timing" as a key event, but the chapter content then describes a road journey to Vercingetorix's territory with a day's worth of travel encounters.

As written, these cannot both happen in one 3-4 hour session at appropriate pacing. The session needs a clear structural choice:

Option A (preferred): The raid is the end of Session 2, not the middle. The session runs: cold open (Sextus body + Tribune arrival) → Tribune's authority established → party investigation of Sextus murder → road journey toward Vercingetorix → raid occurs as an ambush en route. This gives the journey encounters their full 10-15 minutes each and still lands the raid as the session's combat climax before the Vercingetorix meeting happens in Session 3.

Option B: Keep both in Session 2, but cut the road encounter table from d8 to d4, and run the raid as a fort defense SET PIECE that interrupts the investigation window rather than as a travel event. This works but makes Session 2 feel rushed.

**Fix:** Restructure Session 2 as follows:
- Scenes 1-3: fort (Tribune arrival, investigation window for Sextus murder, Tribune asserting authority over the spear)
- Scene 4: departure from fort — Tribune's sealed orders give the party a mission to escort the spear north under his authority; they begin the road journey
- Scene 5: road ambush (the "Germanic raid" is actually an ambush on the road, not a fort assault — this makes more military sense since Vercingetorix would intercept a moving target, not assault a fortified position with 80 warriors)
- Session ends with the party at the river, Vercingetorix visible on the far bank

The fort raid as described (80 Germanic warriors, suspicious timing) is more appropriate as the Session 3 siege that forces the party back to Vindolanda after their forest mission. It has been set up as Session 3's precipitating event.

**HOLE 5 — Sextus murder timeline: when can the party solve it?**

Quintus Flavius leaves with the Tribune's entourage. If the party doesn't solve the murder before the Tribune departs, the killer is gone. The chapter acknowledges this but doesn't specify when the Tribune departs (same day? next morning?).

**Fix:** Establish that the Tribune plans to depart at midday (he's in a hurry; his orders require the spear to move north by a specific date). The party has the morning window. Three clue routes, all accessible before midday:

- Clue 1 (body): DC 13 Investigation of Sextus's body in Scene 1 identifies the weapon type (a pugio, military issue) and the attacker's height and weight from wound angle and depth. Description matches someone shorter than average.
- Clue 2 (storage chains): Sextus interrupted Quintus tampering with the spear's storage chains the previous night. Investigation DC 12 of the spear's storage area finds fresh tool marks on the chain locks, different from the party's original handling. Quintus tried to damage the chains so the spear could be "accidentally" dropped in transit.
- Clue 3 (the entourage): The Tribune's escort includes a "local liaison" named Quintus Flavius who was not with the escort yesterday but appeared this morning with a travel document that looks slightly too fresh. DC 14 Perception (comparing the document's paper wear to the Tribune's sealed orders) to notice it. Or DC 13 History (recognizing that a "local liaison" attached to a visiting Tribune would normally appear in the official manifest — which Quintus does not).

A party that finds all three before midday can confront Quintus directly. He bolts immediately. Athletics DC 13 to catch him. If caught: he talks if given an exit (Persuasion DC 14 or Deception DC 15 about what will happen to him). He names his handler: a man in Carnuntum who sends letters to "G.C.B." He doesn't know what that stands for. The party does (from Handout 5's "G.C.B." signature).

If the party misses all three clues, Quintus leaves. He reappears as an antagonist in Session 4 (Brutus's sleeper agent at the Elder Stair trap is him, returned with new orders).

**HOLE 6 — Tribune Lucius's Scene 3 hesitation: what is he specifically uncertain about?**

The chapter says Scene 3 is the window where Lucius's mask slips on the phrase "enemies of Rome." But what is the scene? What triggers this moment? The chapter needs a specific scene built around this beat.

**Fix:** Scene 3 is the party being briefed by Lucius on his plan to "destroy the spear in the field" — a controlled detonation in the tunnels. He presents this as the safest solution. At the moment he says "anyone who opposes this is opposing Rome's best interests" or equivalent phrasing, he hears himself. Insight DC 15 (active: watching him in that specific moment) catches the half-second pause before "enemies of Rome" lands. He knows the truth: his mission is to destroy evidence of Brutus's interest in the spear, not to serve Rome. He closes down fast. But for one breath, the party saw it.

## Session 3: Through the Dark Forest

### Simulation run

**HOLE 7 — Session 3 → Session 4 geographic transition (critical)**

Session 3 ends with the party at the sacred grove, having performed the ritual. Session 4 starts with the party inside Fort Vindolanda on Day 6 of a siege. The party cannot be in both places simultaneously. No transition mechanism is described.

**Fix:** Add a formal transition block at the END of chapter3.qmd and a matching arrival note at the START of chapter4.qmd.

End of Session 3 (add to DM Notes):

> *After the ritual, Thusnelda speaks plainly: "The fort is under attack. It started two days after you left. I have been watching through the grove's sight. Your people are holding, but not for long." She does not offer comfort. She offers a route: the tunnel that connects the fort's underground water supply to the south ditch — Vercingetorix's warriors know it. They can have you back inside before dawn.*

The transition takes place between sessions. Session 4 opens with the party emerging from the tunnel into the fort at dawn of Day 6. This means: the siege began approximately 2 days after the party departed (consistent with Brutus's sleeper agents having an activation window), and the party's 3-day forest journey (2 travel days + 1 at the grove) plus 1 night of return travel = 4 days absent. Day 6 = party departed Day 1 + 5 days = arriving morning of Day 6. This is mathematically clean.

What the party missed during Days 2-5 of the siege: Varro has been running the defense. Libo died on Day 4 (Session 4 will reference this, not show it — Varro's face when he sees the party tells them something happened that he hasn't said yet). The granary fire started on Day 3 but was partially controlled. The party's absence is not treated as abandonment by Varro; it is treated as the mission continuing as planned. He expected them back by now. He's glad they're back.

**HOLE 8 — The grove sacrifice has no concrete success criteria**

Thusnelda says "most people choose something that hurts a little — that is not enough." But the DM needs to know what actually qualifies. The current text provides feeling but not structure.

**Fix:** Four categories of sacrifice, listed from insufficient to sufficient. The DM privately evaluates which category the party's offer falls into before narrating the result.

**Insufficient (grove rejects it silently — the spear doesn't change, the runes don't dim):**
- Offered by a character who is not the one most bonded to or most affected by the spear
- An object of material value with no personal meaning
- A vow that doesn't cost anything in the current moment ("I swear to be a better soldier")
- A truth the character was already planning to tell

**Minimum (grove accepts grudgingly — ritual succeeds, but the spear remains whole):**
- A personal secret revealed to the entire party, including something the character wished they could keep hidden
- An object of genuine personal meaning (a letter, a specific piece of equipment, a keepsake from before the campaign)
- A vow with immediate mechanical weight (lose a class feature for one session, take one permanent scar)
- Cutting the thread of influence from the influenced NPC through physical contact and an honest statement about what was risked

**Genuine (grove accepts — the spear fragments into ash at the altar; three runes remain glowing, bring them to Session 4):**
- A sacrifice by the character most connected to the spear, offered on behalf of someone else in the party rather than themselves
- A formal renunciation of something the character wanted from the campaign (the glory they were here for, the revenge they were planning, the promotion they could have claimed)
- Telling Thusnelda something true about the campaign that the character is ashamed of
- A character who has been under the spear's influence offering that truth to the party — naming what they nearly did — in front of everyone

**Complete (grove is satisfied — spear dissolves; Thusnelda gives the party one sentence Mars will hear):**
- All of the above in the same ritual
- Plus: the influenced character walks to the altar voluntarily and sets the spear down without being asked, choosing to lose the power they have been slowly accumulating

The DM doesn't need to announce the category. The narration changes:
- Insufficient: the runes on the altar glow but don't change
- Minimum: the spear cracks but doesn't break; the party brings a fragment to Session 4
- Genuine: spear becomes ash; party brings three glowing rune-chips
- Complete: as above, plus the grove feels lighter; Thusnelda looks at the party for a long time before she says anything

**HOLE 9 — The influenced NPC mechanic needs a private note**

The chapter says "pass a folded note to that player at session start." The note's contents aren't specified.

**Fix:** The note reads:

> *You've been under the spear's pull for a week. You don't know it yet. In Scene 1 (the camp at dawn), you wake standing beside the spear with no memory of getting up. You're genuinely confused and frightened. You didn't do it consciously.*
>
> *During the journey, you'll feel the urge to advocate for keeping the spear — not destroying it. You don't have to act on this, but the urge is real. The more your character has personally touched or been near the spear, the stronger it gets.*
>
> *At the grove ritual's climax (Scene 4), if the party hasn't helped you cut the thread, you'll feel an overwhelming urge to stop the ritual. Play this honestly. If other characters help you (holding you, talking to you), make a DC 15 Wisdom save. On a success, you break through. On a failure, you try to take the spear and run. You don't want to do this. You hate that you're doing it. Play both things at once.*
>
> *The DM will tell you when you're free.*

## Session 4: The God's Demand

### Simulation run

Session 4 is the most mechanically complete session. The major gaps are:

**HOLE 10 — Brutus's Plan B is mentioned four times but never described**

The GM notes reference "Plan B" as something Brutus has arranged regardless of what the party does. This appears in:
- gm_intro.qmd (NPC section): "He has arranged a Plan B regardless of what the party does in Sessions 1-4"
- chapter4.qmd DM Notes: "Has already arranged a Plan B ritual in Chapter 5 if this fails"

But the plan itself is never stated anywhere.

**Fix:** Brutus's Plan B is a cult ritual in Rome, timed to coincide with Mars's divine presence at Vindolanda. Quintus Flavius carried a fragment of the spear (a splinter chipped during the storage chain tampering in Session 2) to a cult circle operating in the Caelian Hill district. During Session 5's divine arena, while Mars is occupied with the party, Brutus's cultists attempt to channel Mars's divided attention through the fragment into a prepared vessel. If successful, Brutus gains a divine mandate for his coup — not Mars's actual blessing, but the appearance of one, which in Rome is nearly equivalent.

What this means for Session 5:
- The party cannot directly stop the Rome ritual; they are frozen in the divine arena
- If they succeed in returning the spear to Mars cleanly (Option C or a clean Option A victory), Mars becomes fully present in the arena and simultaneously becomes aware of the theft. He deals with it. The epilogue describes the cult circle in Rome dissolving overnight; three senators die of unexplained causes; Brutus loses his divine backing
- If they delay or fail, the Rome ritual succeeds during the arena. A party member may feel this as a sudden weakening in Mars's attention — Perception DC 15 (active: noticing the god seems distracted in a specific moment during the trial) or Religion DC 16 (passive: recognizing the sensation as divine attention being diverted)
- If the party learned about Plan B through the interrogation of a sleeper agent (DC 15 Intimidation + 5 above = the second name mentioned), they can add a specific argument in Option C: "Your attention is being stolen in Rome right now, while you're talking to us. We are not the threat."

**HOLE 11 — What happens if Corvinus arrests Cassia (session-end conditional)**

The chapter says "If Corvinus felt sidelined he arrests Cassia at the end of the session, forcing a rescue during Session 5's opening." Session 5 has no rescue scene written.

**Fix:** If this condition triggers, add 15 minutes to Session 5's opening before the divine arena activates. Cassia is in the principia locked room (Thieves' Tools DC 14 or Athletics DC 16 to open, or Persuasion DC 15 with Varro's tacit help). She is not harmed; Corvinus isn't a fool. She needs to reach the parade ground before Mars's presence freezes everyone. If she doesn't make it, she is frozen at the principia door, visible but unable to participate. This changes the witnesses at the arena's edge and removes her from the Option C argument support.

If the party frees her and she reaches the arena in time: she speaks one sentence in Option C that auto-succeeds one argument regardless of the party's roll. She knows what Mars needs to hear because she's his daughter. The DM writes this sentence before Session 5 based on what happened in the campaign.

## Session 5: The Wrath of Mars

### Simulation run

**HOLE 12 — Option C arguments not specified**

Option C (Trial of Voice) is the most dramatically rich path — the party argues that destroying or entombing the spear honored Mars rather than defied him. The chapter lists three arguments but doesn't specify what they are.

**Fix:** The three arguments of Option C, in order. Each requires a Persuasion check unless a cascade unlock provides advantage.

**Argument 1: Intent ("we came here because of what the spear is, not what it offers")**

This argument establishes that the party's motive was not theft or ambition but acknowledgment of Mars's rightful claim. The argument must be made by the character who first touched the spear in Session 1 (Mars hears this person louder).

*Persuasion DC 15.* Mars responds with: "Intent is declared by everyone. Show me yours."

*Advantage if:* The character who first touched the spear currently has corruption, demonstrating that they paid a price for their proximity. Mars does not respect clean hands. He respects hands that bore the cost.

*5-above reward:* Mars reduces the DC on Argument 2 by 3.

**Argument 2: Cost ("here is what was sacrificed in your name")**

This argument presents the actual cost of the campaign: who died, what was lost, what the party gave up at the grove. This argument can be made by any party member but is strongest from the one who made the genuine sacrifice at the grove.

*Persuasion DC 16.* Mars responds with: "Rome has lost soldiers before. It does not impress me."

*Advantage if:* The party knows about the original covenant (from the wax tablet + contract scroll in Session 1's Binding Chamber) and can frame the sacrifice in terms of restoring what the covenant intended.

*5-above reward:* Mars turns and looks at the witnesses for a long moment. Cassia, at the arena's edge, puts her hand on her chest. The party has reached him.

**Argument 3: Recognition ("we know what your name has been used for and we refused it")**

This argument names the corruption specifically: Corvinus, the cult of Mars, Brutus's plan, the way the spear was being used to legitimize greed. It requires the party to demonstrate they understand what Mars's name has been made to carry. This is the hardest argument and the most personal for him.

*Persuasion DC 17.* Mars responds first by saying nothing for 30 seconds of real time. Then: "Say the name of my corruption."

*Advantage if:* The party learned from the Wight in Session 1 what the spear was used for (killing Mars's son). Naming Atavitus — saying his name in the arena — causes Mars to go still. He was waiting to see if any mortal had been paying enough attention to understand the wound.

*5-above reward:* Mars concedes something unprompted. He tells the party what the spear's destruction actually meant to him. The table goes quiet. Give it a moment.

**After all three arguments:** Mars either accepts (the spear is formally returned — he absorbs it or allows its destruction — and his judgment is complete) or makes a final demand (one more cost, named specifically, which the party can meet or refuse). If they meet it: clean ending of their choice. If they refuse: Option B begins but with Mars no longer at full anger — his HP is 200, not 300.

**HOLE 13 — Option B has no "until satisfied" stop condition**

The chapter shows Mars at 300 HP, AC 20, but doesn't specify when/how this combat ends.

**Fix:** Mars ends Option B when:
- The party reduces him to 150 HP (he is not surprised and names this: "You have my attention. That is different from victory.")
- OR one PC reaches 0 HP and is not revived within 2 rounds (Mars will not kill all of them; he's conducting a trial, not an execution)
- OR 10 full rounds have passed (Mars calls "Enough")

At each threshold, Mars states what he observed. The party earns his assessment of their conduct, which is worth more than his defeat.

**HOLE 14 — Brutus's Session 5 presence**

Brutus appears in Session 5 for the first time physically. But the divine arena freezes everyone except the party and designated witnesses. How does Brutus appear?

**Fix:** Brutus is not in the arena. He never arrives at Vindolanda. What appears is a projection — Mars allows the party to "hear" Brutus through the arena's suspended time, because his remote ritual is happening simultaneously and Mars is choosing to make the party aware of it. This appears as:

Midway through whichever trial the party chose, the air above one section of the arena ripples. Senator Brutus's voice arrives without a body: calm, genuine, making his argument with complete sincerity. He addresses Mars directly — he has prepared for this. He is good at this. He makes his case for why the spear's power should pass to mortal hands for Rome's benefit.

Mars listens. Then he looks at the party. "You have heard him. Answer him."

This is the embedded fourth test inside whichever Option the party chose. The party must respond to Brutus's argument. If they have assembled evidence across four sessions, they can deconstruct it. If they haven't paid attention, Brutus's argument sounds more compelling than theirs.

After the party responds, Mars reaches out one hand — and the Rome ritual collapses. Brutus's voice cuts off mid-sentence.

## Hidden Information Additions

These are not secrets already in the campaign. They are new threads that make the campaign denser and more rewarding for attentive players.

### The Son of Mars: Atavitus

The vault inscription says "The son of Mars fell here. His father's wrath lingers still." The campaign never explains who this son was. This is a planted thread without a resolution.

**What happened:** Atavitus was a half-mortal champion born of Mars and a Germanic spearwoman three hundred years ago. He was gifted with the spear as a test: could a mortal carry divine power without being consumed? He failed. The spear's whisper of glory turned him from champion to tyrant. The Germanic tribes who had once honored him destroyed him here with his own weapon, and sealed the vault around the killing as an act of respect to Mars: "we ended your son so that your name would not be destroyed by his deeds."

**Cassia's connection:** Cassia is Mars's daughter. Atavitus was her older half-brother, three centuries dead. The spear feels like family to her — not warmly, but with the weight of inheritance. She has never named this connection to anyone. When she says "I have known what I am since I was twelve," part of what she knows is that the vault is her brother's tomb.

**Where this lands in play:**
- Session 1: Players who hit DC 17 on the Wight persuasion (Wight identifies himself as Tiberius Claudius Maximus and explains the covenant) learn the name Atavitus in passing
- Session 2: Cassia's medical log (found if the party searches medicus quarters carefully, DC 15 Investigation) has one entry that reads: *"Examined the spear again. It knows me. It knew Atavitus first. That is why I cannot touch it without — noted for destruction, do not file."* She scratched it out but the text is legible under the scratches
- Session 4: When the party kneels in the antechamber and Cassia says "if no one kneels I will do it alone," she is choosing what Atavitus chose wrong: to face the divine directly rather than run
- Session 5 Option C: Naming Atavitus causes Mars to go still (see Argument 3 above)

**DM note:** Do not announce this connection. Do not give it away if the party doesn't find it. It is a layer for players who are paying attention. If they find it, Session 5's Mars scene deepens enormously. If they don't, the session still works.

### The Nine Shields: The Lost Tribe

The shield hall has nine shields from an unnamed tribe at the lowest position. The Haruspex and Explorator roles can find this but the tribe is never identified.

**What happened:** The nine shields belong to the Tencteri, a tribe whose covenant with Mars was older than Rome. Their prophet, a woman called Grimnisalf, was Atavitus's companion before he was corrupted. When the tribes sealed the vault after killing Atavitus, the Tencteri refused the offering ritual — they did not believe Atavitus had truly fallen, and they carried their shields in but would not complete the offering. They left. Within a generation they were absorbed into the Chauci and vanished from history. Their nine shields are the record of a people who came to honor a god and couldn't finish it.

**Where this lands in play:**
- Session 1 Shield Hall: Explorator DC 15 Insight (active: reading the offering arrangement) — the nine shields are hung wrong. Every other tribe's shields face the room. These nine face the wall. Someone hung them last, after the main arrangement was complete, and turned them around. This is not contempt. It is grief.
- Session 3: Thusnelda knows the Tencteri. If a player asks directly about a tribe with nine shields, she says: "Grimnisalf's people. They never forgave themselves for being right." She does not elaborate without a DC 14 Persuasion or DC 13 History from a character who has seen the shields.
- Session 5: If the party includes this detail in Option C Argument 3 (naming the cost of failing to complete an oath), Mars acknowledges it. Not with words. With the shields becoming briefly visible in the arena's sand, face-up.

### Brutus's Divine Pacts: Observable Consequences

Brutus made pacts with Jupiter, Pluto, and Bellona. These are stated but have no observable effects in the campaign.

**Jupiter:** Ravens carry messages for both the Roman frumentarius network and the Germanic tribes. This is not coincidence. Jupiter's ravens observe and carry information across allegiances. A character with Religion DC 15 who studies a raven brought into the fort in Session 3 finds a tiny wax seal on its leg-band — not a tribal seal, not a Roman military seal. An astral symbol, pre-Roman. Jupiter's eye. Someone is using Jupiter's network for intelligence.

**Pluto:** The undead in the vault are not simply old curses going stale. Pluto has loosened the underworld's hold on this site as part of Brutus's pact. The ghouls and ghast exist because Pluto let them remain. A Haruspex who examines the Bone Chamber's ritual offering in Session 1 (DC 14 Religion, 5-above) notes that the offering pattern is wrong for Mars — it uses grain-blood-coin in a sequence that invokes Pluto, not Mars. Someone has been maintaining a secondary offering cycle alongside the Mars maintenance cycle, invoking a different god in the same space.

**Bellona:** The cultists of Mars who appear in Session 1 (Marius Coda and associates) and throughout the campaign are not truly Mars-worshippers. They are Bellona's devotees operating under Mars's name. Bellona, goddess of war's destructive aspect, is not Mars — she is his dark counterpart. A character with Religion DC 16 who observes the cult's ritual behavior in Session 4 (the captured sleeper agent's possessions, if examined) finds a hidden Bellona icon wrapped inside a Mars pendant. Bellona has backed Brutus because his plan creates maximum war and instability, which is her domain. Mars is being used. He knows this. It is specifically what he is angry about.

### Titus's Papers: Complete Vault History in Session 2

Cassia's predecessor Titus Sempronius Caecilius maintained the vault for decades before his death in AD 173. Cassia has his papers. She hasn't organized them.

**What's in the papers (findable in the medicus/augur's quarters, DC 14 Investigation in Session 2 or later):**

- A map of the vault that matches the party's exploration but includes one room they haven't found: a collapsed antechamber north of the Shield Hall, sealed by the cave-in
- The complete terms of the covenant in Latin, cleaner than the Binding Chamber scroll
- Twenty years of offering records with detailed notes on the offering cycle and what each type of offering accomplishes
- One page written in a different hand than Titus's: a letter from his own predecessor, dated 140 AD, that reads: *"The tribes will renew in AD 174. Whichever augur holds this post must be present at the south ditch at spring equinox, alone, to receive the tribes' representative. Do not send soldiers. Do not report this to the Legate. This is not betrayal. This is how the binding stays alive."*

Titus died in AD 173. He never passed this information to Cassia. The renewal appointment in AD 174 was missed. This is the root cause of everything.

**Where this lands in play:** If the party finds this in Session 2, they understand the full picture before they even reach the forest. The grove ritual in Session 3 becomes purposeful (they know why) rather than exploratory (they're figuring it out). The Session 1/2 mystery resolves into a genuine tragedy rather than a magical problem: a simple missed appointment, a death at the wrong time, no malice in the beginning.

## Balance Review

### Session 1 Vault (Level 3 party)

**Flooded Gallery — 4 Shadows:** Adjusted CR 2 encounter in difficult terrain with dim light disadvantage. Genuinely Deadly if fought. The bypass (extinguish lights, DC 14 Stealth group check) is the intended path. Fix described above (shadow turning toward light before combat) makes this clear. *No adjustment needed after fix.*

**Bone Chamber — 2 Ghouls + 1 Ghast:** Deadly encounter. Stench aura affects all characters in the chamber before they can react. The chapter already notes the 4-player adjustment. For 5 players, run as written. For 4: remove the Ghast. *Verify player count at Session 0 and note on session card.*

**Altar Chamber — 1 Wight + 2 Shadows:** The Wight (CR 3) alone exceeds the Hard threshold for a Level 3 party. The Wight and Shadows are not allied. The exit condition (Wight persuasion) must be available from Round 0, not discovered after casualties. Fix described above (Wight speaks before initiative). *Exit condition fix makes this encounter workable.*

**Total vault resource drain:** The vault contains three significant fights plus one avoidable fight plus one puzzle corridor with damage-on-failure. For a Level 3 party with 2 short rest worth of resources (entering the vault after the principia scene), this is very tight. The design intent is clear: the vault should cost something. But verify that the party has a short rest opportunity somewhere in the vault (the Shield Hall between the hidden alcove discovery and the Bone Chamber is a reasonable beat — 10 minutes, torches still, nothing hostile). Add a note to the DM that a short rest in the Shield Hall is legitimate; Sicus is there and the room is clear.

## Cross-Session Integration: Missing Threads

### Commendationes tracking: Sessions 2 and 3

Session 4 has explicit "Commendationes This Session" notes. Sessions 2 and 3 do not.

**Session 2 commendationes opportunities:**
1. Solving the Sextus murder and naming Quintus Flavius publicly (before the Tribune departs) — one character who drives the investigation to conclusion earns one *commendatio* from Varro, not from Corvinus
2. Successfully negotiating with Vercingetorix (reaching DC 12 Persuasion on the road, without the Vercingetorix background knowledge) — one *commendatio* from Cassia's personal records, unofficial but noted

**Session 3 commendationes opportunities:**
1. Protecting the influenced NPC through the possession episode rather than exposing or restraining them — one *commendatio* from the influenced NPC's player when they recover, in-character
2. Completing the grove ritual with a Genuine or Complete sacrifice — Thusnelda sends a formal message to Vindolanda's shrine that Paterculus files as an official testimonial

### What happens when a key cascade unlock is missed for three sessions

The cascade unlock table tracks what each DC-hitting player earns forward. It doesn't specify what happens if a critical unlock (e.g., Session 1 DC 14 Insight on Corvinus before the Tribune arrives) is never hit.

**Fix:** For each critical unlock, add a "fallback access" that costs more:

| Missed unlock | Fallback |
|---|---|
| S1 DC 14 Insight on Corvinus | DC 18 Insight in Session 3 when Corvinus is desperate; he is less controlled |
| S2 DC 14 Insight on Tribune (Scene 3 only) | DC 20 Persuasion in Session 4 with evidence of his mission; he has no other leverage |
| S3 DC 14 Insight on Vercingetorix (after grove) | He tells the party about Brutus's chieftain contacts during Session 4's council if Cassia directly asks him |
| S1 DC 13 History (tribal arrangement = single authority) | Thusnelda names this in Session 3 if asked "who controlled all the tribes that were here?" |

### Varro's question: when does the DM offer it?

The design says: "if nobody asks, the information sits there through the whole campaign." Varro knows Corvinus is compromised. The question is "what do you know about Corvinus that you haven't reported?" But the DM needs a specific scene where this conversation is possible.

**Fix:** In Session 2, after the Tribune asserts authority and Corvinus backs down publicly, Varro looks at the party at the end of the council scene and says one thing: "Twenty years." That's it. He doesn't elaborate. A player who follows this up — "what do you mean?" or "tell me about Corvinus" — has opened the door. If no one follows up, the moment passes. If someone does, Varro walks away from the group to a corner of the courtyard and the conversation happens there. This is not an Investigation check. It is the player caring enough to ask.

### Decius Turbo's reveal: what triggers it

The frumentarius reveal in Session 3 is described but the trigger isn't specified.

**Fix:** Turbo is revealed when the party's actions in Sessions 1-2 cross a threshold that his orders require him to report. Specifically: if the party (a) found the covenant documents in the Binding Chamber AND (b) shared this information with Cassia or anyone outside their immediate unit, Turbo's handler in Carnuntum sends him new orders. A character observing Turbo's raven correspondence in Session 3 (Perception DC 13, active: watching the camp perimeter during early morning when ravens move) sees him send and receive. That's Clue 1.

Clue 2: Turbo asks unusually specific questions about what the party found in the vault. Not what happened in the fight — what documents or artifacts were recovered. A player who succeeds on Insight DC 13 (active: watching his face while he asks) notices he already knows the answer is yes.

Clue 3: If the party has the contract scroll, and Turbo is nearby, he glances at it once. DC 12 Perception (passive) to catch the glance.

Once the party confronts him: he confirms he is a frumentarius without visible shame. He's been honest about everything except his mission. He offers to cease reporting if the party agrees to let him attach to their unit officially (he loses his cover but gains protection). This is a genuine offer. He is not a villain; he is doing his job. What the party does with him is a meaningful choice with Session 5 consequences: if Turbo is reporting to Rome, Session 5's epilogue includes an Imperial dispatch arriving within the month. If he isn't, that dispatch never comes.

```
A=D  B=E  C=F  D=G  E=H  F=I  G=J  H=K  I=L  J=M  K=N
L=O  M=P  N=Q  O=R  P=S  Q=T  R=U  S=V  T=W  U=X  V=Y
W=Z  X=A  Y=B  Z=C

EXAMPLE:
TKHB → decodes as QHEY → wrong direction
To decode: find the CODED letter in the bottom row, read the plain letter above it.
```

Simpler physical version: a strip of paper with ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ on one line and DEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZABC below it. Align any coded letter on the bottom to find its plain text above.

Give players the encoded letter (Handout 5) and the cipher key strip separately. Watching them work out the cipher is 5 minutes of engaged table time.

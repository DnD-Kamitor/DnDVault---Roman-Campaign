SETTING: The fort sits on a plateau above a shallow valley, surrounded by moorland, bog, and dark conifer forest to the north. Sky almost always overcast: low grey cloud, cold north-northwest wind, smell of peat smoke, wet stone, and iron. The cleared ground between fort and treeline is churned mud crossed by a single military road. Visibility to the treeline: roughly 300 feet.

WALLS AND DEFENSES: Rectangular, roughly 170 by 120 metres, long axis east-west. Stone walls twelve feet high, capped with timber walkways and crenellations. A fighting ditch rings the exterior. Four stone gatehouses - one per compass point - each with a projecting tower, iron-bound timber doors, and a murder hole above the approach. Brazier fires burn on all towers at all hours. The north wall faces the forest and is the most heavily watched.

- Valetudinarium (hospital, east): long and low, smells of vinegar and linen.

THE VICUS (outside east gate): civilian settlement of tightly packed timber buildings - taverna, thermopolium, traders, workshops, a Mercury shrine near the market square. Locals supply the garrison with timber, boots, beer, and clothing. More life and warmth here than inside the walls, and more information leaking out.

Sounds: hammer on iron all morning; rain on timber at all hours; two short trumpet blasts at dawn (watch change); a flat-cracked north bell distinct from the south; the low roar of the bath furnace.

The soldiers are tired, watchful, and running short on everything except the sense that something beneath the flagstones is not finished with them.

THE THING BENEATH: Under the principia flagstones, cold air seeps upward through fitted stone that is older than the fort. Every officer knows it is there. No one discusses it. The sealed staircase leads down to passages carved long before Rome arrived - stone walls that absorb sound, chambers where the torches burn without flicker, an altar that pre-dates any known religion in the region. Something was bound here. The binding is failing. The spear found beneath the altar is not a weapon. It is a key, or a seal, and no one agrees which.

TONE: Dark, cold, practical. Roman order imposed on a landscape that does not accept it. The fort is not heroic - it is a machine for keeping men alive and pointing them north. Function over beauty.
